## Episode title

**20 Aug 2026 | NSE Daily Brief: The rally came from Washington, the brake from Hormuz**

## Description

The Nifty ended a seven-session losing run on Thursday, closing at 24,231.85, up 0.64%. The cause was not Indian. The US Treasury said on Wednesday it would at least double its buybacks of long-dated government bonds, and long-term American yields fell the same session. Asia rallied on that, and India rallied least of all, because Brent crude rose for a fifth straight session to $93.87 a barrel. This episode also covers the sugar order that lifted an entire industry while being designed to cool prices, and the two sets of central-bank minutes published seven hours apart that are both arguing about rate *rises*.

## Key points

- **Nifty 50 closed 24,231.85, +0.64%**, the first rise after seven straight falls. Roughly three and a half shares rose for every one that fell, on a count taken during the session.
- **The cause was a US Treasury buyback announcement**, $2bn to at least $4bn an operation. The US 10-year yield fell 5.7 basis points to 4.647% and the 30-year fell 9 basis points to 5.196%. A buyback is a debt-management operation, not stimulus, and the enlarged operations do not begin until 9 September.
- **India rallied least in Asia** — Korea's Kospi gained roughly 5.5% to 6%, Japan and Hong Kong a little over 1% each — because Brent rose a fifth straight session to $93.87. India buys almost all of its oil abroad, so a dearer barrel feeds into Indian inflation. The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively shut since February.
- **Balrampur Chini Mills +17.87%**, with sugar shares up to +18% across the industry, after the government capped bulk *buyers* of sugar at 15 days of stock. The order binds buyers, not mills, and is designed to *cool* prices. What lifted the shares is the physical price it confirms: spot sugar at ₹5,530 a quintal, reported as a 16-year high.
- **The written brief carries the full mover table**, including Indo Borax & Chemicals +14.82% on its move to buy 64.26% of Kronox Lab Sciences for ₹246 crore, which triggers a compulsory open offer to Kronox's public shareholders at roughly 52% above the price the buyer paid for control. That beat is in the brief, not in this episode.
- **Himadri Speciality Chemical −6.14%** was the only name in our 707-stock universe to fall more than 5%. We ran no search on it, so no cause is offered. Separately, eight of the ten biggest risers have no cause anyone can point to; fifteen checks were run on the risers and three stories were killed as wrongly dated.
- **Two sets of central-bank minutes, seven hours apart on 19 August.** The RBI's carry Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta's case that no further easing is possible and that a rate *increase* may emerge in the current financial year, FY 2026-27, with inflation peaking at 5.9% in the December quarter. India's policy rate is unchanged at 5.25%, stance neutral, that hold unanimous. The Fed's July minutes record a 9–3 hold with three regional presidents dissenting in favour of a hike.
- **Next date that matters: the RBI's 5–7 October meeting**, which those minutes have made considerably more interesting.
