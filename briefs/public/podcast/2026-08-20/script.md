Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Thursday's session.

The Nifty finally rose on Thursday, ending seven straight sessions of falling. But the reason was not made in India. It was made in Washington, and India got the smallest share of it.

Here is where the market closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand two hundred and thirty one, up zero point six four percent. Nifty Bank rose zero point four five percent and the technology index zero point seven nine percent. On one live count taken during the session, about three and a half shares rose for every one that fell. Nine of the eleven sector indices gave us no usable move on the day, so I cannot tell you which sectors led.

Asia rallied on Thursday and India rallied least. Korea's Kospi gained roughly five and a half to six percent, Japan's Nikkei about one point two, Hong Kong about one point one. India came last in that field, because Brent crude rose for a fifth straight session, to ninety three dollars and eighty seven cents a barrel. The rally came from Washington. The brake came from Hormuz.

So, Washington first. On Wednesday the American Treasury said it will at least double the amount of long dated government bonds it buys back, from two billion dollars an operation to at least four billion. Long term American yields fell the same session. A basis point is one hundredth of one percent. The ten year yield fell five point seven basis points, to four point six four seven percent, and the thirty year fell nine, to five point one nine six percent.

Now the part that matters, and it is the part the headlines skipped. A buyback is a debt management operation. It is not the Federal Reserve, it is not new money, and it is not stimulus. And the enlarged operations do not begin until the ninth of September, running to the fourth of November. So India rose on an announcement whose first effect is three weeks away.

The worrying side is oil, and it is the same story as last week. The Strait of Hormuz, the narrow lane that carries much of the world's oil, has been effectively shut since the end of February, and eight attacks on ships have been reported this month.
