Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Thursday's session.

The Nifty finally rose on Thursday, ending seven straight sessions of falling. But the reason was not made in India. It was made in Washington, and India got the smallest share of it.

Here is where the market closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand two hundred and thirty one, up zero point six four percent. Nifty Bank rose zero point four five percent and the technology index zero point seven nine percent. On one live count taken during the session, about three and a half shares rose for every one that fell. Nine of the eleven sector indices gave us no usable move on the day, so I cannot tell you which sectors led.

Asia rallied on Thursday and India rallied least. Korea's Kospi gained roughly five and a half to six percent, Japan's Nikkei about one point two, Hong Kong about one point one. India came last in that field, because Brent crude rose for a fifth straight session, to ninety three dollars and eighty seven cents a barrel. The rally came from Washington. The brake came from Hormuz.

So, Washington first. On Wednesday the American Treasury said it will at least double the amount of long dated government bonds it buys back, from two billion dollars an operation to at least four billion. Long term American yields fell the same session. A basis point is one hundredth of one percent. The ten year yield fell five point seven basis points, to four point six four seven percent, and the thirty year fell nine, to five point one nine six percent.

Now the part that matters, and it is the part the headlines skipped. A buyback is a debt management operation. It is not the Federal Reserve, it is not new money, and it is not stimulus. And the enlarged operations do not begin until the ninth of September, running to the fourth of November. So India rose on an announcement whose first effect is three weeks away.

The worrying side is oil, and it is the same story as last week. The Strait of Hormuz, the narrow lane that carries much of the world's oil, has been effectively shut since the end of February, and eight attacks on ships have been reported this month.

Now what moved. The biggest gain was Balrampur Chini Mills, a sugar producer, up seventeen point eight seven percent, and sugar shares rose up to eighteen percent right across the industry. On Wednesday the government capped bulk buyers of sugar at fifteen days of stock, from the first of September to the thirtieth of November. Read that the right way round. The order binds buyers, not mills, and it is meant to cool sugar prices, not to support them. What lifted the shares is what the order confirms. Spot sugar touched five thousand five hundred and thirty rupees for a hundred kilos on Wednesday, reported as a sixteen year high.

Indo Borax and Chemicals rose almost fifteen percent, and it rose as the buyer in a takeover. It told the exchange during trading hours that it is buying control of a listed peer, Kronox Lab Sciences, sixty four point two six percent of it, for two hundred and forty six crore rupees. That purchase triggers a compulsory offer to Kronox's public shareholders, priced about fifty two percent above what the sellers themselves negotiated. That is the takeover code doing its job. Buyers do not usually get that reception.

Only one share in our seven hundred name universe fell more than five percent. Himadri Speciality Chemical, down six point one four percent. I have nothing for you on that one. We ran no search on it, so I am not going to guess.

And on the risers, eight of the ten biggest have no cause anyone can point to. We ran fifteen checks across two independent lanes and killed three stories that would have shipped as reasons, every one of them wrongly dated. Nobody knows is the accurate answer there, and it is a better one than something plausible.

Here is what struck me most this week. Two central banks published the minutes of their meetings seven hours apart on Wednesday, and both were arguing about raising rates. The Reserve Bank of India's landed around five in the evening our time. Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta records that no further easing is possible, and that a case for a rate increase may emerge in the next financial year, with inflation peaking at five point nine percent in the October to December quarter. The policy rate itself is unchanged at five point two five percent, the stance neutral, and that hold was unanimous. The American Federal Reserve's minutes followed about half past eleven that night. A nine to three hold, with three regional presidents dissenting in favour of a higher rate. Nobody has set those two documents side by side. And here is the irony of Thursday. The relief came from a debt management operation, not from any change in the rate path. Both curves are now arguing about the same direction, and it is not downwards.

Two dates to keep. The buybacks that lifted Thursday do not actually begin until the ninth of September. And the Reserve Bank's next rate meeting is the fifth to the seventh of October, which those minutes have made a good deal more interesting than it looked a week ago.
