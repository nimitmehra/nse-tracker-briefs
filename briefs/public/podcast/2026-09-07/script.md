Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Monday's session.

It has been ten days since I last spoke to you, and the thing that changed most in that gap is the price of oil. Brent crude is reported to have settled near ninety seven dollars a barrel on Monday, about nine percent above where it sat on the twenty eighth of August, after the heaviest exchange of strikes on oil tankers yet in the war between the United States and Iran. That ran over a weekend when India was shut. India imports almost all the oil it burns and has no say in that price. Our own file could not give me a clean oil number, so treat the ninety seven dollars as press reported, not confirmed.

But oil is not what moved share prices here. That was a different foreign event. America's jobs number landed on Friday evening India time, after we had closed. Payrolls rose about a hundred and sixty two thousand against expectations near fifty three thousand, which pushed up the odds of an American rate rise this month. Indian software shares fell on Monday. The link between the two is the read every news carrier ran, and it is a read, not a confirmed fact.


The Nifty fifty closed at twenty three thousand seven hundred and seventy nine, down half a percent, and the Sensex fell the same. The index of Indian software shares fell two point three percent, a fourth losing session in a row, with every company in it lower. Outside software the day was almost featureless. Small caps finished flat and the typical share fell about as much as the index. Only two sector readings came through cleanly on Monday, software and pharmaceuticals, which rose three quarters of a percent, so those are the only two I will put a number on.


Two things about the world India traded into. America was shut on Monday. It was Labor Day, so New York never opened, which means any comparison you read this week between Monday's Nifty and American markets sets Monday's India against Friday's America. Asia did trade alongside us, and Asia went the other way, hard. Korea's main index rose four point six percent, led by Samsung and S-K hynix, and Japan's Nikkei rose about two percent. Same jobs number, opposite directions, because North Asia sells the equipment that artificial intelligence buildouts run on, while Indian software sells project work to American clients whose budgets are the first thing squeezed when borrowing costs look like rising.


Now the movers. Ten shares rose three percent or more on Monday, there was exactly one genuine faller, and only one name on the list has documents behind it. Not the biggest move, which was Syrma S-G-S Technology, up twelve percent with nothing on the record to explain it.

The one with documents is Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri, a hundred and sixty year old Mumbai jeweller, up ten percent. G-R-T Jewellers has agreed to take just over seventy four percent of it from the founding family, and has made the mandatory offer to remaining shareholders at about two hundred and fifty rupees a share. The share closed Monday at about five hundred and twenty nine. So the market is not pricing that offer. It is pricing the business under a new owner.

The one genuine faller was Ram Ratna Wires, which makes copper winding wire, down about six percent. We checked whether the founding family had been forced to sell shares they had borrowed against. They had not. They own sixty nine percent with none of it pledged. Nothing on the record explains Monday.


Here is what struck me most about Monday, and I do not have an answer for it. Four hundred and fifty three of seven hundred and ten shares fell. And neither of the two big institutional blocs was selling. Foreign investors bought a net two hundred and eighty crore rupees, and Indian institutions bought five hundred and sixty seven crore. A broad decline happened with nobody large handing stock out, and no source I could find explains it. The tempting answer is that smaller traders and dealing desks reduced risk on foreign news while the institutions sat still. That is a guess, and I would rather say I do not know.

Three other things did not move when they should have. IndiGo closed slightly higher on a day oil carried a war premium, which is the wrong direction for an airline's fuel bill. The gold lenders fell while gold itself rose one percent, with Muthoot Finance down about one percent and Manappuram down four. And the rupee took a crude shock and a hawkish repricing of American rates on the same day and finished unchanged at ninety four rupees and forty nine paise. Part of that is a soft dollar, which eased slightly. That is a partial account, not a complete one.


Now, what to watch. The American interest rate decision lands on the fifteenth and sixteenth of September, announced around half past eleven at night India time. The odds of a rise sit somewhere between fifty two and sixty five percent depending on which venue you read, so I will keep giving you the range instead of pretending it is one number. Before that, India's August inflation figure arrives in the middle of the month, and it is the first one that can carry oil moving from eighty nine dollars to ninety seven.
