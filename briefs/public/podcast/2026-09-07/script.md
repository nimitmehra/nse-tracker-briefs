Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Monday's session.

It has been ten days since I last spoke to you, and the biggest thing that changed is the price of oil. Brent crude is reported to have settled near ninety seven dollars a barrel on Monday, about nine percent above the twenty eighth of August. Behind it, the heaviest strikes on oil tankers yet in the American war with Iran, over a weekend when India was shut. India buys nearly all the oil it burns and has no say in that price. Our own file could not give a clean oil number, so treat the ninety seven dollars as reported, not confirmed.

But oil is not what moved share prices here. America's jobs number landed on Friday evening India time, after we had closed. Payrolls rose about a hundred and sixty two thousand against expectations near fifty three thousand, which pushed up the odds of an American rate rise this month. Indian software shares fell on Monday, and the link between the two is the read every carrier ran, not a confirmed fact.


The Nifty fifty closed at twenty three thousand seven hundred and seventy nine, down half a percent, and the Sensex fell the same. The index of Indian software shares fell two point three percent, a fourth losing session in a row, with every company in it lower. Outside software the day was almost featureless, with small caps flat and the typical share falling about as much as the index.


Two things about the world India traded into. America was shut on Monday. It was Labor Day, so New York never opened, which means any comparison this week between Monday's Nifty and American markets sets Monday's India against Friday's America. Asia did trade alongside us, and went the other way, hard. Korea's main index rose four point six percent, led by Samsung and S-K hynix, and Japan's Nikkei rose about two percent. Same jobs number, opposite directions, because North Asia sells the equipment artificial intelligence buildouts run on, while Indian software sells project work to American clients who cut budgets first when borrowing looks dearer.


Now the movers. Ten shares rose three percent or more, only one fell far enough to make the list, and just one name in the whole set has documents behind it. Not the biggest move, which was Syrma S-G-S Technology, up twelve percent with nothing on the record behind it.

The one with documents is Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri, a hundred and sixty year old Mumbai jeweller, up ten percent. G-R-T Jewellers has agreed to take just over seventy four percent of it from the founding family, and must now offer the other shareholders about two hundred and fifty rupees a share. The share closed Monday at about five hundred and twenty nine. So the market is not pricing the offer, it is pricing the business under a new owner.

The one that made the falling side was Ram Ratna Wires, which makes copper winding wire, down about six percent. The founding family has not been forced to sell, none of their sixty nine percent is pledged, and nothing on the record explains it.


Here is what struck me most about Monday, and I have no answer for it. Four hundred and fifty three of seven hundred and ten shares fell. And neither big institutional bloc was selling. Foreign investors bought a net two hundred and eighty crore rupees, and Indian institutions bought five hundred and sixty seven crore. So a broad decline happened with nobody large selling into it, and no source I could find explains why. The tempting answer is smaller traders cutting risk while institutions sat still, but that is a guess, and I would rather say I do not know.

Three things also did not move when they should have. IndiGo rose slightly on a day oil carried a war premium, the wrong direction for an airline's fuel bill. The gold lenders, the companies that lend against your jewellery, fell while gold itself rose one percent. And the rupee took a crude shock and a hawkish rate repricing on the same day and finished unchanged, partly on a soft dollar, which is a partial account and not a complete one.


What to watch. The American rate decision lands on the fifteenth and sixteenth of September, and the odds of a rise sit between about fifty two and sixty five percent depending on the venue, which is why I give you a range and not one number. Before that, India's August inflation figure arrives mid month, the first that can carry oil going from eighty nine dollars to ninety seven.
