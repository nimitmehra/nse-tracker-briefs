## Punch list

Ordered by how much a listener loses. None of these is an audio-breaker; all are one-line swaps.

1. **[L1] Sentence 23 — "India flat beside that is non-participation, not strength."** The single worst line in the ear. "Non-participation" is desk shorthand carrying the sentence's whole meaning. Rewrite: *"India finished flat beside that, and flat is not the same as strong. Indian buyers simply did not show up."*
2. **[L1] Sentence 27 — "fifty three point three, the weakest reading in more than four years."** A naive listener cannot place the number, and "weakest in four years" will be heard as contraction when the reading still shows growth. Rewrite: *"the July services survey landed that same morning at fifty three point three. Anything above fifty means the sector is still growing, so this is still growth, just the slowest in more than four years."*
3. **[L2-lite] Sentences 5 to 7 — three companies in the hook, zero descriptors.** Garware is the live risk: "Hi-Tech Films" will be heard as a movie studio when it makes polyester film. Rewrite: *"RateGain, which sells pricing software to hotels and airlines, grew its revenue... Garware Hi-Tech Films, which makes industrial plastic film, had the best quarter in its history... Samvardhana Motherson, which makes car parts, lifted profit..."* Costs about fifteen words; buys the hook its credibility.
4. **[L1] Sentences 19 and 48 — "corroborated" / "the reason is not established".** Audit vocabulary spoken aloud, twice. The honesty is exactly right; the register is not. Rewrite 19 as *"The move is real."* Leave 48 as-is or soften to *"and I could not find out why."* Do not change the substance of either — the hedging is the best thing in the script.
5. **[L1-lite] Sentence 12 — "the worst sector we could measure".** Either cut the qualifier (*"Nifty I-T was the worst sector, down about one percent"*) or drop the sector line entirely, since the composition note already argues breadth is carrying the tape.
6. **[L2-lite] Sentence 16 — "the index closed green on two stocks"**, and only one is ever named. Either name the second in the movers section or say *"on essentially one stock"*, which is what the rest of the beat actually argues.
7. **[L1-lite] Sentence 14 — "Three hundred and two shares rose, three hundred and seventy one fell"** with no denominator. Add three words: *"across the five hundred largest listed companies"* (or whatever the brief's universe is).
