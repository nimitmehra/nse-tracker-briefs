## Headline

**Both FAILs are cleared in the ear, not just on the page.** I re-read cold, script plus exemplar only, and opened the brief only after the ledger below was finished.

- **FAIL 1 (Garware misheard as a movie studio) — CLEARED.** All three hook companies now carry a spoken descriptor, and each descriptor is an appositive that arrives *before* the number, so the listener has the business in mind while hearing the figure. "Garware Hi-Tech Films, which makes industrial plastic film" kills the movie-studio reading outright — "plastic film" and "Films" cannot both be heard as cinema. RateGain's "sells pricing software to hotels and airlines" is the one that does the most work: it turns one hundred and eighty eight percent from an uncalibratable number into a small software company scaling. "Samvardhana Motherson, the car parts maker" is the leanest of the three and the right choice — a bare appositive, no relative clause, so the hook's rhythm survives three insertions.
- **FAIL 2 (growth heard as contraction) — CLEARED, and better than the rewrite I suggested.** The writer split it into two sentences and put the scale between the number and the ranking: "…landed that same morning at fifty three point three. Anything above fifty means the sector is still growing, so this is growth at its slowest in more than four years." The listener hears number, then the line that makes the number mean something, then the ranking. My suggested version said "still growth, just the slowest"; the shipped version says "growth at its slowest", which is shorter and carries the same guard. No listener finishes that pair believing the sector is shrinking.

The four non-FAIL flags are also cleared: "non-participation" is gone and replaced with a mechanism ("Indian buyers simply did not show up"); "corroborated" is gone ("The move is real"); "the reason is not established" is gone ("and I could not find out why"); "the worst sector we could measure" is gone with its whole line; the breadth counts now carry a denominator; the second index stock is named.

The hook now costs three more descriptors and the script still lands at 699 words. That is a tight fit, and the next section is about what it cost.
