Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on today's session.

Five Indian companies filed strong results today and the market bought none of them. RateGain grew its revenue one hundred and eighty eight percent, and the shares fell. S-J-S Enterprises more than doubled its profit, and the shares fell. Garware Hi-Tech Films had the best quarter in its history, and the shares fell. Today the market paid for margins and ignored growth.
Here is where things closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand six hundred and thirty six, up zero point zero five percent. The Sensex and the Bank Nifty each rose about half a percent. Nifty I-T was the worst sector we could actually measure, down about one percent. But that green headline is misleading. Three hundred and two shares rose, three hundred and seventy one fell, and the middle stock in the market finished lower. The Nifty fifty was the weakest group of the lot, with thirty of its members down.
The index closed green on one stock, and I have to be careful here, because we could not establish any reason dated to today for the move. Reliance Industries rose three and a half percent. At roughly eight percent of the index, that one share is worth most of the day's gain. The price is corroborated. The reason is not, and I am not going to invent one for you.
Next door, Asia broke. Korea's main index fell four and a half percent after a mid-morning trading halt, on doubts about whether the money pouring into artificial-intelligence hardware will ever earn a return. India sitting flat beside that is non-participation, not strength.
Two things from this week sit behind all of it. On the supportive side, the Reserve Bank of India held interest rates on Wednesday at five point two five percent. It cut its inflation forecast to five percent and raised its growth forecast to six point seven percent. On the worrying side, the July services survey landed that same morning at fifty three point three, the weakest reading in more than four years. That is the part of the economy producing about fifty five percent of India's output.
Now the movers, and they tell the same story. The biggest fall was Firstsource Solutions, down thirteen percent. It runs customer service and back-office work for foreign clients. Revenue grew almost twenty three percent, but profit went backwards on a one-off charge and cash from operations fell seventy two percent. Growth that does not turn into cash is what got sold.
The second fall was Blue Star, the air-conditioning maker, down five and a half percent. Revenue rose thirteen percent but profit fell fifteen percent. The summer was short, dealers are sitting on unsold stock, and the company could not pass its costs on. The margin in its cooling business halved, and the chairman flagged a cautious year ahead.
And then the one company the market did buy. Navin Fluorine makes speciality fluorine chemicals. Revenue rose forty four percent, profit more than doubled, and its operating margin was over thirty four percent. It closed at a lifetime high. Our own reading of the size of that gain sits above what other sources reported, so please take the direction and not the exact figure.
Here is what struck me most this week. When the Reserve Bank cuts its inflation forecast, that ordinarily helps anything whose customers borrow to buy. Lenders got the message. Shriram Finance is up eleven percent on the week, Cholamandalam over nine, Bajaj Finance over eight. Property did the opposite. Lodha fell today and is down almost six percent on the week, with D-L-F, Oberoi Realty, Prestige and Godrej Properties all lower. Same signal, opposite outcomes, and the reason is not established. Foreign money is not the explanation either. Strip out one single session on the fourth of August and foreign investors are net sellers for the whole month.
Now, what to watch. Tomorrow is a heavy results day, more than two hundred companies filing, among them Life Insurance Corporation and Hindustan Petroleum. Watch whether the market keeps paying for margins and ignoring growth.
