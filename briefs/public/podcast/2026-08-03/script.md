Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toroIQ. Your read on today's session.

The market went up today, and the two numbers everybody quotes disagreed about by how much. The Sensex rose zero point seven zero percent. The Nifty rose one point six zero percent. And neither of them is wrong.

Here is why. The way India's closing prices are worked out changed this morning. Until Friday, a stock's closing price was an average of the last half hour of trading. From today, for every stock that has futures contracts on it, the close is set by a single auction between quarter past three and half past three. All the orders go into one pot, and one price comes out. On day one, some participants bid above the going price in a few heavyweight Nifty names, and that alone can lift an index close. So this ninety basis point gap is day-one plumbing, not a signal. It is the second widest gap between the two indices since twenty ten.

So how much did the market actually rise? The honest answer is in the breadth. Of the six hundred and eighty stocks we track, five hundred and thirty-four rose and one hundred and forty-four fell, and the typical stock gained one point three five percent. The Nifty closed at twenty-four thousand seven hundred and seventy-four. Technology shares were the strongest part of the board, up three point three percent, with no cause that anyone could date to today. Midcaps and smallcaps each rose about one point two percent.

One odd thing sat inside all of that. India VIX [SAY: India VIKS], the market's fear gauge, rose one point four percent, to eleven point nine three, on a strongly rising market and two days before a rate decision. Fear was bid up on an up day.

What lifted the market was oil. Most of the overseas picture came back stale in our data today, but crude is the one input clearly upstream of an Indian session. Brent crude fell seven point three percent over the weekend, from ninety dollars and twelve cents on Friday to eighty-three dollars and fifty-four cents a barrel. Two things did it. OPEC plus [SAY: OH-pek plus] met on Sunday and agreed a September output increase, and Donald Trump called off a planned strike on Iran. India buys about eighty-five percent of the oil it burns, so a fall that size is money that stays in the country.

But be careful with the second half of that story. The strike being called off is confirmed. A deal is not. Iran's foreign ministry said today that there are no negotiations with the United States, and the Strait of Hormuz [SAY: hor-MOOZ] is still physically restricted, with ships needing Iranian permission to pass. Today's oil collapse was priced off the claimed half of the story rather than the confirmed half.

Now the movers. The biggest fall on the board was Zee Entertainment, down more than fourteen percent. The market regulator, SEBI [SAY: SEE-bee], issued a final order on Friday barring the company from the securities market for two months, and its two promoters for a year each, over an unauthorised pledge of company land to secure borrowings by group companies back in twenty sixteen. On that same Friday, shareholders had approved a share issue to those same promoters at one hundred and twenty-six rupees a share. The stock closed today at ninety-eight.

Second, Muthoot Finance [SAY: mu-THOOT], the gold lender, fell over seven percent on a profit that rose forty-three percent. Its lending margin fell one hundred and seventy-four basis points over the year, and profit was actually lower than the previous quarter. The forty-three percent is flattered by a weak year-ago base.

The biggest gain was Urban Company, the home services app, up almost thirteen percent, on revenue up forty-four percent and orders up seventy-nine percent. It is still loss-making, and a year ago it made a small profit.

Here is what struck me about today. Five hundred and thirty-four stocks rose, and only six fell five percent or more. Each of those six had its own private reason, a regulator's order, a set of results, a margin. None of them was about the market. But five of the ten biggest gainers have no explanation anyone could establish, and neither does JNK India [SAY: J-N-K India], down six percent while its own group rose over two percent. Nobody can tell you why those moved, and anyone who says otherwise is guessing.

Now, what to watch. Wednesday brings the Reserve Bank of India's [SAY: R-B-I] rate decision. The repo rate has been held at five point two five percent for three meetings and economists are unanimous that it stays there, so the language and the inflation forecast will matter more than the number itself. And tomorrow, watch whether that gap between the Sensex and the Nifty narrows.
