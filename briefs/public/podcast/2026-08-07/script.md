Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on today's session.

The thing that moved the Indian market today was not oil and not earnings. It was a draft. The Reserve Bank of India published a proposal on Thursday that would stop lenders who are not banks from offering revolving credit, the kind of limit you can repay and draw again. It is not law. Comments are open until the twenty eighth of August. The market priced it today as though it were already in force.

Here is where things closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand five hundred and seventy, down zero point two seven percent. The Sensex fell zero point five eight percent, and that wider fall is arithmetic rather than a second signal, because the Sensex carries more financial weight and financials were where the damage was. Three hundred and twenty shares rose against three hundred and sixty two that fell, so the selling was concentrated rather than broad. The fear gauge did not move at all. Two things we could not see tonight: thirteen of the fifteen sector indices gave us no usable reading, and the government bond curve came back empty for a fourth day running.

Emerging markets went the other way. The four funds that track emerging market shares and bonds all rose while India fell. They trade in American hours, after our close, so they are a read on the day rather than a cause of it. But it is the cleanest evidence we have that today was made in India.

Two things sit behind the session. On the supportive side, Brent crude fell again today, and is down roughly nine point four percent over the week. For a country that imports most of its oil, a week like that matters far more than any single day. On the worrying side, the rupee weakened, to just over ninety five to the dollar, on a day the dollar itself fell half a percent against everything else. When your currency slips while the dollar is being sold, the weakness is your own.

Now the movers. The biggest fall was the one I already named. Bajaj Finance dropped almost six percent, the worst share in both benchmarks. It had touched a fresh fifty two week high a week earlier, on a quarter with profit up twenty nine percent. Nothing about its earnings changed in seven days. What changed was the list of products it might be allowed to sell.

The biggest gain among the large companies was Siemens Energy India, up twelve percent. Revenue rose thirty nine percent and operating profit almost seventy four percent, with an order book above nineteen thousand crore rupees. Growth, margins and orders all moving in the same direction.

The oddest was Shivalik Bimetal Controls, up twenty percent and locked at its daily price limit. Revenue up a third, profit up forty five percent. In the same twenty four hours the company also disclosed that its statutory auditor had resigned. The market priced the first document and not the second.

And five other shares rose more than seven percent today, and I could not establish why any of them moved. We checked the exchange filings, the datelines, the broker actions. It all came back empty.

Here is what struck me most about today. The Indian companies that buy crude did nothing with that weekly fall. Bharat Petroleum finished slightly lower, Indian Oil up half a percent, Reliance Industries up under one percent. Now, Oil and Natural Gas Corporation sits on the other side of that trade. It pumps crude rather than buying it, so a cheaper barrel cuts what it earns. It closed up zero point four percent. So the three that stood to gain sat still, and the one that stood to lose went up. That is a second session running where neither side of India's oil chain has priced a large move in crude, and I cannot tell you why.

Now, what to watch. Titan, Ola Electric and Hitachi Energy India all filed their quarters after the close today, so those are Monday's news and not today's cause. Wednesday brings July inflation and factory output. And the date that matters most for today's story is the twenty eighth of August, when comments close on that draft. Whether the ban survives at all is still an open question.
