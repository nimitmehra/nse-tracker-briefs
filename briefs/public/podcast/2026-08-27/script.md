Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Thursday's session.

More than half of Thursday's fall in the Nifty was one share. H-D-F-C Bank closed down two point two three percent, and on about thirteen percent of the index that is most of a fall of zero point four eight percent. The rest of the banks did not sell off at all. Kotak Mahindra Bank rose one point eight percent and I-C-I-C-I Bank rose almost one percent.

So this is not a banking story. What is wearing H-D-F-C Bank down is its own arithmetic. The gap between what it earns on loans and pays on deposits is at a record low of three point two six percent. Cheap current and savings deposits are now thirty two percent of its book, against thirty eight percent two years ago. And foreign investors have cut their holding for a fifth straight quarter. The share is down about twenty seven percent so far this year, against a Sensex down about eight.

Here is where the market closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand and ninety, down zero point four eight percent. The Sensex fell zero point seven percent, and it fell harder for two mechanical reasons. H-D-F-C Bank counts for more there, and Thursday was the monthly settlement day for Sensex derivatives, which concentrates trading in the last hour.

Underneath the index it was a genuinely weak day. Of seven hundred and seven shares we could price, two hundred and fifty three rose and four hundred and forty nine fell. The typical share lost zero point four six percent.

The selling was concentrated rather than general. Not one of eleven cement companies rose. Metals were next, and state owned banks after them, with exactly one gainer in twelve. The gold lenders were sold too, Manappuram Finance down four point seven six percent and Muthoot Finance down three point eight, because gold is lower over the week and these companies can only lend a capped fraction of the value of the gold pledged with them. Autos, property, capital goods and consumption all held up better than the market. Pharmaceuticals were the one clean positive, up zero point eight four percent, with fourteen of nineteen names higher. Nine of the eleven official sector indices gave us no usable number for the day, so those groupings are ones we built ourselves from the individual shares.

The fear gauge, India V-I-X, rose four point seven one percent to eleven point zero seven. A rising fear gauge alongside a falling index is at least consistent, which is more than the two sessions before managed. But it is lifting off a floor. Eleven is still around sixty percent below its own one year high.

Asia split on Thursday, and India went with the weaker half. Korea rose one point five three percent, Shanghai one point one three and Taiwan zero point three one, while Japan and Hong Kong fell.

Two things sit over this market, and they disagree with each other.

The supportive one is the Reserve Bank of India's August bulletin, published on Tuesday. Its reading is that the economy stayed resilient through the June quarter, with manufacturing and services both growing, and both exports and imports up in double digits in July. That last point cuts against the idea that the eighteen percent American tariff is crushing Indian exports.

The worrying one is the rain. Cumulative rainfall through the twenty sixth of August is thirteen percent below normal, and that shortfall has widened since early August. Three hundred and ten districts are classed deficient, the east and northeast are twenty seven percent short, and the forecast is for below normal rain through September on a strengthening El Nino.

Both readings are true, and quoting either one alone will mislead you. The bulletin describes July, when sowing had recovered. The deficit widened after that, so the bulletin is not wrong, it is a month behind the number. Thursday's market sided with the rain. Fertiliser and agrochemical makers fell, and so did the rural facing consumer and tractor names. We record that the direction lines up and we do not claim it as the cause, because the rainfall figure is Wednesday's data and not a Thursday release.

Now the movers, and the first thing to say is that our own list is wrong about the day. It surfaced ten risers and no fallers, on a session in which four hundred and forty nine of seven hundred and seven shares fell. That is not the market, it is our screen's own rule. Outside the Nifty fifty, a share has to fall ten percent, not five, before it shows up at all. Twenty six shares fell three percent or more on Thursday. So ten green rows is not a good day.

The biggest riser was Bombay Burmah Trading Corporation, up thirteen point one four percent, and it is the one name on the list with a document behind it. The Supreme Court withdrew an observation it had made in May, that four thousand six hundred and fifty five crore rupees of lease rent was still recoverable from the company over a former tea estate in Tamil Nadu. That sum is about forty one percent of what the whole company is worth. Read it precisely though. The court withdrew the observation on procedure, because the amount had never been put to the company in a notice or settled after a hearing, and it did not rule that nothing is owed. The order itself is dated the nineteenth of August, and the share did nothing on it. What moved it on Thursday is the company's own filing to the exchange, made at three fifty nine on Wednesday afternoon, after that session had settled and public before Thursday opened.

Nine of the other ten risers have no cause we could establish. Four stale stories were caught and thrown out along the way, and every one of them would have read as Thursday's news if nobody had checked the date.

The biggest fall was Ramco Cements, down five point one one percent, sitting on top of that broad cement weakness. We could not establish a cause for that one either.

Here is what struck me about Thursday. The biggest company result in the world landed before India opened. Nvidia reported after Wednesday's American close, with revenue up one hundred and six percent on the year and guidance ahead of what the market expected. Korea and Taiwan rose on it. Indian software services fell anyway, with the sector down zero point three two percent, Tata Consultancy Services down zero point nine five and Infosys down zero point eight two.

This next part is our reasoning and not a sourced finding. Korea and Taiwan build the memory and the chips that a bigger chip forecast mechanically orders more of. Indian technology companies sell people's time, and no order for chips turns into an Indian services contract. So the link is one sided rather than absent, which makes the non reaction unsurprising. One caution before anyone builds a theory on it. The same pattern ran backwards on Monday, when Korea sold off sharply and India barely moved. Twice inverted in three sessions is something to describe, not a trend.

The date to keep is Monday the thirty first of August, and three things land in the same closing auction. India's June quarter growth figure is out, with fifty eight economists putting it around seven point one percent against seven point eight the quarter before. The M-S-C-I index review takes effect at that close, which means funds that track the index have to trade it whatever they think. And the Reserve Bank of India's special window for dollar deposits from Indians abroad shuts that day.
