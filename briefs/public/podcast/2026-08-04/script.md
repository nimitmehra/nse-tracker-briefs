Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on today's session.

The Reserve Bank of India decides on interest rates at ten tomorrow morning, and the number to watch is not the rate. It is the Bank's inflation forecast of five point one percent, built on oil at about ninety-five dollars a barrel. Oil has been much cheaper than that all week. Cheaper oil argues for a lower forecast. But the weather office has just cut its August rain forecast, and less rain pushes food prices up. So the two inputs point in opposite directions.

Indian shares fell into that decision. The Nifty fifty closed down zero point six four percent, the Sensex down zero point two seven percent, and the fear gauge rose about two percent to a one-week high. So the market fell and fear rose together. Nearly four hundred shares fell against two hundred and eighty that rose.

The two headline indices spent most of today moving in opposite directions. Since Monday, India has set closing prices a new way, by one auction in the last twenty minutes. On one newspaper's count, the N-S-E auction carried about a hundred and sixty-five rupees for every one on the B-S-E. A thin auction moves prices further, so one company can close at two different prices on the two exchanges. That is plumbing, never a signal.

On the worrying side, that rain forecast. August is now seen about six percent below normal, after a July that ran one percent above, so this is a forecast turning, not a shortfall. And the Bank named monsoon uncertainty when it raised that forecast in June.

On the supportive side, oil. Crude traded near eighty-six dollars a barrel, up almost three percent, while Indian markets were open. By quarter past nine tonight it was seventy-nine dollars, a nine percent round trip in a day. That fall came with American claims that a Gulf settlement is close. Nothing is signed. Iran says it is not negotiating with the United States, its talks are with Oman, and it calls that route temporary.

Now the movers. Six names fell five percent or more today. The biggest move in either direction was Greaves Cotton, down fourteen percent. The engine maker filed results this afternoon. Revenue rose thirty-one percent, but net profit fell twenty-two percent. It sold much more and kept less of it. That is what the market punished. Search that name tonight and the top article says profit jumped two hundred and twenty percent, from July last year.

The second biggest fall was L-I-C, the state-owned life insurer, down almost nine percent. The government is selling six and a half percent of it at a fixed price eleven percent below Monday's close. The share drifted towards it. Supply, not a verdict on the insurer.

The biggest gain was Ather Energy, the electric two-wheeler maker, up almost fourteen percent. Revenue rose eighty-nine percent, and for the first time the core business made money instead of losing it. Said honestly, this is still a loss-making company.

Here is what struck me today. Property was the worst corner of the market on the eve of a rate decision. It is tempting to call that rate fear. It was not. Three of the five worst property names had filed weak results, and D-L-F's new sales bookings fell about ninety-four percent. So do not blame tomorrow's rate decision for a fall that weak results already explain.

One admission. C-G Power rose six and a half percent with no company announcement on Monday or today. Nine of the thirty-two moves we graded have no established cause. Nobody can tell you why, and that is truer than a tidy answer.

Now, what to watch. Ten tomorrow morning, the Reserve Bank's decision, the Governor's press conference at twelve. Listen for whether the inflation forecast moves off five point one, and whether the Bank restates what it assumes oil will cost.

That's your brief. Before I sign off: this has been general market commentary, not investment advice. For investment advice tailored to your situation, consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser. Markets are risky; you may lose money; act with care. See you tomorrow.
