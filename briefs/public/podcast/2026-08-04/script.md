Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on today's session.

The Reserve Bank of India decides on interest rates at ten tomorrow morning, and the number to watch is not the rate. It is the Bank's inflation forecast for this year, five point one percent, which was built on crude oil averaging about ninety-five dollars a barrel. Oil has traded nowhere near ninety-five all week. But the monsoon has just moved the other way, so this is not the easy call it sounds like.

Indian shares fell into that decision. The Nifty fifty closed down zero point six four percent, the Sensex down zero point two seven percent, and the fear gauge, India VIX, rose two point one eight percent to a one-week high. The market fell and fear rose at the same time, on the eve of a rate decision. Two hundred and seventy-nine shares rose against three hundred and ninety-two that fell, out of six hundred and eighty we track. Most of our sector readings failed today, so the only two we can stand behind are technology, down zero point eight percent, and pharma, down zero point two percent.

The two headline indices also spent most of today moving in opposite directions. This is day three of India's new closing auction, and the explanation finally arrived. Auction turnover was fifteen hundred and forty-two crore rupees on the N-S-E against nine crore rupees on the B-S-E, roughly a hundred and sixty-five to one. A thin auction moves a price further for every rupee traded, so the same company can close at two different levels on two exchanges. That is plumbing. It is never a signal.

Asia was firm today and India was not. Korea's main index rose one point six percent on a memory-chip partnership for artificial-intelligence hardware, while India fell, so whatever moved India today was Indian.

Now the two things hanging over tomorrow. On the worrying side, the weather office now forecasts below-normal rain in August, ninety-four percent of the long-period average. Rain through July was one percent above normal, so this is a forecast turning rather than a shortfall that has happened. But the Bank named monsoon uncertainty as one of its reasons for raising that inflation forecast in June.

On the supportive side, oil, and here the timestamp matters. Crude traded near eighty-six dollars a barrel, up almost three percent, while Indian markets were open. That is the rising-oil line in tonight's close reports, and it is the only crude number that belongs to today's session. By quarter past nine tonight it was down at seventy-nine dollars, more than five percent lower on the day, a round trip of about nine percent inside one day. Underneath the price, supply keeps stacking up. The big producers added output for a fifth straight month, Turkey and Iraq extended a pipeline deal, and Caspian flows restarted. The louder story is a Gulf settlement, and there nothing is signed. Iran's foreign ministry says it is not negotiating with the United States, its talks are with Oman, and the route it is discussing is temporary.

Now the movers. The biggest move on the board, in either direction, was Greaves Cotton, down fourteen percent. The engine maker filed results in the afternoon. Revenue rose thirty-one percent, but operating profit fell one percent and net profit fell twenty-two percent. Growth bought at a worsening margin is what punishes a share price hardest. One warning if you go searching that name tonight. The article sitting at the top of the results says profit jumped two hundred and twenty percent, and it is from July last year. Today's number is a twenty-two percent decline.

The biggest gain was Ather Energy, the electric two-wheeler maker, up fourteen percent. Revenue rose eighty-nine percent, and operating profit turned positive for the first time, nine crore rupees against an operating loss of a hundred and six crore a year earlier. That sign change is the substance. Said honestly, this is still a loss-making company.

Third, L-I-C fell eight and a half percent, and that one is mechanical. The government is selling up to six and a half percent of the insurer at a floor price of three hundred and eighty-two rupees, about eleven percent below Monday's close. The share drifted towards the floor and closed at three hundred and ninety-one. That is the ordinary gravity of a share sale in progress, not a verdict on the business.

Here is what struck me today. Property was the worst corner of the market, on the eve of a rate decision, and it is tempting to call that rate fear. It was not. Three of the five worst property names had filed weak results inside the window, and D-L-F's new sales bookings fell about ninety-four percent from a year earlier. Do not hand the Reserve Bank a move that already has an earnings explanation.

One admission before I finish. C-G Power rose six and a half percent today, adding roughly eight and a half thousand crore rupees of value, with no filing on either day and nothing dated to today. Nine of the thirty-two moves we graded have no cause we could establish. Nobody can tell you why those moved, and that is a truer answer than a tidy one.

Now, what to watch. Ten tomorrow morning, the Reserve Bank's decision, with the Governor's press conference at twelve. The repo rate has sat at five point two five percent for three meetings. So listen for whether the inflation forecast moves off five point one, and whether the Bank restates what it assumes oil will cost.
