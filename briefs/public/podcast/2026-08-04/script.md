Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on today's session.

The Reserve Bank of India decides on interest rates at ten tomorrow morning, and the number to watch is not the rate. It is the Bank's inflation forecast of five point one percent, built on crude oil at about ninety-five dollars a barrel. Oil has traded nowhere near that all week. But the monsoon has just moved the other way, so this is not the easy call it sounds like.

Indian shares fell into that decision. The Nifty fifty closed down zero point six four percent, the Sensex down zero point two seven percent, and the fear gauge rose two point one eight percent to a one-week high. So the market fell and fear rose together. Two hundred and seventy-nine shares rose and three hundred and ninety-two fell, out of six hundred and eighty.

The two headline indices also spent most of today moving in opposite directions, on day three of India's new closing auction. Turnover in that auction was fifteen hundred and forty-two crore rupees on the N-S-E against nine crore on the B-S-E, about a hundred and sixty-five to one. A thin auction moves prices further, so one company can close at two different levels. That is plumbing, never a signal.

Asia was firm today and India was not. Korea's main index rose one point six percent on a memory-chip deal for artificial-intelligence hardware. So whatever moved India today was Indian.

On the worrying side, the weather office now forecasts below-normal August rain, ninety-four percent of the long-period average. Rain through July was one percent above normal, so this is a forecast turning rather than a shortfall. But the Bank named monsoon uncertainty when it raised that inflation forecast in June.

On the supportive side, oil, and the timestamp matters. Crude traded near eighty-six dollars a barrel, up almost three percent, while Indian markets were open. It is the only crude number that belongs to today's session. By quarter past nine tonight it was seventy-nine dollars, a round trip of about nine percent in one day. But nothing on a Gulf settlement is signed. Iran says it is not negotiating with the United States, its talks are with Oman, and that route is temporary.

Now the movers. Six names fell five percent or more today. The biggest move in either direction was Greaves Cotton, down fourteen percent. The engine maker filed results in the afternoon. Revenue rose thirty-one percent, but operating profit fell one percent and net profit fell twenty-two percent. Growth bought at a worsening margin punishes a share price hardest. And if you search that name tonight, the article at the top says profit jumped two hundred and twenty percent, which is from July last year.

The biggest gain was Ather Energy, the electric two-wheeler maker, up fourteen percent. Revenue rose eighty-nine percent, and operating profit turned positive for the first time. That sign change is the substance. Said honestly, this is still a loss-making company.

Here is what struck me today. Property was the worst corner of the market on the eve of a rate decision, and it is tempting to call that rate fear. It was not. Three of the five worst property names had filed weak results, and D-L-F's new sales bookings fell about ninety-four percent. Do not hand the Reserve Bank a move that already has an earnings explanation.

One admission. C-G Power rose six and a half percent with no filing on either day and nothing dated to today. Nine of the thirty-two moves we graded have no established cause. Nobody can tell you why, and that is a truer answer than a tidy one.

Now, what to watch. Ten tomorrow morning, the Reserve Bank's decision, and the Governor's press conference at twelve. Listen for whether the inflation forecast moves off five point one, and whether the Bank restates what it assumes oil will cost.
