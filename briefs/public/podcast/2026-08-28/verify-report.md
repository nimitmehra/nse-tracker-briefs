## The three self-flagged lines — independent judgment

**1. "See you Monday" in place of the exemplar's "See you tomorrow" — CORRECT, and it breaks nothing.**
Recording Saturday for Friday's session, "See you tomorrow" would promise a Sunday episode that does not exist. The substitution is the only honest option. It does not touch the firebreak: the disclaimer block (sentences 51-53) is verbatim and intact, and the sign-off sits after it, outside the load-bearing text. Checked against the publication record — the daily podcast folders run Monday to Friday with no weekend editions, and the weekly brief publishes on Fridays, not Sundays, so nothing is being promised that will not arrive. No conflict. Ship it.

**2. "A brokerage note holding a positive view on it was doing the rounds." — NEUTRAL REPORTING, not endorsement. Keep.**
Three things hold it on the right side of the line. The view is attributed to a third party, not adopted. It is reported as circulating, not as correct. And the very next sentence discounts it on timing. Avoiding the word "Buy" was the right call independent of the ban: on air, "a Buy rating" is heard as an instruction no matter whose it is, whereas "holding a positive view" is heard as a description of someone else's opinion. Note the four-day-old fact is the load-bearing half and it is present. One small blemish: "doing the rounds" is mild trade journalese; "was still circulating" is cleaner. Not a flag.

**3. "This was borrowed share price movement, not borrowed business." — a claim about the day. Keep it.**
The test is whether the line asserts something the day's evidence supports, or imports a standing view about how markets work. It is the first. It arrives after four stated pieces of Friday-specific evidence and does nothing but compress them: prices moved, the underlying business did not. Strip the line out and the episode leaves the chip-demand chain standing, which would be the worse error by a distance. It is close to the banned thesis-framing pattern in *cadence* — the balanced X-not-Y shape is the tell — but the content is a summary of what was just proved, not a frame laid over it. Minor listenability caveat: "borrowed" is a coinage and the second half arrives fast; a listener half-attending may catch "borrowed business" and lose the contrast. Optional insurance, one clause earlier: "The price came from America. The business did not." The writer's judgment that this is the episode's strongest line is right.

## Structure deviation, cuts, word count

**Connections wedge placed immediately after the causal chain — SERVES THE LISTENER. Endorse.**
Two independent reasons, and I did not need the writer's argument for either. First, the chain in sentences 14-18 is delivered as an explanation; if three minutes of movers intervene before the rebuttal, the listener has already stored it as true and the rebuttal has to demolish a belief rather than prevent one. Second, and more practically, the Mastek paragraph *depends* on the wedge: sentence 37 splits Mastek's rise into a sector part and an unexplained part, which is unintelligible unless the listener has already been told the sector move was beta. Adjacency is not a stylistic preference here — the later section stops working without it. The standard running order would have produced a worse episode.

**Declared cuts — nothing load-bearing lost.**
Tejas Networks (+7.63%, TCS/BSNL letter of intent filed after Thursday's close) is the only genuine loss, and it is small: its lesson is after-close timing, which the episode already teaches three times (sentences 17, 36, 49). A fourth instance would have been repetition, not reinforcement. The EM/Asia cut is not merely defensible, it is the correct call and the highest-value decision in the file: every global index in the source is stamped Thursday, so writing that section would have compared Friday's India against Thursday's world — a wrong-day comparison, which is exactly the class of error this gate exists to catch. Cutting it earned more than including it would have. July industrial production had no causal role in Friday's story; its absence costs nothing.

**730 words against a 700 soft ceiling — the overage earns itself, but it is not free.**
The band exists to protect duration, and 4.42 minutes sits inside the 3.5-4.5 target, so nothing is actually violated. The extra words are in the wedge and in the Ather "no new money" clarification, both of which are the episode's reason to exist. But the writer's claim that nothing was cut to pay for it is beside the point: there are roughly twenty-five words of slack sitting in the four sentences on the punch list, and removing them fixes the flags *and* the overage in one pass. So the honest verdict is that the length is justified by content while the script simultaneously carries avoidable slack.
