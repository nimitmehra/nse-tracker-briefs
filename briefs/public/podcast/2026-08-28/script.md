# Podcast Script — 2026-08-28 (evening recap: one sector carried the whole market)

Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Friday's session.

One sector carried Friday's entire market. Indian software shares rose three and a half percent, with every one of the nine in that index higher. Take them out and Friday was dead level. And the reason came from America, a session late.

Here is where the market closed on Friday. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand one hundred and seventy five, up about a third of a percent. But more shares fell than rose. Three hundred and forty three were up, three hundred and sixty one were down. Inside the Nifty fifty, if you set the technology shares aside, eighteen rose and eighteen fell, and the typical one did not move at all. The banks did not join in either. Nifty Bank closed flat. One caution on sectors. Nine of the eleven sector indices did not publish a usable move for the day, so software and pharma are the only two we can quote from the exchange itself.

So why software, and why Friday. Nvidia, the American chip designer, reported its results on Wednesday evening American time. The verdict arrived the following day, as a broad rise across American software companies, and it landed at Thursday's American close. That is about half past one on Friday morning here. India had already shut on Thursday, three and a half hours before Wall Street even opened, and Indian software shares actually fell that day. So Friday morning was India's first chance to react.

Here is what struck me about that. The tempting sentence is that a chip company's demand turns into Indian software revenue. I do not think that is what happened, and four things say so. Companies with no artificial intelligence story at all rose just as much. The leaders were smaller, jumpier names rather than the big exporters most exposed to the theme, and Infosys, the name most people would assume led, lagged its own sector. The rupee moved the wrong way for the story. A weaker rupee is what helps a company that earns dollars and spends rupees, and the rupee did not weaken. And no brokerage published a note on the sector all day. This was borrowed share price movement, not borrowed business. A real thing, and a fragile one.

Two things underneath the session. On the supportive side, July industrial production came in six point seven percent higher than a year earlier, and inside it capital goods rose sixteen point one percent. That is machinery and equipment, which companies only order when they intend to expand. On the worrying side, India's own bond market is pricing rate rises. The ten year government bond yield sat near six point nine percent on Friday, a two month high, on a heavy week of government borrowing and renewed expectation that the Reserve Bank of India could raise rates.

On the money. Foreign investors sold about five thousand crore rupees on Friday while domestic institutions bought about five thousand two hundred crore, so foreigners were selling into a rising market. And the fear gauge, India V-I-X, closed at ten point six eight, barely above its lowest level in a year.

Now the movers, and there were no fallers to speak of. Not one share fell five percent on Friday, while twenty four rose five percent or more.

The biggest gainer was Mastek, up about eighteen percent. Mastek is a mid-sized Indian software services company. During the session a brokerage note was circulating, holding a positive view on the company as a possible turnaround and citing an order book of about three hundred and ten million dollars. But that note was dated four days earlier, so it does not explain why the move happened on Friday. The sector accounts for roughly three and a half points of an eighteen percent jump. The rest has no dated trigger, and I would rather tell you that than invent one.

Second was Ather Energy, the electric scooter maker, up eight percent to a genuine new fifty two week high. Hero MotoCorp, already Ather's largest shareholder, bought about seventeen hundred and fifty eight crore rupees of Ather shares from an existing holder in a block deal, taking it to about thirty two point eight percent of the company. Say this plainly. No new money went into Ather. Hero bought shares that already existed, from someone who already owned them, and the deal completes in early September. Separately, Ola Electric launched its S-one-Z scooter during Friday's session at just under eighty thousand rupees, which is well below the price bracket the market had expected Ather's next scooter to occupy.

Third was Tejas Networks, which makes telecom network equipment, up seven and a half percent. It received a letter of intent worth about fifteen hundred and thirty seven crore rupees from Tata Consultancy Services, for radio equipment for the state operator B-S-N-L's fourth generation network. That filing went out on Thursday evening, after the close, which is why Friday was the first session that could price it.

Now, what to watch, and it all lands on Monday. India's June quarter growth figure comes out, and the forecasts run from about six point nine percent to eight percent, which is an unusually wide spread for a number that big. The M-S-C-I index reshuffle transacts in Monday's closing auction, so funds that track that index have to trade at the close whatever they think of it. The Reserve Bank's special window for foreign currency deposits from Indians living abroad shuts. And Monday is the first Indian session that can price Friday night's Jackson Hole speech, in which the American central bank's new chairman said inflation is still too high and that the Fed may have to raise rates. That speech explains nothing about Friday here, because it landed long after India had closed. It is Monday's input, not Friday's.
