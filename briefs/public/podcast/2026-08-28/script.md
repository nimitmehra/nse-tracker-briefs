Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Friday's session.

One sector carried Friday's entire market. Indian software shares rose three and a half percent, with every one of the nine in that index higher. The reason came from America, a session late.

Here is where the market closed on Friday. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand one hundred and seventy five, up about a third of a percent. But more shares fell than rose. Three hundred and forty three were up, three hundred and sixty one were down. Set the technology shares aside and the Nifty fifty was flat, eighteen up and eighteen down. The banks did not join in, with Nifty Bank closing level. And nine of the eleven sector indices published no usable move.

So why software, and why Friday. Nvidia, the American chip designer, reported results on Wednesday evening American time. The verdict arrived a day later, as a broad rise across American software companies, and it landed at Thursday's American close, about half past one on Friday morning here. India had already shut on Thursday, three and a half hours before Wall Street opened, and Indian software shares fell that day. Friday morning was India's first chance to react.

Here is what struck me. The tempting sentence is that a chip company's demand turns into Indian software revenue. I do not think that is what happened, and four things say so. Companies with no artificial intelligence story rose just as much. The leaders were smaller, jumpier names, and Infosys, the one most people would assume led, lagged its own sector. A weaker rupee is what helps a company that earns dollars and spends rupees, and the rupee moved the other way. And no brokerage published a note on the sector all day. This was borrowed share price movement, not borrowed business.

Underneath, India's own bond market is pricing rate rises. The ten year government bond yield sat near six point nine percent on Friday, a two month high, on a heavy week of government borrowing and renewed expectation that the Reserve Bank of India could raise rates.

On the money, foreign investors sold about five thousand crore rupees while domestic institutions bought about five thousand two hundred crore. Foreigners were selling into a rising market. And the fear gauge, India V-I-X, closed near its lowest level in a year.

Now the movers, and there were no fallers to speak of. Not one share fell five percent on Friday, while twenty four rose five percent or more.

The biggest gainer was Mastek, a mid-sized Indian software services company, up about eighteen percent. A brokerage note holding a positive view on it was doing the rounds. But that note was four days old, so it does not explain why Friday. The sector explains about three and a half of those eighteen points. The rest has no dated trigger.

Second, Ather Energy, the electric scooter maker, up eight percent to a genuine new fifty two week high. Hero MotoCorp, already Ather's largest shareholder, bought about seventeen hundred and fifty eight crore rupees of shares from an existing holder, taking it to about thirty two point eight percent of the company. Say this plainly. No new money went into Ather. Hero bought shares that already existed, and the deal completes in early September. Separately, Ola Electric launched its S-one-Z scooter during Friday's session at just under eighty thousand rupees, below where the market expected Ather's next scooter to sit.

Now, what to watch, and it all lands on Monday. India's June quarter growth figure comes out, with forecasts running from about six point nine percent to eight percent, an unusually wide spread. The M-S-C-I index reshuffle transacts in the closing auction, and the Reserve Bank's window for foreign currency deposits from Indians abroad shuts. And Monday is India's first chance to price Friday night's Jackson Hole speech, where the American central bank's new chairman said inflation is still too high and the Fed may have to raise rates. That speech landed long after India closed, so it explains nothing about Friday.
