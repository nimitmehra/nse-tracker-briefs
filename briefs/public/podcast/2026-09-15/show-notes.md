## Episode title

2026-09-15 | NSE Daily Brief: Why the RBI's bond sale hit property shares

## Description

The Nifty 50 closed at 23,118.60 on Tuesday, down 1.19% and its lowest close in five months, in the first session after the Ganesh Chaturthi holiday. Three things turned against India over the long weekend: Brent above $107 after a drone strike shut Saudi Arabia's main export pipeline, a US 10-year yield that touched 5% with a Fed hike priced at about 92% for Wednesday, and the Reserve Bank of India's plan to sell ₹1 trillion of bonds from Thursday to drain surplus cash. Foreign investors sold a net ₹2,977.90 crore, a fifth straight day; domestic institutions bought ₹2,686.00 crore. IT was the only sector that rose. Movers: Solar Industries (−13.64%, the all-cash Omnia acquisition) and Tata Chemicals (+19.99%, the reported RBI letter pushing Tata Sons towards a listing). The connection: how a record reserves build became a cash surplus, a bond-sale programme and a higher yield that sold realty and lenders.

## Key points

- **The close:** Nifty 50 23,118.60 (−1.19%, five-month closing low); Sensex 74,003.82 (−1.04%); Bank Nifty 55,794.75 (−1.43%); Nifty IT +2.19%, the only sector up, after a morning high above +5% on a reported "slower AI" catalyst; Nifty Smallcap 250 −2.44%. Rupee 95.945 per dollar, a seven-week low.
- **What turned against India:** Brent above US$107 during Indian hours (Saudi East–West pipeline shut since the 10 September drone strike); US 10-year yield touched 5% on Monday, Fed hike priced at about 92% for Wednesday; FII net −₹2,977.90 crore, DII net +₹2,686.00 crore.
- **Movers:** Solar Industries −13.64% on the ~₹12,951 crore all-cash Omnia deal, funding undisclosed; Tata Chemicals +19.99% (band-locked) on the reported RBI letter telling Tata Sons it cannot surrender its registration and should move towards a listing. No listing announced, no date.
- **The connection:** the summer's dollar-deposit swap window left about ₹10.5 trillion of surplus cash in the banking system; the RBI's ₹1 trillion outright bond sale (₹50,000 crore on Thursday 17 September, then ₹25,000 crore each on 21 and 28 September) means more supply and a 10-year yield reported at about 7.1%, a four-month high. Realty −4.04% and lenders (Shriram Finance −4.74%) are the cost-of-money trade. Liquidity management, not a rate rise; the repo rate is unchanged at 5.25%.
- **What to watch:** Fed decision Wednesday 16 September at about 23:30 IST, after India's close, so Thursday is the first session that can trade it; Thursday's ₹50,000 crore bond sale (bids 09:30–10:30), where the cut-off yield is the read.
