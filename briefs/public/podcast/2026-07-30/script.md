Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toroIQ. Your read on today's session.

The index went up today and most of the market went down. Two out of every three shares ended lower, four hundred and fifty-seven falling against two hundred and fifteen rising. Anyone who read only the index number today read the opposite of what happened.

Here is where the market closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty-four thousand three hundred and seventeen, up zero point two eight percent, and the Sensex up zero point three five percent. But the median stock fell about six-tenths of a percent, and the fear gauge, India VIX, rose over one percent. Fear rising on a rising market is unusual. Auto shares were the strength, up over one and a half percent as a group, carried by Mahindra and Mahindra's quarter. Property developers were the weakest, down about two percent, with insurers and non-bank lenders sold too.

Today was also the Sensex monthly expiry, on the B-S-E [SAY: B-S-E], not the National Stock Exchange. Expiry days concentrate trading in the biggest index companies, which props the headline up while the average stock falls.

Across Asia, Korea, Shanghai and Taiwan fell while Japan rose, and India finished mid-pack. That was a pause in North Asia's chip leadership, not money moving into India.

Now the two forces underneath. The pressure is interest rates. The Reserve Bank of India [SAY: R-B-I] decides on rates next week, and a Reuters poll of seventy-two economists found sixty-eight expecting no change, four expecting a rate hike, and not one expecting a cut. So the market bought the earnings story and sold anything that depends on rates coming down. The support is oil. Brent crude eased about one point two percent today to eighty-nine dollars and sixty-six cents a barrel. But it is still up almost twenty-three percent over the month, and India imports about eighty-five percent of its oil.

Now the movers. The biggest gain was Balkrishna Industries [SAY: bal-KRISH-na], a tyre maker, up almost eleven percent. It reported revenue up twenty-five percent and profit up fifty-six percent. A clean result-driven move, from a share still well below its one-year high.

Second was Redington [SAY: RED-ing-ton], a technology-products distributor, up over eight percent to a one-year high on record quarterly revenue, up thirty-four percent. But the company itself says part of that came from higher personal-computer prices caused by a global memory-chip shortage. That is margin handed to it by a component squeeze, not margin it earned.

The sharpest fall was PCBL Chemical [SAY: P-C-B-L], a carbon black and chemicals maker, down about ten percent on a profit that rose sixty-five percent. But roughly seventy crore rupees of that profit was a one-off inventory gain, brokerages pointed out, not operating earnings. Strip it out and the growth is a fraction of the headline.

Here is what struck me most about today. Three times over, the market priced where a number came from rather than how big it was. PCBL was one. Hexaware [SAY: HEX-a-ware], a technology services firm, was the same idea in reverse. Its profit fell thirteen percent and the share fell almost seven, but its operating margin actually widened by three and a half percentage points. The whole decline sat below the operating line, in one-off items and tax. And KPIT Technologies [SAY: K-P-I-T], which does engineering work for carmakers, fell nearly eight percent on its guidance rather than its quarter, after European carmakers suddenly cut spending. The size of a profit told you very little today. Where it came from told you almost everything.

Now, what to watch. Four large companies filed results in the last eight to twenty-one minutes of trading today. Vedanta, Bajaj Finance, Hyundai [SAY: HUN-day] and Mankind. Their numbers had no time to trade. Hyundai rose over one percent on profit that fell thirty-five percent, and Bajaj Finance closed flat on profit up almost twenty-eight percent. Tomorrow is when those get priced. And the week's real event is the Reserve Bank's rate decision next Wednesday, the fifth of August.
