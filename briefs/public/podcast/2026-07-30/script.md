Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toroIQ. Your read on today's session.

The index went up today and most of the market went down. Two out of every three shares ended lower, four hundred and fifty-seven falling against two hundred and fifteen rising. Anyone who read only the index number read the opposite of what happened.

Here is where the market closed. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty-four thousand three hundred and seventeen, up zero point two eight percent. But the typical stock fell about six-tenths of a percent, and the fear gauge, India VIX, rose over one percent. Fear rising on a rising market is unusual. Auto shares led, up over one and a half percent as a group, after a strong quarter from Mahindra and Mahindra, the tractor and SUV maker. Property developers were the weakest, down about two percent, with insurers and non-bank lenders sold as well. Part of the strength in the biggest names was mechanical, because the monthly Sensex contracts settled today, on the Bombay exchange, not the National Stock Exchange.

The pressure on the day was interest rates. The Reserve Bank of India [SAY: R-B-I] decides on rates next week, and a Reuters poll of seventy-two economists found sixty-eight expecting no change, four a hike, and none a cut. So the market bought the earnings story and sold anything that depends on rates falling. The support was oil. Brent crude eased about one point two percent today, to just under ninety dollars a barrel. But it is still up almost twenty-three percent over the month, and India imports about eighty-five percent of its oil. Today's dip helps, but only for as long as the oil keeps physically flowing.

Now the movers. The biggest gain was Balkrishna Industries [SAY: bal-KRISH-na], a tyre maker, up almost eleven percent on revenue up twenty-five percent and profit up fifty-six percent.

Second was Redington [SAY: RED-ing-ton], which distributes technology products, up over eight percent to a one-year high on record quarterly revenue. But the company itself says part of that came from higher personal-computer prices caused by a global memory shortage. That is profit handed to it by a shortage, not profit it earned.

Here is what struck me most today. Three times over, the market cared about where a profit came from rather than how big it was. The sharpest fall was PCBL Chemical [SAY: P-C-B-L], which makes carbon black for tyres. It fell about ten percent on a profit that rose sixty-five percent. Brokerages pointed out that roughly seventy crore rupees of it was a one-off inventory gain, not money the business earned. Strip that out and the growth is a fraction of the headline. Hexaware [SAY: HEX-a-ware], a technology services firm, was the same idea in reverse. Its profit fell thirteen percent and the share fell almost seven percent, but the profit from its actual business grew. The entire fall came from one-off items and tax, not from the business itself. And KPIT Technologies [SAY: K-P-I-T], whose customers are carmakers, fell nearly eight percent. Not on the quarter it reported, but on what it said about the next one, after European carmakers cut spending. The size of a profit told you little today. Where it came from told you almost everything.

Now, what to watch. Four large companies filed results in the last twenty minutes of trading today. Vedanta the miner, Bajaj Finance the lender, Hyundai [SAY: HUN-day] the carmaker, Mankind the drugmaker. Their numbers had no time to trade. Hyundai rose over one percent today and then filed results showing profit down thirty-five percent. Bajaj Finance closed flat, and its profit was up almost twenty-eight percent. Neither close had anything to do with those results. Hyundai moved with a strong auto sector, and Bajaj Finance with lenders being sold ahead of the rate meeting. Tomorrow is when the results get their reaction. The event that matters most from here is the Reserve Bank's rate decision on Wednesday, the fifth of August.
