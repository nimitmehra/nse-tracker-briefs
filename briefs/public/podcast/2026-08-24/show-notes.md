## Episode title

**24 August 2026 | NSE Daily Brief: the deposit window shuts on the 31st, and we had the wrong date**

## Description

The Reserve Bank's concessional FCNR(B) window closes on 31 August, not 30 September. The date was brought forward on 14 August because the response was so large: US$72.85bn mobilised under the facility, US$65.4bn of it in those deposits, per the RBI's 22-August release. Monday left seven days of it. Two details travel with that. The RBI's own FAQ page still displays the retired 30 September date, so a reader who checks the central bank's website gets the wrong answer from the central bank. And this brief carried 30 September for several runs before catching it. Elsewhere: a flat Nifty over a busy session, and why we refuse the Hormuz explanation everyone else ran with.

## Key points

- **FCNR(B) window closes 31 August, not 30 September.** Advanced on 14 August. US$72.85bn mobilised under the facility, US$65.4bn of it in the deposits. Swaps against eligible deposits stay available to 11 September; the overseas-borrowing leg runs to 31 December.
- **Two corrections in one story.** The RBI's own FAQ page still shows the retired 30 September date alongside inflow figures as of 31 July. This brief also carried 30 September for several runs and never went back to check it. It is retired.
- **The index was not the session.** Nifty 50 closed 24,219.05, −0.14%, but 302 stocks rose against 400 that fell out of 705 priced — a second straight session of negative breadth under a flat headline.
- **We do not adopt "Indian shares fell on Hormuz fears."** Brent fell 1.81% on the same escalation, Indian refiners rose on the cheaper barrel (Indian Oil +1.91%), and the US sanctions announcement much of the coverage attached to Monday landed after India's 15:30 close.
- **Movers, with their limits stated.** Ratnamani Metals & Tubes +14.55% on a subsidiary's US$286mn export orders filed to the exchange in-session, with the buyer unnamed and the manufactured-versus-traded split undisclosed. BLS International −10.98% on reports of a widened Spanish investigation; we have not read the court's order, every account traces back to a single report, and the company filed a categorical denial while the market was open. Five of the twelve biggest movers, IDBI Bank +6.92% loudest among them, have no cause anyone can point to.
