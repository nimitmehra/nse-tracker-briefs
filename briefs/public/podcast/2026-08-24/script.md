Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Monday's session.

The Reserve Bank of India has a window open for dollar deposits from Indians living abroad. It closes on the thirty first of August, not the thirtieth of September, which left seven days on Monday. The bank moved the date forward on the fourteenth because the response was so large. Its own release on Saturday puts the money raised at seventy two point eight five billion dollars, sixty five point four billion of it in those deposits.

Two things about that date, and the second one is ours. The central bank's own list of frequently asked questions still shows the retired thirtieth of September, so a reader who checks the central bank's website gets the wrong answer from the central bank. And we got it wrong too. This brief carried the thirtieth of September for several runs and never went back to check it. It is retired now.

The Nifty fifty closed Monday at twenty four thousand two hundred and nineteen, down zero point one four percent. Underneath it, three hundred and two shares rose and four hundred fell, out of seven hundred and five we could price, a second straight session of more fallers than risers under a flat index. We cannot rank Monday's sectors, because most of the sector indices gave us nothing usable.

Asia sold technology on Monday. Korea sold off hard and India barely moved, which runs backwards to the pattern of money leaving India for the artificial intelligence trade in North Asia.

Two things on the economy. Foreign exchange reserves rose almost ten billion dollars in the week to the fourteenth of August, a third straight build, but a build funded by that window's dollars stops when the window stops. And the Reserve Bank's house price index, published Monday, has annual growth down to three point six percent from four point five, below its own path for inflation, so house prices are falling in real money.

Now the movers. The biggest gain was Ratnamani Metals and Tubes, up fourteen point five five percent. A subsidiary won export orders worth about two thousand seven hundred crore rupees, filed while the market was open. But the filing names the buyer only as international customers, and part of the work will be sub-contracted and traded on rather than made in house.

The biggest fall was B-L-S International, down almost eleven percent, and here the limits matter more than the number. B-L-S holds outsourced visa processing contracts from governments. Reports say a Spanish court has widened an investigation into an alleged visa fraud network at the Spanish consulate in Algiers, to look at the company's own document handling role. We have not read the court's order, and every account traces back to a single report, so three outlets repeating one claim is one source. That is enough to say the reports caused the fall. It is not enough to say what the company is accused of, or whether it is accused of anything at all. B-L-S rejected the allegations in a filing made while the market was open.

Five of the twelve biggest movers have no cause anyone can point to. The loudest is I-D-B-I Bank, up six point nine two percent on the day state owned banks led the market lower. Nothing was filed. Nobody knows, and that is the accurate answer.

Here is what struck me about Monday. Much of the coverage said Indian shares fell on fear about the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow lane that carries much of the world's oil. Iran had threatened to seize ships crossing it. We do not accept that explanation, and the oil market is the reason. A real threat to that chokepoint does not send crude down, and Brent fell one point eight one percent on the same escalation. Indian refiners rose on the cheaper barrel, with Indian Oil up one point nine one percent. And the American sanctions announcement the coverage attached to Monday landed after our market had shut. A fall that small does not need a geopolitical cause, and we are not giving it one.

The nearest date is Tuesday's monthly derivatives expiry. But the one this episode is about is Monday the thirty first, when that deposit window shuts.
