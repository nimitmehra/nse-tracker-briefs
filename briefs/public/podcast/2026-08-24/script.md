Good evening. This is India Markets Brief from toro I-Q. Your read on Monday's session.

The Reserve Bank of India has a special window open for dollar deposits from Indians living abroad. It closes on the thirty first of August, not the thirtieth of September. As of Monday, that leaves seven days.

The bank brought the date forward on the fourteenth of August, because the response had been so large. Its own release on Saturday the twenty second puts the money raised under the facility at seventy two point eight five billion dollars, of which sixty five point four billion is those deposits. This is the plumbing sitting under three straight weekly builds in India's foreign exchange reserves, and it now has an expiry date on it.

Two things about that date, and the second one is ours. The central bank's own list of frequently asked questions still shows the retired thirtieth of September. So a reader who goes to the central bank's website to check this gets the wrong answer from the central bank. And we got it wrong too. This brief carried the thirtieth of September for several runs and never went back to check it. The date moved on the fourteenth of August, the press carried it that day, and we missed it. It is retired now.

Here is where the market closed on Monday. The Nifty fifty finished at twenty four thousand two hundred and nineteen, down zero point one four percent. Underneath that, three hundred and two shares rose and four hundred fell, out of seven hundred and five we could price. That is the second straight session where more shares fell than rose under a flat index. We are not ranking the sectors, because nine of the eleven sector indices returned nothing usable on our own data. The one sector reading we will publish comes from the press. State owned banks were the weakest corner, down nearly one percent.

Asia sold technology on Monday. Korea sold off hard and India barely moved. For weeks now, money has been leaving India for the artificial intelligence trade in North Asia, and on Monday that ran backwards.

Two things on the economy. On the supportive side, foreign exchange reserves rose almost ten billion dollars in the week to the fourteenth of August, a third straight build, so the line that the Reserve Bank is burning reserves has now failed three weeks running. But one caution travels with it. A build funded by swapping in the same dollars that window is bringing in stops when the window stops.

On the worrying side, and this one is domestic, the Reserve Bank published its house price index for the June quarter on Monday. Annual growth slowed to three point six percent from four point five, which is below the bank's own projected path for inflation. In plain money, house prices across the eighteen cities it tracks are falling.

Now the movers. The biggest gain was Ratnamani Metals and Tubes, up fourteen point five five percent. A subsidiary won export orders for spools and hangers worth two hundred and eighty six million dollars, about two thousand seven hundred crore rupees, filed to the exchange in the afternoon while the market was still open. Two qualifications come from the filing itself. The buyer is described only as international customers and is not named, and part of the work will be sub-contracted and traded on rather than made in house, with the split not disclosed.

The biggest fall was B-L-S International, down almost eleven percent, and here the limits matter more than the number. B-L-S holds outsourced visa processing contracts from governments. Reports say a Spanish court has widened an investigation into an alleged visa fraud network at the Spanish consulate in Algiers, to look at the company's own document handling role. We have not read the court's order. Every account traces back to a single report, so three outlets repeating one claim is one source. That is enough to say the reports caused the fall. It is not enough to say what the company is accused of, or whether it is accused of anything at all. B-L-S filed a statement while the market was still open, categorically rejecting the allegations, saying its role is strictly administrative and that visa decisions rest with the diplomatic authorities.

One reversal. Aegis Logistics fell five point three nine percent, after rising six point one one percent on Friday, a session this podcast did not cover. Across the two together it is up zero point three nine percent, which is a round trip rather than a break, in a share up almost eighty nine percent over three months. Nothing established behind either leg.

And five of the twelve biggest movers have no cause anyone can point to. The loudest is I-D-B-I Bank, up six point nine two percent on the day its own sector led the market lower, which is a gap of about eight percentage points. Nothing was filed. Nobody knows, and that is the accurate answer.

Here is what struck me about Monday. Much of the coverage said Indian shares fell on fear about the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow lane that carries much of the world's oil, after Iran threatened to fine, seize or confiscate ships crossing it. We do not accept that explanation, and the oil market is the reason. A real threat to the world's most important oil chokepoint does not send crude down, and Brent fell one point eight one percent on that same escalation. Indian refiners went the other way and rose on the cheaper barrel, with Indian Oil up one point nine one percent. And the American sanctions announcement that much of the coverage attached to Monday landed after our market had already shut. A fall that small does not need a geopolitical cause, and we are not giving it one.

The nearest date on the calendar is Tuesday's monthly derivatives expiry. But the one this episode is about is Monday the thirty first, when that deposit window shuts.
