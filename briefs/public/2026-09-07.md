## The Lead

Brent crude is reported to have settled around US$97 on Monday, roughly 9% above where it sat on 28 August, after the largest exchange of strikes on tankers yet in the US–Iran war ran over the weekend while India was shut. India imports nearly all the oil it burns and has no say in that price. What moved Indian share prices, however, was a different foreign event: Friday's American jobs number, released after India had closed, pushed up the odds of a US rate rise this month. Nifty IT fell 2.28%, and the link between the two is **reported** rather than confirmed — it is the read every carrier ran. The Nifty 50 fell 0.50% to 23,779.15, and both foreign and domestic institutions were small net buyers anyway.

## Macro & Policy

*This channel last published on 28 August, so two of the three items below are ten-day catch-up rather than Monday's news, and they are labelled as such.*

**On the supportive side, the growth number landed at the top of its range and the external position looks stronger than anyone here guessed.** India's June-quarter GDP, published on 31 August, grew **7.8%** against a year earlier, with value added up 8.2%, manufacturing 9.2% and services 10.0%. The 6.9%-to-8.0% forecast spread we flagged on 28 August resolved at the optimistic end. *(Confirmed at the statistics ministry's own release, plus two independent outlets.)* Separately, foreign exchange reserves hit an all-time **US$740.803 billion** on 4 September, a fifth straight weekly build. **But that is not a pile of money India owns outright.** The Reserve Bank has now disclosed US$136.38 billion mobilised under a special dollar-rupee swap window, which is roughly 18% of the total and carries an obligation to hand the dollars back. The record and the swap are the same fact.

**On the worrying side, the price of oil, and what a foreign central bank does about jobs.** Brent has travelled from about US$89 on 28 August to about US$97, because the war in the Strait of Hormuz turned physical again over the weekend. The United States struck three Iranian crude tankers and destroyed one; Iran says it hit three US-linked ships and fired a ballistic missile at American warships. Tanker traffic through the Strait is at its lowest since May. *(Confirmed across five independent outlets.)* Ten days ago this desk read Iran as an economic confrontation rather than a supply threat. **That read is dead and we are saying so rather than quietly dropping it.** We are not computing what this does to inflation or the current account; the arithmetic is not ours to invent.

**And a jobs number whose headline is stronger than its contents.** American payrolls rose 162,000 in August against a consensus near 53,000, which is what repriced the Fed. Look inside it, though, and the gains sit in restaurants and local-school-district hiring, the information sector *lost* jobs, July was revised down to about 21,000, and wages are up 3.1% on the year. **That is not a wage-inflation print.** The defensible sentence is that the headline was big enough to move rate expectations, whatever its composition says. Where those expectations now sit: **somewhere between about 52% and 65% odds of a 25 basis-point rate rise on 15–16 September**, depending on which venue you read. **We will not print a single number for a coin flip.**

**At home, the Reserve Bank kept draining cash and kept the repo rate at 5.25%.** On Monday it ran a **30-day** auction to absorb surplus bank liquidity, the longest tenor in a sequence that started at two days in late August. **This is not tightening.** These auctions mop up money the banking system already has spare; the policy rate has not moved and the next rate meeting is 5–7 October. The tenor has been volatile, but the outer edge of it is lengthening, and India's 10-year government bond yield sitting around 6.95% is consistent with that. *(Yield is a level only, single-sourced from outside our pipeline, whose own bond feed has now failed for 29 straight sessions.)*

## Regulatory

- **The way the trading day opens changed on Monday morning.** Under a market-regulator circular from January, the pre-open session now ends at a random moment between 09:08 and 09:10 instead of a fixed 09:08, and from 09:05 only limit orders are accepted, with market orders rejected. This is the second half of the same reform that changed how the *close* is formed in August. **Making the exact moment unpredictable makes the opening price harder to push around.** *(Confirmed at the regulator's own circular.)* **No part of Monday's move is attributed to it here** — a first-day plumbing change is exactly the kind of thing a market report invents a cause out of.
- **The regulator has also said it will re-examine how derivative contracts are settled, and that follows directly from the change above.** Futures and options settle against a closing price in the cash market; the closing price is now formed by an auction rather than the old averaging window, so the settlement rule needs looking at again. *(Confirmed — press release dated 3 September.)* **There is no announced scope and no timeline, and we are not inventing either.**
- **The Reserve Bank fined three credit bureaus on 4 September, and the money is not the point.** TransUnion CIBIL, CRIF High Mark and Equifax were penalised a combined ₹34.9 lakh for failing to pay complainants compensation inside the prescribed window. *(Confirmed that the orders exist at the central bank's own release page; the amounts are reported by one specialist outlet.)* **Trivial sums, but enforcement has moved from lenders to the companies that hold your credit record, and it landed on three of them at once.**

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**One American jobs number pushed money in opposite directions across two Asian technology trades, because they are not the same trade.** Korea's chipmakers rose 4.61% as an index and Japan's Nikkei 2.07%, on the same Monday Indian software fell 2.28%. The reason is what each side sells. **North Asia sells the physical equipment for artificial-intelligence buildouts, and a hotter American economy does not hurt that order book.** *(The catalyst the chip press names is an OpenAI model unveiling on 3 September; that attribution is reported by one specialist outlet, so treat it lightly.)* **Indian software sells discretionary project work to American corporate clients, and those clients' budgets are the first thing squeezed when US borrowing costs are expected to rise.** So this is not "emerging markets sold off" and it is not "global tech sold off" — both are false, because Asian tech rose hard and emerging-market equity was bid (Friday's stamp). **Indian IT fell alone.** What to watch: whether Nifty IT keeps falling into Wednesday, which would tell you this is a budget story rather than a one-day reaction.

**A shooting war in a shipping lane is the bigger economic fact, and it left almost no fingerprint on Indian shares.** Brent is reported up about 9% in seven sessions on the Hormuz escalation, and India buys nearly all its oil. Yet the state refiners moved in the right direction and barely moved at all: Bharat Petroleum (BPCL) −0.89%, Indian Oil (IOC) −1.78%. **That is a shallow reaction to a 9% run in the thing they buy, and we could not source an explanation for how shallow it was.** What it means practically is that the oil leg has no same-day sector reading behind it while the Fed leg does. **Oil will show up in the September inflation print released in mid-October, not in Monday's chart** — which is exactly why it is the more durable of the day's two stories and the less visible one.

**Two more things did not move that should have, and non-moves are often the better read.** IndiGo (INDIGO) closed **up 0.48%** on a day crude carried a war premium, which is the opposite of what fuel-cost arithmetic says for an airline. And the gold lenders **fell on a day gold rose 1.06%**: Muthoot Finance (MUTHOOTFIN) −1.16%, Manappuram Finance (MANAPPURAM) −4.15%. Their loan books are secured against gold, so a higher gold price normally raises what they can lend against. **No source we found explains either one**, and we are surfacing them as open questions rather than fitting a story to them.

**And the rupee absorbed both shocks without moving, which is the quietest and possibly the most important line in this brief.** A crude spike and a hawkish repricing of American rates landed on the same session, and USDINR moved 0.01%. **The partial explanation is that the dollar itself was soft** — the dollar index fell 0.23% — so there was no dollar-side pressure to pass on. That is genuinely partial. India's reserve position is now backstopped by a US$136 billion swap window, and how that interacts with a rupee under real pressure is untested. **The test, if it comes, is the mid-September inflation print into an early-October rate meeting.**

## What to Watch Tomorrow

- **Tuesday, American markets reopen after a three-day weekend.** This is only the second US cash session that can price Friday's jobs number, and the first one India gets to trade against. Every US figure in this brief is Friday's and goes stale at 19:00 India time.
- **Tuesday, the weekly Nifty settlement.** Index weekly expiry moved to Tuesdays a year ago, so expect the intraday positioning that comes with it. **Monday's move had no expiry in it at all**, which is worth holding on to when you read anyone attributing it to one.
- **Tuesday, the Reserve Bank runs an overnight cash-absorption auction and a government bond auction.** **Watch the tenor rather than the amount:** a return to overnight the day after a 30-day auction is what would tell you the 30-day was a one-off rather than a step.
- **Friday 11 September, weekly central-bank statistics at about 17:00.** This is the first such release after the special foreign-deposit window closed on 31 August. **If the reserve builds stop or slow sharply from here, the swap-facility explanation for the last five of them is confirmed; if a sixth large build arrives anyway, something else is running.**
- **Mid-September, India's August consumer price inflation.** The convention points at Monday 14 September because the usual date falls on a Saturday, and we are not publishing that as a hard date until the ministry's own calendar confirms it. **This print cannot carry crude going from US$89 to US$97 — an August number measures August prices, and that leg came in September**; the first print that can carry it is September's, released in mid-October. What the August number does do is land into a central bank that has just extended its cash-draining out to 30 days.
- **Tuesday 15 to Wednesday 16 September, the US rate decision**, announced about 23:30 India time on the Wednesday. **Odds of a rise sit somewhere between 52% and 65% depending on venue, and we will keep publishing the range.** The first Indian session that can price it is Thursday 17 September.
- **Undated and most likely to overwrite all of the above: the Strait of Hormuz.** The two sides struck each other on consecutive weekend days. Iran has said a shipping arrangement with Oman is "days away" — that is Iran's claim about a future deal, and we are not treating it as a fact.

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own release pages opened directly, end-of-day data from our own feeds, and established Indian and international financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim. **Where a cause could not be established, the entry says so, and on Monday that was ten of the eleven names on the movers list.** One was sourced to primary documents.

**The timing rule, which decided the whole brief.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35, so nothing published later can explain that day's close. The American jobs number landed at 18:00 on Friday and the tanker exchange ran across Saturday and Sunday, which is why Monday was the first session that could price any of it. **Three of the four things that set this session happened when India was shut.**

**What we are deliberately not publishing.** No sector move other than Nifty IT and Nifty Pharma — fourteen of sixteen sector fetches failed. No daily change on Brent from our own file, which printed 0.00% on five separate energy contracts at the same timestamp; the ~US$97 level and the ~1.7% session move are web-reported and labelled as such, and we have shown our own figure alongside rather than quietly replacing it. No single probability for the American rate decision. No daily change or reason for India's 10-year bond yield, only the level. No US two-year yield, because our file's label for it points at the wrong instrument. No figure from the central bank's balance-of-payments release, whose existence we confirmed and whose contents we did not open. And no inflation or current-account arithmetic off the oil move.

**Dates and provenance on the borrowed numbers.** Institutional flow figures are Monday's, from two aggregators rather than the depository's own page, which failed on us — and they are not the figures sitting in our own pipeline file, which still carries Friday's. Korea's and Japan's index closes are press-sourced, because our global file returned Tuesday's stamps on every Asian index and every currency. **Where our own end-of-day file and the day's headlines disagreed on a closing price, we used our file and said so** — Infosys is the case in point, and Monday's press carried a different figure for it than our data does. Growth is the June quarter; reserves cover the week to 28 August; the swap-facility total is as of 31 August.
