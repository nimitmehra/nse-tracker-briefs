## The Lead

The Nifty 50 closed at a five-month low on Tuesday, down 1.19% at 23,118.60, after a holiday weekend in which oil, American interest rates and the cost of money at home all turned against India at once. Brent traded above US$107 after a drone strike shut Saudi Arabia's main pipeline around the Strait of Hormuz, the US 10-year Treasury yield touched 5% on Monday, and the Reserve Bank starts selling ₹1 trillion of bonds on Thursday to drain surplus cash. India imports most of its oil, so the rupee fell to a seven-week low, the 10-year government bond yield rose to about 7.1%, and foreign investors sold a net ₹2,977.90 crore, a fifth straight day of selling. Mid- and small-caps fell twice as hard as the index; IT was the only sector that rose.

## Global Setup

**The overnight input for Tuesday was Monday's American bond market, not Monday's American stock market.** The US 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.01% during Monday's session and closed near 4.95%, after Friday's US inflation report (prices up 0.4% on the month, mostly petrol) pushed the odds of a Fed rate rise this Wednesday to about 92% on the CME's tracker. A 5% risk-free dollar yield is the number that makes foreign investors sell emerging-market shares, and Tuesday's ₹2,977.90 crore of foreign selling is what that looks like in India. Oil went the same way: Brent settled about US$105 on Monday, from US$104.61 on Friday, and traded above US$107 while India was open, a four-month high, after Houthi strikes on Saudi territory added to the pipeline shutdown.

The dollar index rose about half a percent to 99.6 and gold fell about 1.8% to US$4,330, so gold did not work as the safe asset on the day; the dollar did. Asia, which trades alongside India, was mixed rather than crushed: Japan's Nikkei rose 0.2–0.5%, Korea's KOSPI fell 0.8%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.4–0.6% and Australia's ASX 200 fell 0.9%. **India fell more than every one of them, and the difference is India's own: a rupee at a seven-week low on an oil bill it cannot avoid, a bond yield at a four-month high, and a foreign seller.**

## Macro & Policy

*This channel last published on 7 September, so some of what follows landed between then and the holiday. Each item carries its date.*

**On the supportive side, the inflation number did not surprise and the cash cushion at home is real.** India's August consumer price inflation came in at 4.82% on Monday, released while the market was shut. That is the tenth monthly rise in a row and the highest since December 2024, but it matched forecasts and sits inside the Reserve Bank's 2–6% band, so the number itself did not move Tuesday's market. *(Confirmed at the statistics ministry's release plus two outlets.)* Foreign exchange reserves jumped a record US$44.9 billion in the week to 4 September, to US$785.7 billion. **That is the special dollar-deposit swap window settling onto the central bank's books, not money India owns outright**; the Reserve Bank has to hand those dollars back later. And domestic institutions kept buying: ₹2,686.00 crore on Tuesday, against ₹2,977.90 crore of foreign selling.

**On the worrying side, three things at once.** First, oil: Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline, the route carrying 4–5 million barrels a day around the closed Strait of Hormuz, has been shut since a drone strike on Thursday 10 September, with no restart date given. Brent traded above US$107 on Tuesday. Second, American rates: the US 10-year yield touched 5% on Monday and a Fed rise on Wednesday is priced at about 92%.

**Third, and this one is Indian: the Reserve Bank announced on 11 September that it will sell ₹1 trillion of government bonds outright, ₹50,000 crore on Thursday and ₹25,000 crore each on 21 and 28 September.** The banking system is sitting on roughly ₹10.5 trillion of surplus cash created by that same swap window, and overnight rates had slipped below the 5.25% policy rate. Selling bonds soaks the cash up. **This is liquidity management, not a rate rise**; the repo rate is unchanged. But it means more bond supply, and the 10-year yield has risen to about 7.1%, a four-month high, up roughly a quarter-point in three weeks.

**The number nobody is talking about is the gap between factory-gate and shop-shelf prices.** August wholesale inflation was 9.92%, with fuel and power up 22.93% on the year, against consumer inflation of 4.82%. A five-point gap between what producers pay and what they can charge is the widest of this cycle, and it lands on the margins of every input-heavy manufacturer. Also dated in the window: the GST Council meeting was moved from 12 September to 7 October because of the BRICS summit in Delhi; the date change is the only publishable fact.

**What to watch:** the Fed decision on Wednesday at about 23:30 India time, the first ₹50,000 crore bond sale on Thursday, and the Reserve Bank's own rate meeting on 5–7 October. That meeting now arrives with rising consumer inflation, double-digit wholesale inflation, a ₹1 trillion bond-sale programme in progress and, if the Fed moves, a higher American policy rate. **No Reserve Bank official has said the word "hike", and we are not saying it for them.**

## The Market

**The market opened up and closed at the low, which is the shape of a session absorbing bad news it could not trade on Monday.** The Nifty 50 opened 0.76% higher on an IT bid, then fell all day to 23,118.60, down 1.19%, its lowest close in five months. Breadth was worse than the headline: Nifty Midcap 50 fell 2.08%, Nifty Smallcap 250 fell 2.44% and the Nifty 500 fell 1.70%. The stocks that had run hardest through July and August fell hardest: the exchange's Momentum-Quality 50 index dropped 3.53%, twice the broad market. Tuesday was also the weekly Nifty options expiry, which adds positioning noise to a gap-and-reverse day; that is structure, not a cause.

**One sector rose and every other sector index we can read fell.** Nifty IT closed +2.19% at 29,555.30 after being up 5.2% at its morning high: Infosys (INFY) +3.79%, HCL Technologies (HCLTECH) +3.95%, Mphasis (MPHASIS) +3.62%, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) +2.28%. A weaker rupee helps IT too (they earn dollars and pay salaries in rupees), but a 0.26% rupee move does not buy a 5% open; the AI story does. **That more than half the gain was gone by the close tells you the index selling swallowed it.** Coforge (COFORGE) fell 3.85% against its sector after its nomination-committee chairman resigned on 11 September.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**A record reserves number and a five-month low in property shares are the same story, and no wrap we read joined them.** Banks raised about US$136 billion, mostly foreign-currency deposits from Indians abroad, and swapped the dollars with the Reserve Bank under a window that closed at the end of August. The dollars showed up as a record US$44.9 billion weekly jump in reserves; the rupees paid out for them showed up as a ₹10.5 trillion cash surplus in the banking system. The Reserve Bank's answer is to sell ₹1 trillion of bonds and take the rupees back, and more bond supply means a higher bond yield, now about 7.1%. **A higher risk-free rupee yield is what sold realty (−4.04%), lenders (Shriram Finance −4.74%) and every other business that is really a bet on the cost of money.** What to watch: whether Thursday's ₹50,000 crore auction clears with cut-off yields above the market, which would say buyers want a premium to absorb the supply.

**The refiners that were supposed to win from expensive oil lost more than the refiners that were supposed to lose, and nobody can explain it.** A week ago, Chennai Petroleum (CHENNPETRO) and Mangalore Refinery (MRPL) hit records on the logic that standalone refiners keep the fat diesel margins a supply shock creates. On Tuesday, with Brent at a four-month high, they fell 6.43% and 5.15%, while the state fuel retailers whose pump prices are politically capped fell only about 2%, and ONGC, the producer that sells crude at the world price, rose 1.48%. **Two refiners falling together says the cause is group-level, but no windfall-tax notice, margin print or duty change dated to Tuesday exists.** The likeliest reading is that a quarter of 30%-plus gains had filled those stocks with holders who leave first on a bad day. It is a reading. What to watch: the next fortnightly windfall-levy notification.

**Indian IT rose on a "slower AI" story while AI shares fell abroad, and both moves are the same idea read from opposite ends.** Anthropic's chief executive called for a slower pace of frontier-AI development over the weekend, reportedly endorsed by the heads of OpenAI and xAI; the primary was not opened, so the catalyst is reported. AI-linked shares fell in the US on Monday and in Asia on Tuesday, because slower AI is bad for the companies selling it. Indian IT rose 5% at the open, because slower AI gives services firms more time to reposition before clients' budgets move. **The stable fact across three weeks of this channel is that Indian IT trades the AI story as a threat: "AI slows" is a bid, "AI accelerates" is an offer.** A weaker rupee helped at the margin, but 0.26% of currency does not explain a 5% open. What to watch: whether the 2.19% that survived the close holds on Wednesday without the story.

**Gold fell on a day the world was scared, and the gold lenders fell with it.** Muthoot Finance (MUTHOOTFIN) dropped 2.50% to a 52-week low. The reason gold did not work as the hedge is that a 5% dollar yield makes cash in dollars the safe asset; the dollar index rose 0.51% and gold fell 1.79%. Gold lenders lend against the metal's price, so a falling gold price shrinks what they can lend and raises the risk on loans already made. **This cuts the other way if Wednesday's Fed decision disappoints the dollar.** Two-sided, and stated as such.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own release pages opened directly, the exchange's Tuesday bulk-deal file, end-of-day data from our own feeds, and established Indian and international financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim. **Where a cause could not be established, the entry says so, and on Tuesday that was nine of the seventeen names on the movers list.** Six were sourced to primary documents; two moved with their sector and no company event is claimed.
