## Macro & Policy

**A correction to what we told you on Monday, first, because it was the wrong way round.** That brief said the Reserve Bank was "draining cash for longer" and that its absorption of surplus rupees was being "termed out rather than simply rolled." Three business days later the ladder has come back down. Read directly off the central bank's own press-release index, the sequence of its reverse repo auctions runs overnight, three-day, seven-day, then two-day on 25 August, and then back to overnight, announced on the 25th and held on the 27th. The tenor reversed. We are not dropping that line quietly and we are not repeating it. What we still cannot tell you is how much is being absorbed, because we did not open the individual result pages. And for the record, since a stale figure circulates in our own files: the repo rate is **5.25%**, the standing deposit facility 5.00% and the marginal standing facility 5.50%. The August decision was unanimous. The next rate meeting is 5 to 7 October.

**The supportive side, and it is the document the reader missed.** The Reserve Bank's August Bulletin came out on Tuesday 25 August, inside the two sessions this brief did not cover. Its reading of the economy is that domestic activity stayed resilient through the June quarter despite global uncertainty, with manufacturing and services both expanding and both merchandise exports and imports growing in double digits in July. That last point cuts against the idea that the 18% American tariff is crushing Indian exports. We use the Bulletin's data and not its adjectives.

**The worrying side, and it contradicts the Bulletin on the one thing that matters most.** The weather office's cumulative rainfall through 26 August is 577.8 mm against a normal of 664.7 mm — 13% below par, and wider than the roughly 11% shortfall in early August. Fourteen states and union territories are deficient, 310 districts are classed deficient and 38 large-deficient. East and northeast India are 27% short. The forecast is for below-normal rain through August and September on a strengthening El Niño.

**Both readings are true, and quoting either one alone will mislead you.** The Bulletin's line is that July's recovery brought kharif sowing "closer to normal acreage, partly mitigating risks to agriculture." That describes July. The deficit then widened through August, which is what the weather office data shows and what the American agriculture department's own August India report says in its title: monsoon recovery fails to boost sluggish crop planting. The Bulletin is not wrong; it is a month behind the number.

**What to watch, and it is four days away.** India's June-quarter national accounts land on Monday 31 August. The median of a 58-economist survey is about 7.1%, in a range of roughly 7.0% to 7.2%, against 7.8% the quarter before — a four-quarter low, which the surveyed economists put down to the late monsoon and softer services. One trap sits inside it: the same release switches the industrial-production and price indices to a revised 2022-23 base year, so some series will not be directly comparable with what came before. Separately, a ₹34,000 crore government bond sale prices tomorrow, into a yield curve this desk still cannot see.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**India sat out the biggest company result in the world, and Korea and Taiwan did not.** Nvidia's numbers were public before India's open, and they were enormous. Indian software services fell anyway — Nifty IT down 0.32%, with 13 of 20 constituents lower, Tata Consultancy Services down 0.95% and Infosys down 0.82%. Korea rose 1.53%, Shanghai 1.13%, Taiwan 0.31%. **The honest description is that the link between an American chip result and Indian shares is one-sided rather than absent.** Korea and Taiwan build the memory and the chips a higher graphics-processor forecast mechanically orders more of; Indian technology companies sell people's time, and no order for chips converts into an Indian services contract. **That is our reasoning and not a sourced finding**, and it is offered to explain why the non-reaction is unsurprising rather than to claim it as the cause. One more caution before anyone builds a theory on it: this same pattern ran backwards on Monday, when Korea fell 2.49% and India barely moved. Twice inverted in three sessions is something to describe, not a trend.

**The central bank and the weather office are describing two different monsoons, and Monday will pick one.** The Bulletin's July view is of a recovery that pulled sowing back toward normal. The rainfall data through 26 August is 13% short and widening. Thursday's tape sided with the second: the agrochemical and fertiliser makers fell — UPL down 1.90%, Rallis India 1.72%, PI Industries 1.65%, Coromandel International 1.09% — and so did the rural-facing rest, with Escorts Kubota down 3.03%, TVS Motor 1.21%, Dabur India 1.53% and Hindustan Unilever 0.59%. Every one of them did worse than or in line with the market. **We record that the direction lines up and we do not claim the cause**, because the rainfall figure is Wednesday's cumulative data rather than a Thursday release. What makes it worth holding is Monday's growth release, whose consensus the surveyed economists themselves attribute to the late monsoon.

**Cheaper crude reached Indian shares in exactly one place, and it was not the obvious one.** Brent is lower than it was at India's close on Monday, through a week in which Iran threatened shipping through the Strait of Hormuz — the second week running that escalation and a falling oil price have pointed opposite ways. The textbook beneficiaries did nothing at all: Bharat Petroleum flat at 0.00%, Indian Oil down 1.37%, GAIL down 1.17%, Asian Paints up 0.17% and IndiGo down 1.17%. **Not one of them delivered the input-cost relief the arithmetic says they should have.**

**Where a commodity did reach equities was gold, and in the opposite direction.** Gold is lower over the window and the gold lenders moved as a group — Manappuram Finance down 4.76%, Muthoot Finance 3.80%, Capri Global Capital 4.51%, IIFL Finance 2.81%. **The mechanism needs no percentage**: these lenders can only advance a capped fraction of the value of the gold pledged with them, so a falling metal price shrinks every new loan and raises the risk on loans already made. That is why the gold trade transmits and the crude trade does not.

**Nine of the ten biggest risers have no explanation, and the missing piece is a data feed rather than effort.** Five full checklists were run and four stale-dated stories were caught and thrown out — a July order, a December-2025 takeover thread, a 21-August rating upgrade and an 18-August results release, every one of which would have read as today's news if nobody had checked the dateline. What is left is a hole with a name: the record of large negotiated trades has been unavailable to us for nine-plus runs, and a mid-sized company moving 5% to 13% with nothing filed is precisely the shape one of those makes. "Nobody established why" is the accurate answer, and it is worth more than a plausible one.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own press-release pages opened directly, end-of-day data from two independent market feeds, and established Indian and international financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim. Where a cause could not be established, the entry says so.

**The timing rule, stated once because it decided the day's one sourced story.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35, so nothing published later can explain that day's close — but it works in reverse too. Bombay Burmah's disclosure went out at 15:59:29 on Wednesday, after that session had settled and before Thursday opened, which is exactly why a court order dated 19 August is Thursday's cause and not last week's.

No India government bond yield, for a twenty-fourth consecutive run, which also means we cannot check any yield-based explanation the press offers. No official sector ranking, no pre-market GIFT Nifty level, and no 52-week superlative on the volatility index, each for the reason given where it appears above.
