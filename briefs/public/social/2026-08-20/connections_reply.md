A few cross-currents from Thursday worth thinking about.

- Two sets of central-bank minutes were published seven hours apart on Wednesday and both are arguing about rate rises, the Reserve Bank's about 17:00 India time, in which Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta records that no further easing is possible and that a case for an increase may emerge in 2026-27, with inflation peaking at 5.9% in the October to December quarter, though the August decision itself left the rate at 5.25% and was unanimous.

- The US Federal Reserve's July minutes followed about 23:30 and record a 9 to 3 decision to leave rates unchanged, in which three regional presidents dissented in favour of a higher rate, the most divided vote there in years, and nobody has set the two documents side by side.

- Thursday's relief came from a debt-management operation rather than from any change in the rate path, so for anyone who owns assets priced off a discount rate, from lenders to expensive growth shares, both curves are now debating the same direction and it is not downwards, which is what the Reserve Bank's 5 to 7 October meeting will test.
