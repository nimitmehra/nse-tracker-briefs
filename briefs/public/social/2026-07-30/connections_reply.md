A few cross-currents from Thursday worth thinking about.

Rate pain arrives in a fixed order, and on Thursday you could watch it move through the queue. Property developers were sold first and were the worst group of the session. Insurers and non-bank lenders went next. The banks barely moved, and some closed higher.

The reason is how quickly each business feels a rate change. A developer's project maths and an insurer's long-dated promises reprice the moment the expected rate path shifts, while a bank's lending margin takes quarters to feel it.

So if next Wednesday's decision leans towards higher rates for longer, that is the order it spreads in, and a slow reaction in banks is not evidence they escaped it. One insurer went the other way anyway, after earning its profit from insurance itself rather than from its investments, which is what a genuinely stock-specific result looks like.
