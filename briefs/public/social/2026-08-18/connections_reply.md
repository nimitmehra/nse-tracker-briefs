A few cross-currents from Tuesday worth thinking about.

- What factories charge at their own gate rose 8.29% in July, and raw materials 8.52%, while what households pay in the shops rose 4.45%, and that gap of roughly 380 basis points is absorbed by whoever sits in between and cannot reprice, which is packaged goods, chemicals, cement and building products.

- The rupee's main support has a published closing date, since the reserves that rose more than $5 billion in each of the last two weeks to about $707 billion are being built by a Reserve Bank swap window for non-resident deposits that shuts on 30 September.

- Gold is being priced by the American central bank rather than by West Asia, since tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and a weekend of almost no shipping produced a gold move of under 1% while silver and platinum each fell about 1.5%, so the West Asia channel into India runs through crude and freight.
