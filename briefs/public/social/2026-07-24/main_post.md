The 10% US tariff surcharge on Indian exports hit its legal expiry on Friday and lapsed, but brought no relief. The Nifty fell 0.43% to 23,767, a fifth straight down day.

- That is the worst losing streak since early January, though the fall stayed shallow as domestic funds bought ₹5,454 crore against ₹3,893 crore of foreign selling.

- The cliff did not clear, it only reset, with Washington shifting the same duty onto an open-ended footing while the 1 August deadline stays live and the trade deal unsigned at the last 1%.

- Oil was the other weight, with Brent having breached $100 on Thursday on fresh attacks near Saudi Arabia and the Strait of Hormuz, and India imports about 85% of its crude.

- Brent eased about 2.7% only after the Indian close, so that is a possible breather for Monday rather than any help to Friday's session.

- India fell far less than its neighbours, with Taiwan down 2.67% on the same risk-off, but that gap is a flows story and not good news, because only domestic buying cushioned a foreign exit.
