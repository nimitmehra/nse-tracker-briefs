**The FTSE mechanism, in plain words.** An index provider publishes a list of members and weights. Funds that promise to copy the index must hold exactly that list. That buying is a one-time event: it is demand from a rule, not from a view on the company. The exchange's bulk-deal record shows Vanguard buying Avalon (7.02 lakh shares at ₹2,527.69, 16% of the day's volume, on volume twelve times normal), SJS Enterprises, Pine Labs and SKF India at the close; Route Mobile, deleted, fell 5.72%. ## Block 6: the three explainers

### 6a. Reserves, cash, bonds, property

**The four-step chain in full.** Step one: Indian banks raised about US$136 billion over the summer, mostly foreign-currency deposits from Indians living abroad, and swapped those dollars with the Reserve Bank under a special window that closed at the end of August. A swap means the bank hands the Reserve Bank dollars today and gets rupees, with an agreement to reverse the exchange later. Step two: reserves rose a record US$44.9 billion in the week to 4 September to US$785.7 billion; that is the swap settling, and the Reserve Bank has to hand those dollars back, so a record reserves headline is a loan, not strength. Step three: the rupees paid out became a surplus of roughly ₹10.5 trillion in the banking system (about ₹9.85 trillion by Wednesday), which pushed the overnight inter-bank rate below the 5.25% policy rate, the rate at which the Reserve Bank lends to banks and the anchor for every other rate. Money looser in practice than on paper, with consumer inflation up ten months in a row. Step four: on 11 September the Reserve Bank announced ₹1 trillion of outright bond sales, ₹50,000 crore on Thursday 17, then ₹25,000 crore on each of Monday 21 and Monday 28 September, the first large net sale of this kind in two years. Outright means the Reserve Bank keeps the rupees permanently, unlike the reverse-repo auctions where banks park cash for a short period.

**Why the reverse-repo auctions matter to the story.** On Wednesday banks parked ₹2.90 trillion at one auction but a second auction for ₹1 trillion drew only ₹40,302 crore: banks would rather hold the cash than lend it to the central bank at 5.24%. That is why the surplus is sticky and the bond sale is what removes it. This is liquidity management, not a rate rise; the policy rate is unchanged at 5.25%.

**Why a higher yield hurts shares.** More bonds for sale, lower bond prices, higher yields. The ten-year yield went from 7.02% on 11 September to about 7.08% on Tuesday (a four-month high, up roughly a quarter of a percentage point in three weeks), eased to about 7.05% on Wednesday, and sat at about 7.06% on Friday after the first sale cleared. It is the risk-free rupee rate: when it rises, a property developer's future sales are worth less today, a lender's cost of funds rises, and a bank's or insurer's bond holdings lose value. Realty −4.04% Tuesday, +0.99% Wednesday, +1.19% Friday; PSU banks −2.29%, +1.44%, +0.66%. In a bond sale the cut-off is the yield at which the last accepted bid clears; a sale that clears without buyers demanding a higher yield is one the market absorbed comfortably. Two tranches remain.

**The American leg.** A foreign fund can earn about 5% a year in dollars lending to the US government, the highest since 2007. To hold Indian shares it needs more than that after allowing for rupee risk, and the rupee was at a seven-week low. Foreign selling of ₹2,977.86 crore on Tuesday and ₹3,208.76 crore on Thursday is what that looks like. Domestic institutions bought on every session with a figure, ₹7,323.49 crore in total, about 1.3 times foreign selling; on Tuesday they absorbed roughly 90% of what foreigners sold, which is a large part of why a five-month low happened in an orderly way. The Reserve Bank's own rate committee meets 5 to 7 October, announcing on the 7th; the GST Council, moved from 12 September because of the BRICS summit, meets the same day. Inputs going in: CPI 4.82%, WPI 9.92%, a bond-sale programme in progress, a higher American rate. Nobody at the Reserve Bank has said "hike".

**Likely question: "does this mean rates are going up in India?"** Our brief does not say so and neither do we. What it says is that the policy rate is unchanged, the bond sales are about draining cash, and the October meeting walks in with those inputs. That is a description of the calendar, not a forecast.

**Likely question: "why are reserves falling now if they just hit a record?"** The Reserve Bank's Friday statistics showed US$780.78 billion for the week to 11 September, down US$4.93 billion from the record. Our Tuesday public daily had set a test in advance: a fall of US$5 billion or more would be the first sign of genuine rupee defence that the deposit inflows can no longer hide. The fall came US$70 million short, the split between currency sales and gold revaluation was not published, and we do not call it intervention. Next Friday's figure, covering the week to 18 September, is on the watch list for that reason.

### 6b. Tata Sons

**Act one, the arithmetic.** Tata Sons is unlisted; nobody can buy its shares. A core investment company is the regulator's label for a company whose business is holding shares in other group companies. Tata Sons had applied to surrender that registration; the Reserve Bank's letter dated 11 September, reported over the weekend, said no and said proceed to a listing. The letter is not public and its reasoning is not on our record. Tata Chemicals owns 2.53% of Tata Sons; at the roughly US$200 billion private valuations cited in the press, that stake alone is worth more than Tata Chemicals' entire market value of ₹18,722 crore. If Tata Sons lists, that holding becomes a visible, sellable asset priced by the market rather than a line in an annual report. Tata Chemicals rose 19.99% on Tuesday and locked at its upper price band (the exchange's daily limit on how far a share may rise; a lock means more buyers than the limit let through). ### 6c. UPI merchant fee

**What was decided.** On Tuesday the steering committee of the National Payments Corporation of India, which runs UPI, decided to levy a merchant discount rate (the fee a shop pays its own bank on a digital payment) of 0.4% on person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000, from 15 October, capped at ₹300 per transaction, with telecom, rail, fuel and insurance payments carrying a flat ₹5 instead. Payments up to ₹2,000 and all person-to-person transfers stay free under a government notification of Monday 14 September, which is the one primary document; the 0.4% figure is reported by several outlets and held as firm until the corporation's own circular is on file.

**Why it matters more than the small numbers suggest.** UPI has never carried a merchant fee. Payments above ₹2,000 were only 4% of transactions by count last year but two thirds of the money moved. Whether shops pass the fee on in prices is not established. Who gets the fee (banks, payment apps, aggregators) is not settled. No listed company has filed anything on it. On Thursday the Finance Ministry called an allegation that the decision came under external influence "patently false", carried by one outlet; the fee is a political object before it is a revenue line.

**Likely question: "which stocks benefit?"** Our brief does not name any and no listed company has filed anything. Do not speculate.

**The screen and the arithmetic.** Companies worth more than ₹1,000 crore that moved more than 6% on the week: 22 names, twelve risers, ten fallers. The vendor holds no Thursday bar for any stock and no Friday bar for several, so for each name the desk divided the week's move by what the three observed days account for, with the remainder assigned to Thursday (or Thursday plus Friday). Six sourced (eMudhra, Yatharth, Tata Chemicals, Avalon, Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri, Solar Industries), four partly (Rossell, Firstsource, Apollo Micro, Godrej Properties), twelve not established.

**eMudhra +24.17%.** The Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation appointed it a validation agent for the Indian body that issues Legal Entity Identifiers, the 20-character corporate identity codes regulators require of large borrowers and derivative users; being a validation agent lets it issue and renew those codes inside its digital-signature business. Filed Saturday 13 September, so Tuesday was the first session to price it: +20.00% to a band lock, +8.34% Wednesday clearing the queue, −3.14% Friday as the unwind.

**Yatharth Hospital +17.92%.** Thursday filing: Advent International will put ₹3,150 crore into the company for 24.87% at ₹985.17 a share, in shares plus warrants (rights to buy shares later at a fixed price), with two board seats. The three observed days account for about 8% of the week's rise; roughly 9 points sit on Thursday; Friday +6.26% to ₹1,139.50, 15.7% above the issue price.
