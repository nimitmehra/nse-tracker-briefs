## 1. The Week in a Line

The week's prices were set by documents that oblige nobody, a regulator's draft and a policy statement, while the one document that obliges everybody, a final and enforceable order, repriced no company at all and instead changed the hours at which every price gets recorded.

## 2. The Themes

Three threads ran the week.

The consequential one was paper. Within five sessions the market got all three rungs of official writing at once, a draft with a comment window still open, a binding rate decision, and a final enforceable order, and it paid the most attention to the least binding of the three.

The second was a green week that was really one morning. The Nifty 50 rose 0.77% to 24,570.65 and the Sensex 0.52%, but Monday's +1.60% was the entire gain and the four sessions after it gave a little back, Friday included at −0.27%. Breadth was healthy at 427 up against 259 down and eight of ten sectors rose, so the softness was in the index rather than the market.

The third was the widest gap in the sector map we have printed. Auto rose 9.88%, the best sector, on July passenger-vehicle wholesales up 33% to a record and a crude price that fell 9.4% in the same week. Realty fell 5.46%, the worst, on DLF's bookings collapsing 94% and Godrej Properties' profit falling 41.6%. Two domestic sectors, the same five days, fifteen percentage points apart.

## 3. Global Setup

Only one global input genuinely reached India this week, and it was oil. Brent ended the week down about 9.4% near $81.65 a barrel. The path was not a line: it fell 7.30% over the first weekend as an OPEC+ agreement to complete the rollback of 2.2 million barrels a day of voluntary cuts landed alongside an American strike on Iran being called off, then spent the week between $79 and $86, at one point moving 9% inside a single day. India buys roughly 85% of the crude it burns, so a fall of that size is money that stays in the country through a smaller import bill and a gentler inflation path.

Everything else global was noise India declined to trade. Korea had four violent sessions, the KOSPI rising 1.62% on a memory-chip partnership and then breaking 4.58% on Thursday after a mid-morning circuit breaker. India closed that same Thursday at +0.05%, which is non-participation rather than strength. On Friday the exchange-traded funds tracking emerging-market shares and bonds all rose while India fell on an Indian regulator's draft, the cleanest evidence that the week's last move was made at home.

The rupee strengthened about 0.5% over the week to 95.198, but it weakened 0.13% on Friday against a dollar that was itself falling 0.54%, and lost ground to the euro and the yen too. When a currency slips while the dollar is being sold, the weakness is its own.

## 4. Macro & Policy

The Reserve Bank held the repo rate at 5.25% on Wednesday, unanimously, a fourth straight hold. This desk did not publish that Wednesday, so we covered it a day late and we are not pretending otherwise. The rate was never the news. The Bank cut its inflation projection for the year to 5.0% from 5.1%, crediting cheaper crude, and cut its core inflation forecast by a much larger 40 basis points to 4.3%. Core strips out food and fuel, so cutting it that hard is the Bank saying it reads current price pressure as a supply problem rather than something spreading through the wider economy. It raised the growth forecast to 6.7% and still sees inflation peaking at 5.9% in the October to December quarter.

Lenders responded to the hold gently and over days, which is what a binding document that surprises nobody does to prices.

One tension went unaddressed. The July services survey landed the same morning at 53.3, a 53-month low, down from 57.4 in June, in the part of the economy producing roughly 55% of India's output. The July factory survey finalised at 53.5, itself revised down and near a five-year low. A growth forecast raised on the morning services momentum printed a four-and-a-half-year low is a contradiction nobody reconciled. Behind it sits weather: August rain is forecast at 94% of the long-period average and below normal for the rest of the season. Rain through July was still 1% above normal, so this is a forecast turning rather than a shortfall that has happened, but a weak second half feeds food prices with a lag into exactly the quarter the Bank has flagged as the peak.

The bond market's answer to a week of falling oil and a held rate is simply not available to us. The India government bond curve failed for a fourth consecutive run and returned no weekly change at any tenor. We are printing no yield number and no direction rather than guessing at one.

## 5. Regulatory

Then came the draft, and the word draft is load-bearing.

On Thursday the Reserve Bank issued a proposed amendment to its directions on credit facilities from non-bank lenders. It would leave those lenders only term loans: a fixed amount, a set repayment schedule, and a limit that cannot be topped back up once repaid. Revolving credit, the kind where you repay and can borrow again up to the same limit, would go. The single exemption covers lenders the Bank authorises to issue credit cards, reportedly two companies, so an exemption that reads wide is narrow in practice. Comments are open until 28 August. None of it is law.

The market priced it as though it were. Bajaj Finance fell 5.84% in a session, Bajaj Finserv 3.70% and Jio Financial 2.39%. Three things are genuinely unsettled and none is priced: whether the ban survives the comment window, whether the card exemption widens, and whether a transition period gets written in.

The final and enforceable document of the week, by contrast, moved one company. SEBI's order on Zee took the stock down 14.33% on Monday. It obliged somebody, and its consequence stopped at that somebody's boundary.

The third regulatory item repriced nobody and changed the clock. Continuous trading in futures-and-options names now ends at 15:15, with the close discovered by auction between 15:15 and 15:35. This was not sprung on anyone: the exchange explained it publicly on 04 August and the press covered it. The narrower and correct complaint is that SEBI has not commented on a change to how India's closing prices, the numbers that settle derivatives and value every mutual fund holding, are formed.

## 6. The Market

The Nifty 50 closed the week at 24,570.65, up 0.77%, with the Sensex up 0.52% and Bank Nifty up 0.84%. Monday's +1.60% was the whole of it. Every session after that leaked a little, Friday included at −0.27% for the Nifty and −0.58% for the Sensex.

The breadth said something different from the index. 427 stocks rose against 259 that fell and eight of ten sectors closed green, so this was not a narrow rally that ran out of buyers. It was a broad, quiet market with a large-cap financial drag laid over the last two sessions.

The sector map was the widest we have printed. Auto led at +9.88%, paid twice, once by July passenger-vehicle wholesales rising 33% to a record and again by a 9.4% fall in crude. Realty was worst at −5.46%, punished by its own two biggest names. Between them sits an awkward blank: Nifty Financial Services returned neither a weekly nor a daily move this week. The sector at the centre of the week's biggest story is the one sector we cannot measure, and we would rather say that than substitute Bank Nifty for it.

## 7. Earnings

Here is the part that sharpens the week's story rather than softening it. On company filings this market was strict, not credulous.

Muthoot Finance fell 7.33% on a quarter in which profit rose 43%, because a gold-loan lender's profit rises mechanically when gold prices rise, and the market wanted to know what happens when they stop. Escorts Kubota's 72% profit "collapse" was read straight past, because the year-ago quarter contained a divestment gain that will never repeat, so the fall was arithmetic rather than deterioration. DLF reported profit up 4.1% and nobody cared, because new bookings had fallen 94%, and for a developer bookings are next year's revenue while profit is last year's. Godrej Properties' profit fell 41.6% in the same sector in the same week.

Firstsource grew revenue 22.9% and carried a ₹72 crore charge. Garware Technical Fibres posted its best quarter ever with profit up 60%, and the share moved −0.96%, which is a market that had already assumed it. Blue Star fell 5.65% on Thursday and 3.63% on Friday to a 52-week low.

So the market read company paper for what it actually says, and official paper for what it might one day become. That is the week in one sentence, and it is not a flattering one.

## 8. Corporate News

Capital actions carried the week. The government opened a two-day sale of up to 6.5% of Life Insurance Corporation at a floor price of ₹382 against Monday's ₹428.50 close, and the stock drifted to ₹391.30 while the book was open, which is the ordinary gravity of a large share sale rather than a verdict on the insurer. At Rossell Techsys the promoter group sold about 4.9% to a mutual fund at roughly ₹900 a share and the stock closed 18.6% above that price, the market reading the buyer rather than the seller. Zee's shareholders had approved ₹3,143 crore of preferential warrants to the promoters at ₹126 a share on 31 July, and after Monday's order the stock sat at ₹98.14, some 22% below that subscription price. A reported ₹5,000 crore fundraise authorisation at Premier Energies rests on a single carrier's report and is carried here as reported rather than confirmed.

## 9. Big Movers

Twenty-two names cleared our size floors this week. Nine have a cause we could source to a document dated inside the week. Thirteen do not, and we are not going to invent one for them.

**Zee, −17.77% on the week**, is the cleanest sourced fall and the week's spine in one stock. SEBI's final order, a two-month bar and ₹1.48 crore of penalties, took it down 14.33% on Monday. A final order is the most binding document a company can receive, and it repriced exactly one company.

**Shivalik Bimetal Controls, +22.88%**, put 87% of that move into Friday alone and closed locked at its upper price limit at a 52-week high, the largest single session in our whole 687-name universe. The sourced half is the quarter: revenue up 33.4% and profit up 44.9%. Out of the same board meeting on the same afternoon came a second disclosure, that the statutory auditor resigned mid-term with immediate effect. The market priced the first and not the second. We assert no cause for the resignation and draw no line between the two. This is a flag on co-occurrence and timing, nothing more.

**MTAR Technologies, +23.74%**, was the week's biggest gainer and we cannot date a cause to any session inside it. Its June quarter was filed on 29 July, before the week began. It climbed in repeated near-limit sessions rather than on one event.

The rest of the sourced list is ordinary and useful. ASK Automotive rose 20.24% on July record volumes and a quarter with revenue up 52.4%, though margin fell 176 basis points and Friday gave 6.46% back unexplained. Ather Energy rose 17.55% on revenue up 89% and its first positive operating profit. Greaves Cotton fell 16.47% on revenue up 31% with operating profit down 1%, which is growth that costs more than it earns. Blue Star fell 9.95% to a 52-week low across Thursday and Friday. Tata Technologies' 16.74% rise stays contested, since the media report behind it drew exchange queries and the company's own reply declines to confirm it.

Among the thirteen unexplained, two clusters are worth naming rather than burying. Sapphire Foods rose 21.13% and Devyani International 18.00%, both hard on Friday with an empty announcement feed. Sanghvi Movers, MapmyIndia, Cempro and Nuvama appear nowhere in the week's briefs at all. Pondy Oxides was searched on two separate days and came back empty both times.

## 10. The Connections

*How the week's cross-currents ran between commodities, sectors and the currency.*

The most repeated wire of the week was the rupee into IT services, and it fired with the wrong sign both times. The textbook mechanism is simple: Indian IT companies earn dollars and pay salaries in rupees, so a weaker rupee pads their margins and a stronger rupee squeezes them. This week the rupee strengthened about 0.5% and IT rose anyway. Monday's brief flagged it at the time rather than after the fact. A wire that fires backwards twice is not a wire, it is two coincidences sharing a name, and the honest reading is that IT was trading its American demand outlook and the currency was noise around it.

Oil paid nobody either. Crude fell about 9.4%, and neither side of the chain priced it. The refiners and marketers that buy crude, and whose costs a cheaper barrel cuts, barely moved. Nor did ONGC, which pumps crude rather than buying it, so a cheaper barrel cuts what it earns and should have hurt. When a 9.4% move in the single most important input to an importing economy leaves both the buyers and the seller untouched, the market was busy with something else. It was. It was reading paper.
