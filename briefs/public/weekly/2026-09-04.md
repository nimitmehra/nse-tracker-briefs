## The week in plain English

The Nifty 50 finished the week at **23,897.70, down 1.15%** from 24,175.65 the Friday before. That is the third losing week in a row and the widest of the three. The Sensex fell 0.97% to 76,515.43, the Nifty 500 fell 1.17%, the Nifty Bank fell 0.22%, and the Nifty Midcap 50 fell 1.51%. The Nifty Smallcap 250 fell 0.08%, which is not a fall at all. India VIX, the market's own estimate of how much the Nifty is likely to move over the next month, started the week at 10.68 and finished at 10.68.

Underneath the index, **258 shares rose and 446 fell** out of the 704 we can price at both ends of the week. About 37 in every 100 finished higher than they started.

**The week's biggest story was a cement company deciding to sell electrical wire.** UltraTech Cement told the exchange at 07:11 on Tuesday 1 September that its ₹1,800 crore wires-and-cables plant at Bharuch had started commercial production, about four months ahead of its own published schedule. It filed again at 18:29 on Thursday evening to launch the brand, Ultravolt, which on day one makes the Aditya Birla group the second-largest wire producer in the country. Both filings landed outside trading hours: the first before Tuesday's open, which Tuesday priced, and the second after Thursday's close, which Friday priced. Over the five days **R R Kabel fell 15.53%, KEI Industries 12.93%, Polycab 8.67%, Havells 5.63% and Finolex Cables 5.32%**, while UltraTech itself rose 1.18% on Friday.

**Nobody cut a forecast of what those companies will sell.** What changed is how much a rupee of their profit is worth now that a cement group with a very large balance sheet is in the same business. That distinction is explained in full below.

**The worst sector of the week was autos, down 3.95%**, and about half of that has a dated cause. Hero MotoCorp filed its August dispatch figures at 20:01 on Tuesday evening. Dispatches are units shipped from the factory to dealers, not units sold to customers, and Hero shipped **5,68,398 against an expectation near 5,90,000, with exports down 24.56%** on a year earlier. The share fell 4.59% the next day and the weakness spread to Eicher Motors, Mahindra and Mahindra and every one of the twenty-four component makers we group together.

**The shape of the week turned on Thursday, and it turned hard.** Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday each saw more than four hundred shares fall, with the middle share in our universe losing between half and three quarters of a percent. On Thursday **435 shares rose against 271 that fell and the middle share gained 0.32%**, while the Nifty 50 itself fell 0.17%. Friday finished almost exactly level, 349 up against 354 down. The smaller the company, the better Thursday was: Smallcap 250 +1.03%, Midcap 50 +0.28%, Nifty 500 +0.13%, Nifty 100 −0.11%, Nifty 50 −0.17%, Sensex −0.55%.

**On money, domestic institutions did all of the buying.** Across the four of five sessions where a figure was published, **foreign investors sold a net ₹12,300.31 crore and domestic institutions bought a net ₹20,343.40 crore**. Foreign money left on three of those four days. Domestic money arrived on all four. On Friday domestic buying was nearly three times foreign selling, which is the arithmetic behind an index finishing slightly up on a day when as many shares fell as rose.

### Monday 31 August

**The busiest session of the week for causes, and the one people got most wrong.** The MSCI index review took effect at Monday's close, transacting in the closing auction, which is the short window at the end of the day in which the official closing price is set. Funds that track that index had to trade at that close whatever they thought of the prices. The change had been public since 12 August.

**Adani Enterprises fell 9.76%, the largest Nifty 50 move of the day in either direction, and Adani Ports fell 6.70%.** This must not be written as an index outflow, because the review actually raised both companies' weights. Roughly a third of the Adani Enterprises fall landed in or around the closing auction, and the best available explanation is people unwinding positions put on ahead of a change everybody had known about for nineteen days.

**India's largest private bank said it is changing chief executive.** Sashidhar Jagdishan told HDFC Bank's board over the weekend that he will not seek reappointment and retires on 26 October. The share fell 1.57% to a fresh 52-week low on a day the banking index rose 0.92%, with Axis Bank up 2.77%, IndusInd up 2.27% and ICICI Bank up 2.19% doing the lifting instead.

**Six large consumer staples companies closed at 52-week lows in one session**, and our own reconstruction put the consumer staples index down 1.69%, the worst of the eleven sectors we can see that day, with eleven of its fourteen members lower. The candidate explanation in the brief is a scare about the cost of raw materials these companies buy. It was not established then and it is not established now.

**The explanation almost every outlet published did not survive checking.** The chain being run was that tension between the United States and Iran lifted crude oil, which lifted fear about input costs, which sank Indian shares. Crude was verified at about US$88.91, and the widely repeated claim that it had crossed US$90 was refused. One site was dropped from our source list for publishing two contradictory closes for the same session.

**And two of the three sector falls were one company each rather than a sector.** Media fell 2.84% on our own reconstruction, but Zee Entertainment alone fell 7.81% and a six-company index cannot absorb that. Metals fell 2.45% on a day copper itself rose 1.95%, which is the wrong way round for a cost story.

## Breadth and flows

Breadth is simply how many shares went up against how many went down. It answers a different question from the index, which is weighted towards the biggest companies and can be moved by a handful of them.

**This is the third consecutive week in which more shares fell than rose, and it is the widest of the three.** The week to 21 August ran 313 up against 385 down, the week to 28 August ran 316 up against 388 down, and this week ran **258 up against 446 down**. **The mid caps fell hardest and the small caps did not fall at all, which is the opposite of the usual shape.** When the market falls, the smallest companies normally fall the most, because they are the hardest to sell in a hurry. This week the Nifty Midcap 50 lost 1.51%, more than the Nifty 50, while the Nifty Smallcap 250 lost 0.08%.

**Nearly the whole of that gap opened on two days, and both are days we did not write up.** On Monday the mid caps rose 0.47% to a fresh 52-week high while the small caps fell 0.67%. On Thursday it went the other way again, small caps up 1.03% against mid caps up 0.28%. **No brief exists for Tuesday and none carries a cause for that reversal.** We are stating the fact rather than building a story about small-company resilience on top of it.

## Four stories in detail

### The cable repricing

**A share price is two numbers multiplied together: the profit a company earns, and how many rupees investors will pay for each rupee of that profit.** The second number is the multiple, or the price-to-earnings ratio. It is not a fact about the company. It is a statement about the future, and mostly about how long the current level of profit is expected to last.

**So a share can fall in two completely different ways.** Either the expected profit comes down, which is a company doing worse. Or the profit stays where it is and the multiple comes down, which is the same profit being trusted less far into the future.

**Almost nothing that happened this week says these companies will sell less cable next quarter.** Nobody published a cut to their revenue. What changed is who else is in the market. A new plant serving the same customers is more capacity arriving to meet demand that has not grown to match it, and the usual result of that is not fewer sales. It is lower prices, and thinner margins, for everyone already there.

**The identity of the entrant is what does the work.** A cable company that competes on price has to worry about its own survival while it does so. UltraTech does not. It is a cement group, the wires business is a small fraction of it, and it can afford to accept poor returns in cable for years without that threatening anything else it owns. A competitor who can afford to be patient about price is exactly the thing that shortens the expected life of a margin, and the expected life of a margin is most of what a multiple is.

For scale, and this arithmetic is ours rather than anyone's: **KEI's market value fell by roughly ₹6,900 crore over the week and R R Kabel's by roughly ₹5,100 crore. That is about ₹12,000 crore off two companies, against a ₹1,800 crore commitment by the company that caused it.**

**Two broker notes ran in the same week and they are not the same argument.** On 2 September JM Financial cut Polycab and KEI from Buy to Add and R R Kabel from Add to Reduce, citing absent volume growth and high copper costs, with twelve-month targets of ₹9,550, ₹5,750 and ₹2,750. **Those are worries about earnings.** On 4 September a Nuvama note argued UltraTech's entry could reprice the whole industry for a while, while still naming Polycab and KEI as its preferred holdings and saying industry fundamentals remain supported. **That is a multiple cut without an earnings cut, which is the same distinction in professional language.** Both are reported rather than confirmed, single desk each, and no analyst is named in our files.

**The market sorted the industry twice, and the second sort was better than the first.** On Wednesday it sold everything with the word cable attached to it. By Friday it had begun sorting by which metal each company actually buys. **Apar Industries sells aluminium power conductors, the heavy overhead lines strung between pylons, and holds a conductor order book of ₹7,779 crore against a cables book roughly a fifth that size.** An order book is the value of work already won and not yet delivered. Ultravolt attacks copper house wire, a different product for a different customer. On Wednesday none of that mattered and Apar fell 3.37%, the entire move being its industry and none of it the company. On Friday it fell 1.60%, about 2.05 percentage points better than its group and 7.35 points better than KEI. **Apar is the version of this that lasted, because it held on two separate sessions.** Diamond Power rose 2.76% on Friday and still finished the week down 5.04%, so for that name the sorting is a one-day effect that dissolves over five.

**Two limits on it.** The end-market split is our own reading of UltraTech's capacity disclosure and not a stated fact in any filing. And aluminium is not a free pass: **Lumino Industries, an aluminium-conductor and power-cable maker, listed on 3 September at a 34.15% premium on 118 times subscription, with annual revenue of ₹2,089.31 crore and profit of ₹160.00 crore.** A real competitor in that half of the market arrived in the same week, carrying a published price for the first time.

**One line nobody else drew.** Set the cable selling against two large orders in the same corner of the economy from late August, Skipper's ₹1,305 crore transmission order and Tejas Networks' ₹1,537 crore letter of intent. **Demand for capital equipment is visibly intact while the profitability of making cable is being repriced.** Those are two different things and the week separated them cleanly.

**And one company that rose all week and is not part of this story.** Birla Cable gained 27.60%, the largest riser in the industry. **It closed at the exchange's daily price limit on all five days: +5.00%, +5.00%, +4.99%, +4.99%, +4.99%.** When a share closes exactly at that limit, the price at which buyers and sellers would actually have cleared is unobservable, so the printed figure is a floor on demand rather than a measure of it. **The first two of those limit closes were 31 August and 1 September, and the Ultravolt filing did not exist until the evening of 3 September**, so any sentence attaching Birla Cable's week to it is wrong on the dates. It makes telecommunications cable rather than house wire, it filed nothing all week, and **cause not established in our briefs.**

### The exchange listing

**The market regulator cleared the National Stock Exchange to list its own shares on Friday evening, after the market had shut.** The timing is fixed from both sides: one outlet published at 15:35 saying approval was still pending, and the clearance was reported at 17:48. Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction ends at 15:35, so nothing published at 17:48 can have caused a price recorded at 15:35.

**The two shares that moved on it had been moving for three sessions.** The anticipation did not begin on Friday. **IFCI rose 12.67% on Wednesday 2 September, crossing ₹100 for the first time in eighteen years, and New India Assurance had already added 7.18% that day.** Both then rose again on Friday, New India Assurance by 17.70% and IFCI by 5.70%, to 52-week highs on roughly six times normal volume.

**Why those two companies.** New India Assurance holds 3.52 crore shares in the exchange, about 1.42% of it, **carried in its accounts at a weighted-average cost of ₹0.32 a share**, and will sell up to 1.05 crore of them in the listing. Company accounts generally carry an investment at what was paid for it rather than at what it is worth today, so a stake bought decades ago for almost nothing sits on the balance sheet at almost nothing however valuable it has become, and a listing converts that into a public price. **IFCI's link is one step longer:** it owns about 52.86% of Stock Holding Corporation, which in turn holds about 4.4% of the exchange.

**It is not an insurance story, and the check was run rather than assumed.** The insurance group's median move on Friday was −0.10% and ICICI Lombard closed at a 52-week low.

Every term of the listing is reported and sourced to unnamed people rather than to a document: an offer for sale of about ₹30,000 crore by existing owners, so no new money reaches the exchange itself, a valuation of ₹5.2 to ₹5.3 lakh crore, a price band expected on 11 September and a listing targeted on 25 September. **The shares will list on the other exchange, because an exchange may not list itself on its own platform.** That sounds like an error and is not.

**One number we cannot reconcile, carried openly.** Press reports put IFCI up about 12% to ₹107.45 on Friday. Our own file says 5.70% to ₹101.38. **That is a 6.3-point gap on a headline mover and the two are not blended.**

### The record in the reserves

**The Reserve Bank's weekly supplement showed India's foreign exchange reserves up US$11.475 billion to a record US$740.803 billion for the week ended 28 August**, taken off the bank's own document rather than a wire summary.

Reserves are the pile of foreign currency, mostly dollars, that the central bank holds. **The standard misreading of a big reserves number is that the bank has been busy propping up the rupee, and the direction gives it away.** Defending a currency means spending the pile, not building it: to hold the rupee up the central bank sells dollars out of reserves and buys rupees with them, and the reserves go down when that happens. A rise of eleven and a half billion dollars is evidence of the opposite activity, dollars coming in and being absorbed.

**Two cautions travel with it.** The data covers the seven days ending 28 August, so it says nothing about how the rupee traded between 1 and 4 September, and it cannot be written as "the central bank did not need to intervene last week." And part of the pile is gold, which changes in value when the gold price changes with nobody buying or selling anything. Last week's release included US$2.80 billion of gold. A record total is partly a record gold price.

It is the fifth consecutive weekly build. The week to 21 August added US$12.42 billion to reach US$729.33 billion, and 729.33 plus 11.475 reconciles with Friday's figure to two decimal places. The rupee did firm over the week: the dollar bought 94.475 rupees on Friday, 1.04% fewer than a week earlier.

### A growth target that keeps being restated instead of dropped

**KPI Green Energy has not abandoned a growth promise. It has moved the number four times while keeping the promise in place**, and that is a much harder thing to notice. This is built entirely on the company's own filed earnings-call transcripts, both read in full.

**The path, in the company's own numbers.** In May it was committing to grow 40% to 50% a year. By August it was committing to 30% to 40%. **The June quarter actually delivered total income of ₹710 crore against ₹614 crore a year earlier, a rise of 16%, and profit for that quarter fell to ₹95 crore from ₹111 crore.** The 16% is one quarter's revenue growth and not a year's delivery, and it should not be read as one.

**On the May call an individual investor read the whole ladder back to management.** Ashish Rampuria, introduced by the moderator as an individual investor rather than an analyst: *"Farukji, historically used to talk about 70%, 80% CAGR till FY '30, then it became 60%, 70%. Last 2 quarters, you were speaking 50%, 60% CAGR. Now last 3, 4 days with interviews now, we started talking 40%, 50% CAGR. Is there a structural slow down that you all are seeing?"* CAGR is the compound annual growth rate, the average yearly rate of increase over several years. So the target for the same year had already been cut three times before the one on that call. **The answer refused the word:** geopolitical conditions had produced *"some kind of what we say, less acceleration, I would say, I won't say slowdown."*

An analyst on the same call put the most recent step down directly, and the reply was *"There I don't see any decrease, 50% to 60% is there and 40% to 50%. So, 50% is common in both."* **The ranges overlap at one number, and the overlap is offered as proof that nothing changed.** In August the same exchange happened one step lower, when an analyst pointed out that the chairman had said 40% to 50% in an interview the day before.

**Why this is a mechanism rather than a complaint.** Guidance is what management tells investors to expect. It is not audited and it is not binding, so the only discipline on it is whether anyone checks it against what arrives. **A dropped promise is easy to catch**, because the company stops saying it and the silence shows up when you compare one transcript with the next. **A restated promise defeats that test completely.** The commitment is still there, described the same way, with the same confidence and the same horizon. Only the digits inside it are different, and the previous digits are not mentioned. Nothing is missing, so a check that looks for missing things finds nothing.

*One correction we owe on this company. A brief published on 2 September said the finance chief's seat was vacant with no named successor. That is withdrawn. Kapil Kriplani has been Group Chief Financial Officer since 11 August, appointed the same day his predecessor resigned, and the August transcript's opening remarks welcome him by name. Salim Yahoo, who gave the numbers quoted above, resigned on 11 August, presented the June quarter eight days later, and ceased to hold office at 15:55 on 2 September.*

### Company filings that never reached a brief

Two of the week's five sessions were never written up, and some of the largest corporate filings of the week landed on those days.

- **Ceigall India's ₹5,300 crore letter of intent is not an order, and the annexure says so.** The company's own press release is titled as a ₹5,300 crore letter of intent for a transmission project. The formal annexure says **₹5,300 crore is the total project cost including tax, which is capital the company must deploy, and what it will be paid is ₹608.67 crore a year in transmission charges for 35 years.** It is a competitively bid concession: build it, own it, operate it, over 36 months of construction and a 35-year life. Transformative in scale either way, but the transformation lands on the balance sheet first, because a project that size needs debt and equity raised against it.
- **A court ordered a forensic audit into a change of control at Fortis Healthcare.** The company told the exchange at 00:37 on 1 September that the Delhi High Court had directed the appointment of a forensic auditor to look into the dissipation of the former promoters' shareholding, the subsequent change of control, and the role of the relevant stakeholders. Its own statement notes that the court imposed no liability, penalty or fine, which is true and beside the point: **a court-ordered forensic audit is an evidence-gathering step, so there was no penalty stage to reach.** We cannot read the order itself. Separately, Fortis held an unscheduled analyst meeting on Friday "to discuss the recent developments" and filed the recording at 17:35, naming no quarter and no subject.
- **Four companies announced buybacks in five sessions, and the reason is a rule change.** A buyback is a company spending its own cash to buy back its own shares, leaving fewer outstanding. The open-market route, where a company simply buys through the exchange over time, had been closed and **the regulator's amended rules reopened it with effect from 1 August 2026.** Great Eastern Shipping, PVR INOX, Man Infraconstruction and Tips Music all announced in the week. Great Eastern is the one whose documents we read in full, and the headline is not the commitment: **the maximum is ₹900 crore and the binding minimum is ₹675 crore**, because the announcement says the company shall use at least 75% of the maximum. The ₹1,530 a share in it is a price ceiling, not a price.
- **ITC's technology arm is combining with a listed company.** ITC told the exchange on 31 August that ITC Infotech India will combine with Happiest Minds Technologies. Reported terms across several outlets: about 22.1% of Happiest Minds bought from its promoter group for roughly ₹1,330 crore, then a merger at 25 ITC Infotech shares for every 81 Happiest Minds shares, leaving ITC with about 73.4% of a company carrying roughly ₹7,033 crore of revenue. **No primary filing was opened on the swap ratio or the shareholding, so all of that is reported and not confirmed.**
- **Persistent Systems' board sat from 19:45 to 21:58 on 2 September and approved borrowing headroom of up to US$1,250 million and equity-linked fundraising of up to US$450 million**, filed at 22:16, past every Indian newsroom's deadline. **The purpose is not stated anywhere in the filing**, and an enabling authorisation of that kind is not required to state one.
- **Carraro India disclosed a customs order on Friday and the money is in the annexure.** The covering letter says only that the order confirms a differential tax demand with interest and penalties. The filing says there is "no immediate impact", and the word doing the work is "immediate". ## What to watch next week

Next week is five full sessions. The one exchange holiday in September is Ganesh Chaturthi on **Monday 14 September**, the Monday after next. *(Reported from holiday-calendar carriers; the exchange's own page was not opened.)*

**Monday 7 September, the exchange changes how the trading day opens.** From Monday the National Stock Exchange moves its 15-minute morning pre-open auction onto the same framework as the closing auction it introduced in August. The window stays 09:00 to 09:15 and splits into stages: orders may be placed, changed or cancelled from 09:00 to 09:05, only limit orders from 09:05 to 09:10 with a system-driven random close inside those two minutes, then matching and the setting of the opening price from 09:10 to 09:12. It applies to cash and derivatives. **Nothing about any company changes. What changes is when an order can still be pulled and when the first price of the day is fixed**, and the first sessions under a new mechanism are where odd opening prints come from. So an unusual open next week is more likely to be the plumbing than the news. *(Reported from Business Standard and several broker notices; the exchange's own circular timed out twice and was not read.)*

**Monday is also the first session that can price the exchange's own listing clearance**, which arrived after Friday's close with a full weekend of uncontested framing behind it. Two shares already moved on the expectation before the clearance existed.
