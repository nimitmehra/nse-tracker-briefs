## The week in plain English

The Nifty 50 finished the week at **23,897.70, down 1.15%** from 24,175.65 the Friday before. That is the third losing week in a row and the widest of the three. The Sensex fell 0.97% to 76,515.43, the Nifty 500 fell 1.17%, the Nifty Bank fell 0.22%, and the Nifty Midcap 50 fell 1.51%. The Nifty Smallcap 250 fell 0.08%, which is not a fall at all. India VIX, the market's own estimate of how much the Nifty is likely to move over the next month, started the week at 10.68 and finished at 10.68.

Underneath the index, **258 shares rose and 446 fell** out of the 704 we can price at both ends of the week. About 37 in every 100 finished higher than they started.

**The week's biggest story was a cement company deciding to sell electrical wire.** UltraTech Cement told the exchange at 07:11 on Tuesday 1 September that its ₹1,800 crore wires-and-cables plant at Bharuch had started commercial production, about four months ahead of its own published schedule. It filed again at 18:29 on Thursday evening to launch the brand, Ultravolt, which on day one makes the Aditya Birla group the second-largest wire producer in the country. Both filings landed outside trading hours, so each one was priced by the session that followed it. Over the five days **R R Kabel fell 15.53%, KEI Industries 12.93%, Polycab 8.67%, Havells 5.63% and Finolex Cables 5.32%**, while UltraTech itself rose 1.18% on Friday.

**Nobody cut a forecast of what those companies will sell.** What changed is how much a rupee of their profit is worth now that a cement group with a very large balance sheet is in the same business. That distinction is the most useful thing in this week's market and it gets a full explanation further down.

**The worst sector of the week was autos, down 3.95%**, and about half of that has a dated cause. Hero MotoCorp filed its August dispatch figures at 20:01 on Tuesday evening. Dispatches are units shipped from the factory to dealers, not units sold to customers, and Hero shipped **5,68,398 against an expectation near 5,90,000, with exports down 24.56%** on a year earlier. The share fell 4.59% the next day and the weakness spread to Eicher Motors, Mahindra and Mahindra and every one of the twenty-four component makers we group together.

**The shape of the week turned on Thursday, and it turned hard.** Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday each saw more than four hundred shares fall, with the middle share in our universe losing between half and three quarters of a percent. On Thursday **435 shares rose against 271 that fell and the middle share gained 0.32%**, while the Nifty 50 itself fell 0.17%. Friday finished almost exactly level, 349 up against 354 down. The smaller the company, the better Thursday was: Smallcap 250 +1.03%, Midcap 50 +0.28%, Nifty 500 +0.13%, Nifty 100 −0.11%, Nifty 50 −0.17%, Sensex −0.55%.

**On money, domestic institutions did all of the buying.** Across the four of five sessions where a figure was published, **foreign investors sold a net ₹12,300.31 crore and domestic institutions bought a net ₹20,343.40 crore**. Foreign money left on three of those four days. Domestic money arrived on all four. On Friday domestic buying was nearly three times foreign selling, which is the arithmetic behind an index finishing slightly up on a day when as many shares fell as rose.

### What we can and cannot show you this week

**Three of the five sessions were written up. Two were not.** The pipeline runs for Tuesday 1 September and Thursday 3 September both died before a brief was produced. The briefs that exist are Monday 31 August, Wednesday 2 September and Friday 4 September. And **the public feed published nothing at all this week**: the last public daily on file is 28 August, so a reader who follows only that chain has had nothing for five trading sessions.

**Market data is complete for all five sessions.** So the two missing days are measured but not explained. We have every closing price, every breadth count and every index level for Tuesday and Thursday, and no account of why any of them moved. **Thursday is the most interesting single session of the week and it is one of the two we did not write up.** That fact sits behind several honest gaps below, and it is stated once here rather than apologised for repeatedly.
