## The week in plain English

The Nifty 50 finished the week at **23,897.70, down 1.15%** from 24,175.65 the Friday before. That is the third losing week in a row and the widest of the three. The Sensex fell 0.97% to 76,515.43, the Nifty 500 fell 1.17%, the Nifty Bank fell 0.22%, and the Nifty Midcap 50 fell 1.51%. The Nifty Smallcap 250 fell 0.08%, which is not a fall at all. India VIX, the market's own estimate of how much the Nifty is likely to move over the next month, started the week at 10.68 and finished at 10.68.

Underneath the index, **258 shares rose and 446 fell** out of the 704 we can price at both ends of the week. About 37 in every 100 finished higher than they started.

**The week's biggest story was a cement company deciding to sell electrical wire.** UltraTech Cement told the exchange at 07:11 on Tuesday 1 September that its ₹1,800 crore wires-and-cables plant at Bharuch had started commercial production, about four months ahead of its own published schedule. It filed again at 18:29 on Thursday evening to launch the brand, Ultravolt, which on day one makes the Aditya Birla group the second-largest wire producer in the country. Both filings landed outside trading hours, so each one was priced by the session that followed it. Over the five days **R R Kabel fell 15.53%, KEI Industries 12.93%, Polycab 8.67%, Havells 5.63% and Finolex Cables 5.32%**, while UltraTech itself rose 1.18% on Friday.

**Nobody cut a forecast of what those companies will sell.** What changed is how much a rupee of their profit is worth now that a cement group with a very large balance sheet is in the same business. That distinction is the most useful thing in this week's market and it gets a full explanation further down.

**The worst sector of the week was autos, down 3.95%**, and about half of that has a dated cause. Hero MotoCorp filed its August dispatch figures at 20:01 on Tuesday evening. Dispatches are units shipped from the factory to dealers, not units sold to customers, and Hero shipped **5,68,398 against an expectation near 5,90,000, with exports down 24.56%** on a year earlier. The share fell 4.59% the next day and the weakness spread to Eicher Motors, Mahindra and Mahindra and every one of the twenty-four component makers we group together.

**The shape of the week turned on Thursday, and it turned hard.** Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday each saw more than four hundred shares fall, with the middle share in our universe losing between half and three quarters of a percent. On Thursday **435 shares rose against 271 that fell and the middle share gained 0.32%**, while the Nifty 50 itself fell 0.17%. Friday finished almost exactly level, 349 up against 354 down. The smaller the company, the better Thursday was: Smallcap 250 +1.03%, Midcap 50 +0.28%, Nifty 500 +0.13%, Nifty 100 −0.11%, Nifty 50 −0.17%, Sensex −0.55%.

**On money, domestic institutions did all of the buying.** Across the four of five sessions where a figure was published, **foreign investors sold a net ₹12,300.31 crore and domestic institutions bought a net ₹20,343.40 crore**. Foreign money left on three of those four days. Domestic money arrived on all four. On Friday domestic buying was nearly three times foreign selling, which is the arithmetic behind an index finishing slightly up on a day when as many shares fell as rose.

### What we can and cannot show you this week

**Three of the five sessions were written up. Two were not.** The pipeline runs for Tuesday 1 September and Thursday 3 September both died before a brief was produced. The briefs that exist are Monday 31 August, Wednesday 2 September and Friday 4 September. And **the public feed published nothing at all this week**: the last public daily on file is 28 August, so a reader who follows only that chain has had nothing for five trading sessions.

**Market data is complete for all five sessions.** So the two missing days are measured but not explained. We have every closing price, every breadth count and every index level for Tuesday and Thursday, and no account of why any of them moved. **Thursday is the most interesting single session of the week and it is one of the two we did not write up.** That fact sits behind several honest gaps below, and it is stated once here rather than apologised for repeatedly.

### Monday 31 August

**The busiest session of the week for causes, and the one people got most wrong.** The MSCI index review took effect at Monday's close, transacting in the closing auction, which is the short window at the end of the day in which the official closing price is set. Funds that track that index had to trade at that close whatever they thought of the prices. The change had been public since 12 August.

**Adani Enterprises fell 9.76%, the largest Nifty 50 move of the day in either direction, and Adani Ports fell 6.70%.** This must not be written as an index outflow, because the review actually raised both companies' weights. Roughly a third of the Adani Enterprises fall landed in or around the closing auction, and the best available explanation is people unwinding positions put on ahead of a change everybody had known about for nineteen days.

**India's largest private bank said it is changing chief executive.** Sashidhar Jagdishan told HDFC Bank's board over the weekend that he will not seek reappointment and retires on 26 October. The share fell 1.57% to a fresh 52-week low on a day the banking index rose 0.92%, with Axis Bank up 2.77%, IndusInd up 2.27% and ICICI Bank up 2.19% doing the lifting instead.

**Six large consumer staples companies closed at 52-week lows in one session**, and our own reconstruction put the consumer staples index down 1.69%, the worst of the eleven sectors we can see that day, with eleven of its fourteen members lower. The candidate explanation in the brief is a scare about the cost of raw materials these companies buy. It was not established then and it is not established now.

**The explanation almost every outlet published did not survive checking.** The chain being run was that tension between the United States and Iran lifted crude oil, which lifted fear about input costs, which sank Indian shares. Crude was verified at about US$88.91, and the widely repeated claim that it had crossed US$90 was refused. One site was dropped from our source list for publishing two contradictory closes for the same session.

**And two of the three sector falls were single companies wearing a sector's clothes.** Media fell 2.84% on our own reconstruction, but Zee Entertainment alone fell 7.81% and a six-company index cannot absorb that. Metals fell 2.45% on a day copper itself rose 1.95%, which is the wrong way round for a cost story.

One thing Monday could not explain: the rupee strengthened 0.33% on a day crude rose and the dollar firmed, which is backwards for a country that imports most of its oil. That question gets a partial answer on Friday.

### Tuesday 1 September

**No brief exists for this session.** Most of Tuesday's causes did reach the desk one day late, because Wednesday's brief deliberately covered both days and dated each item to where it belonged.

**UltraTech's commercial-production filing landed at 07:11, before the 09:15 open, which makes Tuesday the first session that could price it.** KEI Industries fell 6.83%, R R Kabel 2.00% and Polycab 5.82%. **HFCL disclosed a three-year optical-fibre supply agreement worth about ₹2,329 crore at 08:49 and its share fell 4.38% the following session anyway**, because the selling sweeping the industry was about copper power cable and HFCL sells glass fibre.

**It was the only session of the week on which both foreign and domestic institutions bought**, foreign investors ₹1,143.38 crore and domestic institutions ₹1,846.94 crore. Crude rose to a six-week high near US$95 as the United States began strikes on Iranian targets.

**What no brief explains, and it is a real hole.** Nifty Bank fell 1.06% and the Nifty Midcap 50 fell 1.31%, the two largest index moves of the day, and that mid-cap fall is the single biggest reason the Midcap 50 finished the week worse than the Nifty 50. **Cause not established in our briefs.**

### Wednesday 2 September

**The worst breadth of the week: 236 shares up, 472 down.** The cable selling ran a second day and widened into the industries that supply the same customers. Our own group of twenty-four auto-component makers fell a median 2.35% with not one of the twenty-four positive, and vehicle makers fell 1.87% on the Hero dispatch miss filed the previous evening.

**Money did not rotate anywhere. It just stopped.** Companies with a cost problem were sold and owners of power capacity were not: power and utilities rose a median 0.87% on the same session.

**The technology index understated the damage, and the gap is the finding.** Nifty IT closed down 1.25%, while the middle company in our own 21-name software group fell 2.05%. An index weighted towards the biggest companies will always hide this. Infosys at −1.38% and Tata Consultancy Services at −0.89% sat near the index. RateGain at −3.94% and Cyient at −3.64% did not.

**One number that is still circulating and is wrong.** Several desks filed by lunchtime reporting the Sensex down nearly 700 points at 76,254. **Those were 11:37 intraday levels, not closes.** The Sensex finished down 375 points, or 0.49%. Roughly half the morning fall was taken back after every desk had filed.

And on fear: India VIX rose less than 1% on a session with 472 fallers and 111 companies down 2% or more. That is what steady selling looks like, not panic.

### Thursday 3 September

**No brief exists for this session either.** The run gathered its data and finished its graph work, then stopped before the writing.

**This is the pivot of the week.** Advancers went from 236 to 435, decliners from 472 to 271, and the middle share went from −0.65% to +0.32%. The gradient by company size was clean and ran in one direction: the more concentrated the index, the worse it did. Nifty Bank rose 0.36%, India VIX fell 2.18%, foreign investors sold ₹2,345.87 crore and domestic institutions bought ₹4,977.46 crore.

Two sector figures for Thursday survive inside Friday's brief and can be used: **Nifty Realty rose 2.58% and Nifty IT fell 0.85%.** So property companies were the strongest thing on the board and software was among the weakest.

**No brief on file explains why breadth inverted that day.** The Wednesday brief was written before it happened and the Friday brief does not go back for it. There is a plausible mechanical candidate, which is that weekly index derivatives settle on the Nifty and the Bank Nifty and essentially nowhere else, so settlement-day positioning lands on the index rather than on smaller companies. **We cannot use it, because our own briefs give three different days for this week's weekly settlement.** That is dealt with at the end.

### Friday 4 September

**The cable repricing took its second and larger step.** UltraTech's Ultravolt filing had gone out at 18:29 on Thursday evening, so Friday was the first session that could price it. **KEI fell 8.95%, R R Kabel 6.25%, Polycab 5.76%, Havells 3.83% and Finolex Cables 3.46%, and UltraTech rose 1.18%.**

**Two shares rose sharply on a regulator's decision that had not been announced yet.** The market regulator cleared the National Stock Exchange's own listing on Friday evening. One outlet published at 15:35 saying approval was still pending, and the clearance was reported at 17:48. **New India Assurance rose 17.70% and IFCI 5.70% during the session, both to 52-week highs on roughly six times normal volume.** They anticipated the clearance. They did not react to it.

**Domestic institutions bought nearly three times what foreigners sold**, ₹8,930.12 crore against ₹3,111.94 crore.

**And India's foreign exchange reserves hit a record**, up US$11.475 billion to US$740.803 billion in the week ended 28 August, read off the Reserve Bank's own weekly statistical supplement rather than a wire summary. The desk had been watching for a fall of five billion or more, which would have been evidence the central bank was spending reserves to hold the rupee up. It got the opposite sign. **The figure covers the week to 28 August, so it says nothing about how the rupee traded between 1 and 4 September**, and it must not be written as "the central bank did not need to intervene."

## Breadth and flows

Breadth is simply how many shares went up against how many went down. It answers a different question from the index, which is weighted towards the biggest companies and can be moved by a handful of them.

**This is the third consecutive week in which more shares fell than rose, and it is the widest of the three.** The week to 21 August ran 313 up against 385 down, the week to 28 August ran 316 up against 388 down, and this week ran **258 up against 446 down**. On four of the five sessions the index and the middle share told the same story. Thursday is the day they disagreed, and they disagreed sharply.

**The mid caps fell hardest and the small caps did not fall at all, which is the opposite of the usual shape.** When the market falls, the smallest companies normally fall the most, because they are the hardest to sell in a hurry. This week the Nifty Midcap 50 lost 1.51%, more than the Nifty 50, while the Nifty Smallcap 250 lost 0.08%.

**Nearly the whole of that gap opened on two days, and both are days we did not write up.** On Monday the mid caps rose 0.47% to a fresh 52-week high while the small caps fell 0.67%. On Tuesday it reversed completely: mid caps down 1.31%, small caps flat. On Thursday it went the other way again, small caps up 1.03% against mid caps up 0.28%. **No brief exists for Tuesday and none carries a cause for that reversal.** We are stating the fact rather than building a story about small-company resilience on top of it.

That is what an index review transacting in a single closing auction looks like in the flow data. The turnover figures are ours, computed from our own daily files.

### The price of insurance did not change all week

India VIX started at 10.68, rose about 8.5% into the middle of the week, and gave all of it back to finish at exactly 10.68. At that level it sits 1.04% above its own 52-week low of 10.57, the same distance it sat a week earlier.

A low reading means protection against a surprise is cheap, because almost nobody is buying it. The market charged close to the least it has charged in a year for that protection through a week in which the United States and Iran were exchanging fire around the Strait of Hormuz. **Low volatility is what protection costs when nobody is buying any. It is not a forecast of calm.**

*One reading inside that path is broken and we are not quoting it. Our 1 September file records India VIX unchanged at 11.19 with the same weekly, monthly and yearly percentages as the 31 August file, which is the signature of a stale copy. The 2 September reading of 11.5925, described as a rise of 0.89%, back-solves to a prior close near 11.49. Every other link in the chain checks out exactly, so the two endpoints are sound and the middle is not.*

## Earnings

**Not one company in the 708 shares we price reported results this week, and this section is short because that is the honest length.** India's June-quarter reporting season finished before the week began. What is left is the paperwork behind it.

Twelve companies appear on our system's reporter roster for the week. **Every one of the twelve was checked by name against the priced universe and none of them is in it.** Nor does any of the twelve appear anywhere in the three briefs that exist. Five of the twelve genuinely reported for the first time: Milky Mist Dairy Food, LEAP India, Setco Automotive, Technocraft Ventures and Supreme Engineering. **All five filed after the market had shut**, at 15:57, 16:25, 16:39, 17:05 and 20:12, against continuous trading that ends at 15:15 and a closing auction that ends at 15:35. Not one result filed this week could have moved its own share price on its own day.

The other seven filed paperwork about numbers that were already public. Two of them carry the evidence in the filename: Kritika Wires' document is named for a board meeting held on 4 August, a month before the copy, and Shah Metacorp's results row is a machine-readable copy of accounts approved on 12 August, filed on the same second as a change of auditors. **Its Monday board meeting was about the auditor, not about the numbers.**

### The exchange asked fifty-six companies to explain their accounts

Regulation 33 is the listing rule setting out what a company must file with its quarterly accounts and by when. A clarification request under it means the exchange thinks something is missing or unclear. Last Friday it sent ten in one thirteen-minute block. **This week it sent fifty-six, and seventeen replies came back.** Every request cites the quarter ended 30 June 2026.

**Sixty-five of the sixty-six requests in our window say nothing about what is wrong.** Only one, to BF Utilities, states a reason: consolidated results not submitted. For the rest the feed carries a rule citation and the words "the response of the Company is awaited," and nothing else. We cannot tell you what those companies did wrong, and neither can anybody else reading the same feed.

**One case runs from the question to the answer and it is worth the space.** On 27 August the exchange wrote to Supreme Engineering about the year ended 31 March 2026 saying it had not submitted a statement of the impact of its audit qualifications. When an auditor cannot sign off on a company's accounts cleanly, it records what it could not verify, and the company must then file a separate statement setting out what those reservations do to the reported numbers. Supreme Engineering had filed the accounts and not that statement. **On Friday at 16:25 it filed a document named for revised financials with a covering letter and the qualification.** We did not open it, so we can tell you what class of document arrived and not what it says.

Only one of the fifty-six companies asked this week is a share we price. Aditya Infotech was asked at 17:53 on Thursday, after the close, and fell 1.24% on Friday against a Nifty 50 up 0.10%. That is well inside ordinary daily movement. **Cause not established in our briefs.**

### The other half of reporting is mostly missing

**The good example first, and it comes from a company nobody has heard of.** LEAP India reported on Monday evening and **filed the full transcript of its earnings call on Friday, four days later**. Milky Mist filed a call recording within a day of reporting. Neither company is large enough for anyone to price.

Against that, **four companies we do price reported weeks ago and have still published no transcript**: Gandhar Oil Refinery at 41 days outstanding, Rajratan Global Wire and Sandhar Technologies at 35 each, and KRN Heat Exchanger at 21. Gandhar has posted a recording link, which is not a transcript. Three of the four have had a strong quarter of share-price performance while that record stays unpublished: Gandhar up 69.51% over three months, KRN up 28.14%, Rajratan up 12.29%, with Sandhar down 11.98%.
