## 1. The week in plain English

The Nifty 50 finished the week at 24,175.65, down 0.31% across five sessions. The Nifty 500, the broadest index we track and the one that comes nearest to describing the whole listed market, finished down 0.01%. After a full week of trading, that is dead flat.

It was not a quiet week. It was a round trip: 24,219.05, then 24,334.55, then 24,207.75, then 24,090.85, then 24,175.65. Tuesday's close was the week's high, Thursday's was its low, and Friday gave back a little under half of the fall. The Nifty 500's best and worst closes sit 129 points apart and the week's net change is under two points.

Three separate forces pushed in three directions and roughly cancelled. None of them had anything to do with the other two.

**Technology moved on an American clock.** Nifty IT, the index of the country's software services companies, fell 1.47% on Wednesday after the United States paused visa appointments at its embassies and consulates worldwide, which constrains how quickly Indian firms move staff to client sites. It fell a further 0.32% on Thursday and rose 3.51% on Friday alone, after an overnight American technology rally. Over the week it gained 2.45%.

**Crude oil fell on every single session.** Brent, the global benchmark grade against which India prices most of what it imports, closed around US$88.36 against US$94.19 the previous Friday, a fall of roughly 6%. The cause was diplomacy rather than demand: Iran and Oman resumed talks over the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea passage through which most of India's crude arrives, discussing a temporary navigational corridor and agreeing to work on clearing mines. Cheaper oil is a tailwind for a country that imports about 85% of what it refines.

**And the domestic-demand complex sold off quietly all week.** Consumer staples, meaning the everyday products Indian households buy from Indian companies, fell on almost every session we can measure. On our own recomputed figures, Nifty FMCG lost 1.46% on the week, Nifty Consumption 0.95% and Nifty Auto 0.94%. Those are the three largest weekly sector falls in the market and no headline named any of them.

Now the number that describes the week. **Across roughly 704 shares we can price reliably, 316 rose over the week and 388 fell.** The typical Indian share lost money in a week the headline called flat, and the Nifty Smallcap 250 index still finished Friday at a 52-week high. Day by day it is starker: **on three of the five sessions the index moved one way and the typical share moved the other**, and on a fourth the index understated the fall by nearly half. Exactly one session, Thursday, had a headline that honestly described what happened to the average shareholder.

Anyone who read this week off the index read almost none of it.

## 2. How each day moved, and why

Two words first. **Breadth** is the count of shares that rose against the count that fell, out of roughly 705 companies we can price each day. The **median share** is the one exactly in the middle of that list, so it describes the typical company's day rather than the biggest company's day. An index cannot tell you either, because it weights every company by size.

| Session | Nifty 50 | Rose | Fell | Median share | Did the index describe the market? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon 24 Aug | 24,219.05, **−0.14%** | 302 | 400 | −0.24% | Direction yes, scale no |
| Tue 25 Aug | 24,334.55, **+0.48%** | 320 | 386 | −0.12% | **No, opposite** |
| Wed 26 Aug | 24,207.75, **−0.52%** | 383 | 322 | +0.14% | **No, opposite** |
| Thu 27 Aug | 24,090.85, **−0.48%** | 253 | 449 | −0.46% | **Yes, cleanly** |
| Fri 28 Aug | 24,175.65, **+0.35%** | 343 | 361 | −0.04% | **No, opposite** |

**Monday: a flat index sitting on a falling market.** The Nifty lost 32.95 points, which on 24,219 is not worth an explanation in itself, and 400 shares fell against 302 that rose, the second consecutive session of a falling market under a flat headline. The day's named cause was geopolitical and it pointed the wrong way. The United States Treasury Secretary announced what he called the single greatest financial offensive ever mounted against Iran, and the part that reaches India is that Washington says it will sanction any country that keeps trading with Iran. Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority replied that vessels breaching its rules face fines, seizure or confiscation. **And yet Brent fell about 1.8% on the day, to roughly US$92.68.** A threat to oil supply that leaves oil cheaper has not been priced as a threat to oil supply. What appears to have been priced instead is the payments and trade channel, meaning the risk to how India settles and routes purchases, rather than the size of the import bill. Keep the scale in view: a 0.14% close is a very small reaction for a country that imports 85% of its crude through that waterway. And the sanctions announcement itself landed between roughly 17:30 and 19:00 India time, after our market had shut. **Monday traded the anticipation, pre-announced on Sunday. It did not trade the contents.**

**Tuesday: the index rose and most shares fell.** The Nifty rose 0.48% to the week's high while 386 shares fell and 320 rose. This is the cleanest explanation of the week and it has nothing to do with sentiment. Tuesday was the National Stock Exchange's monthly derivatives expiry, the day the month's futures and options contracts settle and positions in them must be closed or rolled forward. The date was checked against the calendar rather than assumed: 1 August was a Saturday, which puts the month's Tuesdays on the 4th, 11th, 18th and 25th, and the convention is the last Tuesday. Settlement demand concentrates in a narrow band of very large, heavily traded companies, which is exactly the band an index weights most. **Expiry propped the index through a handful of large companies while the rest of the market did not participate.** India VIX, the index measuring what traders pay for protection against a sharp move over the coming month, fell 3.95% on a day the index rose, which is not the usual pairing.

**Wednesday: the index fell and two-thirds of shares rose.** The Nifty fell 0.52% while 383 shares rose, the exact reverse of Tuesday on consecutive days. The dated cause of the weakness at the top was real and narrow: the United States paused visa appointments at embassies and consulates worldwide while it re-screens and retrains consular officers, with no announced date for normal availability to resume. That is the one dated cause behind Nifty IT falling 1.47%. **But the index badly overstates it.** Across the IT index's 34 constituents the median share fell only 0.39%, twelve rose, and Cyient gained 7.01%. Five very large companies were effectively the whole move, and the gap between the sector's best and worst performer was 12.41 percentage points, the widest in the market, which is the opposite of what a sector-wide shock looks like. One caution: the six-figure H-1B visa fee circulating alongside this story is a proposal, not a rule in force.

The genuinely broad falls on Wednesday went unnamed everywhere. Consumption fell 0.82% with six of 27 names higher, FMCG 0.70% with six of 22. Against them metals rose 1.75% with sixteen of eighteen higher, pharma 0.89% with seventeen of 24, public-sector banks 0.62% with all thirteen higher. **The size gradient reads as one line: Nifty 50 constituents down 0.66%, Nifty 100 down 0.43%, Midcap 50 down 0.17%, Smallcap 250 up 0.59%.** The bigger the company, the worse the day. Crude fell again on the Iran and Oman corridor talks, with two cautions attached: a corridor has been discussed, not announced; and a political claim that all mines are already cleared sits in the same news cycle as an agreement to clear them, and both cannot be true. India VIX fell 4.65% to 10.565, near its 52-week low, through a session that opened with an unresolved Hormuz blockage and a visa pause on the country's largest export-services sector. **The option market had stopped pricing either as a live risk.**

**Thursday: the one session where the index told the truth.** The Nifty fell 0.48%, 449 shares fell against 253, and the median share fell 0.46%. It was the week's worst day for the typical shareholder and the only day the headline matched the experience: 238 shares fell more than 1% against 120 that rose more than 1%, so the whole distribution shifted rather than just its tail.

There are two answers to why, and the first is arithmetic. **HDFC Bank fell 2.23% to ₹711.00, and on roughly a 13% weight in the index that single share takes about 0.29 percentage points out of a 0.48% fall.** Its heavier weight in the Sensex is why the Sensex fell 0.70%. Nobody should write that banks sold off: Kotak Mahindra Bank rose 1.80%, ICICI Bank 0.91%, Axis Bank 0.08%, and the private-bank group's median of minus 0.34% beat the market. What is wearing HDFC Bank down is its own arithmetic: its net interest margin, the gap between what a bank earns on loans and pays on deposits, is at a record low 3.26%, low-cost deposits are 32% of the book against 38% two years ago, and foreign holding has fallen for a fifth straight quarter. Two dated items sat alongside: a United States lawsuit disclosed that day on a single credible report we did not second-source, and an escalation dated 26 August in which more than 75 investors holding over US$13.5 million of principal in a Luxembourg life fund, sold through the bank's Dubai operations, are writing to the Prime Minister's Office and approaching the Reserve Bank. **That is reported investor intent, not a filed complaint, and it should be read at that grade.**

Second, Thursday was the BSE Sensex's monthly expiry, on the last-Thursday convention, while the Nifty's own monthly expiry had gone on Tuesday. So the Sensex falling harder needs no sentiment explanation at all.

Positives first on the sectors. Pharma was the day's one real advance and it was broad: the index rose 0.84%, fourteen of nineteen constituents rose, and removing the two largest risers still leaves the remaining seventeen positive. Underneath it sat a split with no established cause, as pharmaceutical manufacturing and hospitals rose while diagnostics were sold hard, Thyrocare down 3.92%, Concord Biotech down 3.91%. **Cement was the casualty: not one of eleven cement names rose and the median cement share fell 1.03%.** No dated cement demand, pricing or policy item from Thursday turned up anywhere, and after a full search that is a finding rather than a gap. India VIX rose 4.71% to 11.0675, the textbook pairing Tuesday and Wednesday both lacked, though 11.07 is 60.3% below its own 52-week high, so it corroborates direction and establishes nothing about stress.

One last Thursday finding, because it cuts against the usual story. Midcap 50 fell 0.10% and Smallcap 250 fell 0.12%, against a small-company median of minus 0.54%, the worst of any group measured, while the Nifty 50's own median constituent fell 0.27%, shallower than the market. **The small-company indices understated the damage in exactly the way the large-company indices overstated it.**

**Friday: a green headline on a flat market.** The Nifty rose 0.35% and 361 shares fell against 343. The median share lost 0.04%. It was one sector: **Nifty IT rose 3.51% with all nine of its priced constituents higher, and on that sector's weight it accounts for effectively the whole index gain.** Coforge rose 5.93%, Tata Consultancy Services 4.16%, Persistent Systems 3.91%, Tech Mahindra 3.53%, Infosys 2.99%, Mphasis 1.72%. Strip the technology names out of the Nifty 50 and its own constituents were 18 up, 18 down, one flat, median 0.00%. Why technology and why Friday rather than Thursday is section 6's second explainer.

Two facts belong side by side. The Smallcap 250 closed at a 52-week high on a session where 361 of 706 shares fell, so the index was at a record and the median small-company shareholder made nothing. And not one share in the 707-name universe fell 5% or more; the worst close anywhere was minus 4.66%.

**One thing the reader is owed about this week's record.** Two of the last seven trading sessions never got their own evening write-up. Friday 21 August and Tuesday 25 August both died mid-run, Tuesday's at a 600-second stall after it had written 91 signals onto 36 nodes and before it could compute any of them. Neither is a blank: Monday's brief covered two sessions and Wednesday's covered two, and everything above dated to Tuesday comes from Wednesday's brief, written a day late and dated honestly. **What is genuinely missing is the public record. There is no public brief for Tuesday and none for Wednesday, so a reader who follows only the public feed went from Monday evening to Thursday morning with nothing.** The prices for all five sessions exist and are used throughout. What was missing was our writing, not the observation.

Both statements are true, and the reason is the weighting arithmetic that runs through the whole week: within the small-company universe, the larger small companies can carry the index while the count of shares goes the other way.

There is a second week of evidence. **The weekly breadth counts for the two most recent weeks are almost identical**: 313 up and 385 down in the week to 21 August, then 316 up and 388 down this week, out of roughly seven hundred. Against the week ending 7 August, when 427 rose and 259 fell, the change in character is unmistakable. There is no row in our record for the week ending 14 August, so that comparison skips a week.

India VIX closed the week at 10.68, down 4.64%, having run 11.525, about 11.07, 10.565, 11.0675 and 10.68. It measures what traders pay to insure against a sharp index move over the next month, so a low number is a statement about complacency rather than about safety. **It ended 1.04% above its own 52-week low, three days before a growth release with a forecast spread of more than a full percentage point on it.** Low volatility is not a forecast of calm. It is what protection costs when almost nobody is buying any.

## 4. Earnings: the week that was not one

**Six Indian companies actually reported results this week, and all six filed on Tuesday and Wednesday.** Not one of them is a company we can price, and not one price move in any of the week's seven briefs is attributed to a result.

### A company being asked a question is not a company answering one

Our own scan flagged KEI Industries and Gallantt Ispat as reporters on Friday. Both filings were the opposite of a result: the exchange asking each company for a clarification on its June-quarter numbers, reply awaited, with no figures attached. Reading the underlying feed, that mislabel was five times larger than the two names we caught. **The exchange broadcast ten clarification requests in a single block between 12:08 and 12:21 on Friday, under thirteen minutes, every one for the quarter ended 30 June 2026.** Regulation 33 is the listing rule setting what a company must file with its quarterly accounts and by when; a clarification request under it means the exchange thinks something is missing or unclear. **Ten inside thirteen minutes is one compliance sweep by the exchange, not ten company events.**

The point generalises. **The file on this desk named for financial results contained no financial results at all this week.** All seventeen of its rows are the exchange asking for a clarification, thirteen of them, or a company replying to one, four. One of those replies concerned the quarter that ended 31 March 2021, five years ago.

### The six, and why our own count said fifteen

Our weekly data file counts seventeen filings from fifteen companies. Reading each row, they split into three classes: companies reporting for the first time, the same result arriving again in a different wrapper, and things that are not results. Monday's single "reporter" is a newspaper advertisement of results already published. Thursday's seven are machine-readable re-filings, press releases, call recordings, a revision of results approved on 22 August, and one copy of a board meeting held on 11 August.

**The six that genuinely reported.** Tuesday: Ardee Industries at 14:35, MV Electrosystems at 16:33, Vineet Laboratories at 19:35 and again at 19:52, State Trading Corporation of India at 21:51. Wednesday: Simbhaoli Sugars at 18:00 and Juniper Green Energy at 19:53. Vineet Laboratories is a finding on its own: it filed two sets of results seventeen minutes apart, the June 2026 quarter and then **the full year ended 31 March 2026**, an annual account published five months after the year finished. Why is not established anywhere in our briefs.

### Why none of it moved anything

We price 707 Indian shares. **Not one of the fifteen names on the roster is among them.** The only two symbols in this week's entire earnings conversation that we can price are KEI and Gallantt, and those are the two that were not reporters. Their non-reaction is measurable precisely because they are in the universe: KEI rose 0.61% and Gallantt 0.17% on Friday, against an index up 0.35%.

**So we did not fail to explain a results reaction. There was no reaction to explain.** This was a week of very small companies filing very late, and almost nobody was looking.

Two further points. **Only one of the six landed while the market was open.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and Ardee filed at 14:35, the single result all week that could have moved its own share on its own day, and it arrived on Tuesday, the session whose pipeline died. **Four of the six filed on Tuesday and two more on Wednesday evening, so two-thirds of the week's real earnings news landed on the day we published nothing and the rest on a day the public feed did not cover either.** We hold every one of those filings. We published no same-day read on any. And we do not hold their numbers: the revenue and profit figures sit in PDF attachments nothing in our chain parsed.

### The results still moving Indian shares were reported before this week

Positives first. **LT Foods rose 7.34% to ₹459 on Monday on a competitor's numbers, not its own.** KRBL's June quarter showed basmati realisations, meaning the average price a tonne of rice actually fetched, holding about 20% higher than a year earlier despite West Asian shipping disruption, with management guiding export volumes to recover from the September quarter. LT Foods finished the week up 10.30%, though it touched ₹482.85 during Monday and closed at ₹459, handing back roughly half the move. There is a tension inside it: the rice read and the oil read price opposite outcomes of the same event, because the rice case depends on West Asian shipping normalising while the week's oil fall was a bet on the same thing.

**A two-week-old result was still doing the work on Friday.** The exchange asked NMDC Steel why its trading volume had spiked. The company replied before Friday's open that nothing new had happened, pointing back to results of 14 August and a credit-rating upgrade. The share then rose another 8.96%, taking it to plus 23.20% over the week.

Now the negatives. **KPI Green Energy's June quarter shows the business and the balance sheet moving in opposite directions.** Operating profit rose 19% and the operating margin, the share of every hundred rupees of sales kept as profit after running costs, improved by 1.3 percentage points. Net profit still fell 15%, because finance costs, the interest paid on borrowings, more than doubled at plus 109%. One quarter is not a pattern; the test is whether it persists into the September quarter.

**IRCTC's June quarter is a mix problem, and our two readings of it do not agree.** Revenue grew 18.1% to ₹1,370 crore while operating profit fell 2.77% and the operating margin dropped 5.1 percentage points, because ticketing, which earns margins above 80%, grew 0.5% while catering grew 33.8%. Growing fast in the low-margin half and standing still in the high-margin half does that to a profit line even when sales rise by nearly a fifth. A second reading later in the week put the fall at 6.1 percentage points to 28.2%, from a single aggregator with no second source. **Both describe the June quarter's margin falling year on year, and 5.1 and 6.1 percentage points are not the same number.** They may be different measures rather than a contradiction, since operating margin and EBITDA margin are not the same line, but nothing in our chain reconciles them. The 5.1 figure carries the stronger provenance and is the one used here.

**Garware Hi-Tech Films carries an open question rather than a result:** management guides its margin about 5 percentage points below the quarter it has just delivered.

**And one earnings-adjacent event with no earnings in it.** After Hexaware's AI Day presentation on Friday 21 August, six houses published price views on identical disclosed facts: CLSA at ₹730, Kotak ₹660, IIFL ₹620, Axis ₹610, HSBC ₹575 at Hold, Jefferies ₹500 at Underperform. That is quoted with attribution and is not our view. The share fell 2.16% on the day of the presentation and rose 5.64% on Monday. **A spread that wide published off the same slides is the market admitting it has no settled method for pricing an "AI-powered revenue" claim**, and that claim, covering about half of revenue, is the chief executive's own and is defined nowhere.

### The season's missing half

The call, where management takes questions, is the other half of reporting, and this week it is largely absent. **Apar Industries' June-quarter transcript was 34 days pending by Thursday**, 20 days past its own timeout, and the company filed on Wednesday and Thursday without filing that. **Varun Beverages' is 28 days pending**, which has a direct consequence: the company approved a new subsidiary to enter ready-to-drink alcoholic beverages this week, and nobody has been able to ask whether its bottling agreements permit the parent to build one. Five quantified commitments made on Transrail Lighting's 3 February call are all past their own check dates and none can be tested, because the transcripts that would settle them cannot be read.

Against that, the week's one positive process finding. **Several of the smallest companies on the exchange ran a better disclosure calendar than several much larger ones.** Juniper Green filed a press release, an investor presentation, a call recording and a newspaper advertisement around its result, and pre-announced the call. Ardee and MV Electrosystems each filed presentations and call material. Lohia Corp filed the transcript of its 20 August call, and Patel Retail filed a recording, a presentation and its quarterly transcript inside the same week.

## 5. The sectors, and why they moved

### Read this before the table

**For all five sessions, the exchange's own sector indices were almost entirely unreadable on our feed.** Nine of the eleven produced no usable daily move on Monday, and the same nine were dark again on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday. On several days four more failed to arrive at all, including Consumer Durables, Healthcare, Oil and Gas and Private Bank. **Only Nifty IT and Nifty Pharma produced a live reading on any day this week.**

**That has two consequences, and both belong to the reader.**

**First, our own weekly sector figures were wrong, and we caught them.** The automated file this brief is built from published a table showing seven sectors up and three down. Recomputing every sector by hand from the closing prices we hold for 21 and 28 August, **nine of its eleven rows are wrong, including four where the sign is reversed.** Auto, Media, Consumption and PSU Bank were all reported higher and all four fell. The true count is three up and eight down. This is the third consecutive week the same block has failed the same way, and this week's row was published without the correction note the previous week carried.

**Second, everything below that is not IT or Pharma is our own reconstruction.** Where the index was missing we built sector direction from the individual shares: take every company in the sector we can price, find the middle one, report that. It answers "what did the typical share in this sector do", which is not the same question as "what did the sector index do". We tested the substitute against the two live indices every session. It reproduced Pharma to within six basis points on Monday and matched both indices on Thursday and Friday, and **it failed visibly on Wednesday**, when Nifty IT fell roughly four times harder than its own middle member. The method works most days and it broke once, in the open.

### There is one story here, not two

The best and worst sectors share one explanation. On Wednesday money moved out of consumer-facing companies into metals, pharma and public-sector banks. On Friday the same shape returned: FMCG, consumption, autos and financials all fell on two-to-one decline breadth or worse, inside an index that closed up, while money went to technology and metals.

**In plain words: companies that make their money selling to Indian households were sold, and companies whose fortunes are set outside India were bought.** That rests on counts of named shares rather than on the index levels that failed us.

**Two honest qualifications travel with it.** The metals half has a hole never filled. On Monday the one base-metals price we could honestly read was copper at plus 0.20%, which does not support a metals rally, and the aluminium reading was one of six figures suspected of a contract-roll error and was not used in either direction. Commodity daily changes were suppressed all week for the same fault. So Friday's description of metals as tracking global prices goes further than Monday and Wednesday could support. **Indian metal shares rose through most of the week and we could not, on any session, tie that to a metal price we trusted.** That disagreement is inside our own record and is reported rather than resolved.

And we explicitly refused to call autos weak on Wednesday. One outlet did; our own 35-name build read plus 0.37% with 20 shares up. The auto index was dark, so neither party can settle it, and no auto direction is asserted for that session. The week's reconstruction has autos down 0.94%, consistent with weakness overall, and it does not adjudicate that one day.

### Technology, and one corner with no reading at all

Nifty IT finished up 2.45%, containing two opposite events: Wednesday's 1.47% fall on the visa pause, which the index badly overstated, and Friday's 3.51% rise, the largest single sector session of the week, fully sourced to the previous night's American close. The second was larger than the first, which is how a sector spends a week going down on domestic-facing news and still finishes up.

Nifty Consumer Durables was among the indices that failed outright on three sessions, producing nothing and not even reconstructable. That has a visible cost: Blue Star closed at a 52-week low inside what looked like the weakest sector of a day, and we could not settle that cleanly. **For one part of the market this week there was neither an index nor a reconstruction, only a hole.**
