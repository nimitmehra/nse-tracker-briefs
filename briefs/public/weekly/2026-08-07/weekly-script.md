## COLD OPEN

Three documents landed on this market in five days.

The one that binds nobody moved the most money.

One line before I start. This is general market commentary, not investment advice. I am not a SEBI-registered Research Analyst.

## 1. THE DRAFT

On Thursday the Reserve Bank published a **draft**.

Not a rule. Not a law. A draft, open for public comment until the 28th of August.

It proposes that lenders who are not banks stop offering revolving credit. That is a limit you repay and can then draw again.

Bajaj Finance fell 5.84% in a single session.

## 2. THE BINDING ONE

Now the one that actually was binding.

On Wednesday the Bank held the repo rate at 5.25%. A fourth straight hold.

Non-bank lenders ran up 9 to 11 percent across the week on it. Bajaj Finance was up 8.67% by Thursday.

One session of the draft took about two-thirds of it back.

The third document was the only final, enforceable one. SEBI's order on Zee. Zee fell 17.77% on the week, and moved nothing else.

## 3. ONE THING, EXPLAINED

Line the three up and the ranking inverts. That looks irrational. It isn't.

Legal force tells you what is binding. It doesn't tell you what is new. Prices move on what is new.

A fourth hold surprised nobody. It was already in every price. A draft that would delete a product line from an industry surprised everybody.

So don't ask first how binding a headline is. Ask what was already assumed, then ask what this changes.

## 4. THE PART THAT DOESN'T HOLD UP

Pricing a draft is not madness. A proposal that would remove a product line costs real money today.

What I can't defend is where the selling went.

ICICI Bank fell 2.50%. Axis Bank fell 1.43%. The draft does not reach them. They are banks, not non-bank lenders, and it would push revolving credit toward exactly their balance sheets.

That is a reflex, not information.

## 5. THE COUNTER-EVIDENCE

On company filings this same market was strict.

Muthoot Finance fell 7.33% on a quarter where profit rose 43%, because a gold-loan lender's profit rises mechanically when gold does.

DLF's profit was up 4.1% and nobody cared, because bookings were down 94%.

So it read company paper for what it says, and official paper for what it might one day become.

## 6. THE WEEK IN NUMBERS

Nifty up 0.77%. Monday's 1.6% was more than the whole of it. Friday down 0.27%.

427 stocks up, 259 down, eight of ten sectors green. Auto best at 9.88% on record July car wholesales. Realty worst, down 5.46%.

The rupee strengthened about half a percent. Brent crude **fell** about 9.4% on the week, and neither side of India's oil chain priced it.
