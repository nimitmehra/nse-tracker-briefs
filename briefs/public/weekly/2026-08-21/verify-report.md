## Punch list

**1. "Margin" is used as jargon and never glossed.** §7, line 69: *"NGL Fine-Chem fell nearly sixteen per cent on Monday, in the last twenty-eight minutes of trading, **because the company explained its own margin**."* And in the next sentence: *"part of the quarter's **margin** improvement came from inventory gains"*. This is the hinge of the single best story in the script and the word is opaque on one hearing. The brief defines revenue, operating profit, operating margin and net profit explicitly (its §4) precisely because the section leans on them; the script kept the story and dropped the definition. *Inventory gains* is correctly glossed in the same sentence — *margin* is not. Fix: one clause at first use, e.g. "explained its own profit margin, how many rupees out of every hundred of sales it keeps."

**2. "Most individual small companies still went down" overstates the brief.** §3, line 31: *"So the small-company index had the best week of the seven, and **most individual small companies** still went down."* The brief's 313-up / 385-down count covers the whole ~700-company screened list, not small companies specifically, and its own sentence reads *"most individual **companies** still went down"* (brief §3). The script narrows the population and the brief does not establish that narrower claim. Delete the word "small".

**3. Oil rises six per cent and the script never says why, or that it cannot say why.** §6, line 57: *"Brent crude... rose from about eighty-nine dollars on Monday to about ninety-four by Friday. Up every single session, about six per cent in a week."* This is the one thread the script says runs through the whole week, and it is the only move reported with neither a cause nor an explicit "we could not establish one" — a rule the script otherwise honours scrupulously (Monday IT, Tuesday, Wednesday's remainder, all of Friday, nine of the movers). The brief supplies the cause in its §5: seaborne premium, the Brent-to-WTI gap around $7.16, the Strait of Hormuz shut since 28 February, eight ship attacks in August, the UAE suspending trade with Iran on 19 August. One sentence closes it.

**4. Runtime has no headroom.** 1,900 spoken words is 11.9 minutes at 160 wpm. Passages like *"24,252"*, *"0.27 per cent... 0.47... 0.50... 0.60"* and *"eleven thousand six hundred crore"* read slower than 160 wpm, so a live take will very likely cross twelve minutes. Trimming roughly 100 words buys the margin; §3's index recital and §8's sector coda are the softest.

**5. Two sentences will not survive being spoken.** §10, line 99, 38 words: *"Friday held the largest single-company move of the week, and the Reserve Bank's weekly reserves release, which we had named in advance as the test of whether the rupee is now being held up out of the reserves."* Two objects hung off one verb with a relative clause on the second. And §7, line 71, 33 words: *"It fell on Wednesday on a headline of profit up 141 per cent, because revenue actually fell, and more than half that profit rise came from businesses it part-owns but does not run."* Split each in two.

**6. Five companies are named but never placed.** A listener who does not follow markets cannot tell what any of these does: **Ipca Laboratories** (line 67 — pharma; the brief supplies branded medicines and exported drug ingredients), **NGL Fine-Chem** (69), **Engineers India** (71 — a state engineering consultancy; the "businesses it part-owns" point needs the listener to know what the main business is), **Menon Bearings** (79), **Ahluwalia Contracts** (81 — a builder; without that, *"a cost problem sitting underneath the sales line"* has nothing to attach to; the brief's fixed-price-contract mechanism is the missing half). Balrampur Chini and Jio Financial Services are adequately placed by context and by name. Two or three words each fixes all five.

**7. Substack is not in the brief.** §11, line 107: *"The full written brief is on Substack, and the audio version is on Spotify."* The brief's own header lists Spotify, Instagram and X, not Substack. Not a market figure, so not a source failure, but it is the only assertion in the script with no backing in the permitted source — confirm the Substack destination is live before recording.

**8. Menon Bearings loses the brief's "cause not established".** §8, line 79 explains the price-band artefact but never says the rise itself is unexplained, which is what the brief records. The blanket line above it (*"Nine of the twenty-two have no cause anywhere in our briefs"*) covers it generically, so this is a nit rather than a defect — but the item reads explanation-shaped when it is not one.

**9. Two numbers are not clean as written.** §7, line 67: *"revenue up 21 per cent and net profit up 72"* — the unit is dropped on the second figure. Same line then says *"profit grows three times as fast as sales"* where the brief says "nearly three times". §3, line 25: *"The Nifty 50 fell 0.47. The Nifty 100 fell 0.50."* — bare decimals with the unit stripped; "nought point five zero" is an awkward thing to say aloud. Also §6's *"about six per cent"* rounds the brief's preferred, reader-checkable 5.8% upward toward the 6.41% figure the brief deliberately set aside; "just under six per cent" is truer to the source.
