## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side.** The Reserve Bank of India held the repo rate at 5.25% on Wednesday, unanimously, a fourth straight hold. The news was in the forecasts, not the rate. It cut this year's inflation projection to 5.0% from 5.1%, crediting cheaper crude, and cut its core inflation forecast 40 basis points to 4.3%, four times the headline cut. Core strips out food and fuel, so cutting it that hard is the Bank saying it reads today's price pressure as supply-driven rather than spreading through the wider economy. It raised growth to 6.7% and sees inflation peaking at 5.9% in the October-to-December quarter.

**The worrying side.** July's services survey, out the same morning, came in at 53.3, a 53-month low, in the part of the economy producing roughly 55% of India's output. A growth forecast raised and services momentum at a four-and-a-half-year low, on one date, is a tension the Bank did not address. The August rain shortfall is the other half of it, in The Connections.

## The Connections

*Behind the session — what moved, what did not, and which industries it points at.*

**The rate signal went to lenders and never reached property.** Wednesday's twin inflation cuts ordinarily help anything whose customers borrow. Non-bank lenders responded across the week: Shriram Finance (SHRIRAMFIN) +11.08%, Cholamandalam Investment and Finance (CHOLAFIN) +9.37%, Bajaj Finance (BAJFINANCE) +8.67%. Developers did the opposite. Macrotech Developers (LODHA) fell 2.88% today and 5.68% on the week, with DLF (DLF), Oberoi Realty (OBEROIRLTY), Prestige Estates (PRESTIGE) and Godrej Properties (GODREJPROP) all lower. Same signal, opposite outcomes, reason not established.

**The Bank's inflation peak and the weather office's rain shortfall point at the same quarter, and nobody has put them in one sentence.** The Reserve Bank sees inflation peaking at 5.9% in October to December. The weather office forecasts below-normal August rainfall and a strengthening El Niño, and a weak second half of the monsoon feeds food prices with a lag into that quarter. Both legs are sourced; the join is ours.

**Cheaper crude has not yet paid the companies it should.** Brent is down about 10% on the week, yet every domestic beneficiary closed lower today: Bharat Petroleum (BPCL) −0.78%, Indian Oil (IOC) −0.93%, Berger Paints (BERGEPAINT) −1.39%, InterGlobe Aviation (INDIGO) −1.51%. All four are higher on the week, so the relief was taken earlier and today was give-back with no established cause. Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) −1.00% is the correctly aligned one: it produces crude rather than consuming it, so a falling oil price hurts it. When three macro forces point one way and the assets that should respond do not, this market is trading company results, not macro.

## Sources & Method

Built from NSE filings read directly, company releases, end-of-day market data, the Reserve Bank's and SEBI's own press-release indexes, and established financial press. Every earnings figure comes from the filing: our screening feed carried the previous quarter's numbers for six of today's reporters and was set aside. A wire report that Firstsource's operating profit fell 45% sequentially contradicts the company's own factsheet, which shows a 7.1% rise, so we do not carry it.

Known gaps, stated rather than hidden. Eleven of thirteen sector indices are unreadable. The bond curve failed on every tenor, so the 10-year is one vendor's number; the GIFT Nifty feed was blocked a fourth run; block-deal data is not wired to this pipeline, which weakens every "cause not established" here. Our gold quote is a futures contract about 1.8% above spot, so we publish direction rather than level. Where a move had no cause we could source, we say so. Where a catalyst is sourced and dated but the size of the reaction is not explained, as with Cantabil and Inventurus today, we say that instead.
