## The Lead

India's headline indices rose on Thursday while two of every three stocks fell. The Nifty 50 closed 0.28% higher at 24,317.15 and the Sensex 0.35% higher at 77,928.15, but 457 stocks declined against only 215 that advanced, the median stock fell 0.60%, and the fear gauge rose on an up day. The split has one main explanation: the market bought the earnings-and-cheaper-crude story and sold the interest-rate story, four sessions before the Reserve Bank of India decides. Anyone who read only the index number read the opposite of what happened.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side was oil, and the shape of it is strange enough to state plainly.** The halt in US-Iran strikes collapsed on the night of 28-29 July. Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at US forces at the Muwaffaq Salti air base and a US Central Command centre in Jordan, all intercepted, and the US retaliated the same day against dozens of Guard sites. Brent jumped 7.9% on that, then gave some back today to $89.66. Across the six days since our last brief crude is down about 8.5% from roughly $98, even as three tankers were claimed hit and Caspian Pipeline Consortium loadings were suspended. An escalating war with a falling oil price is not a contradiction: markets price barrels that fail to arrive, not headlines, and so far the barrels are arriving. Hold that distinction, because it is the whole basis of India's macro relief right now — a strike headline moves little, a confirmed and sustained blockage of physical flow re-prices the import bill, the rupee and next month's inflation. The relief is real for an economy importing about 85% of its oil, and it is contingent rather than structural. Brent is still up 22.96% on the month.

**The pressure side is the rate path, and it hardened on both sides of the world.** The Fed's hold came on a 9-3 vote in which all three dissenters wanted a rate *hike*. That is the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers dissented in the same direction, and the central bank's own year-end projections contain no cut. The US 10-year yield sits at 4.66%. At home, the Reserve Bank meets 3-5 August with the repo rate at 5.25%, and a Reuters poll of 72 economists reads 68 expecting a hold, four expecting a hike and none expecting a cut. The cut trade is dead and the only live tail is hawkish. That asymmetry is what a rising fear gauge on a rising market is pricing.

**A correction to what we told you on 24 July: there is no 1 August tariff deadline.** The question closed on 24 July. The US Trade Representative's final measures under Section 301, its forced-labour investigation, took effect that day and placed India in the lower 10% tier, cut from the 12.5% originally proposed, alongside 16 other economies. India's Commerce Ministry says roughly 45% of exports to the US fall outside the new duty. The same day, the emergency surcharge carrying the identical 10% hit its 150-day statutory limit and lapsed. The rate on Indian goods is unchanged, but the legal basis moved from a time-capped emergency measure to a finding with no expiry date. Exporters have lost the deadline they were planning around, which is worse than "unchanged" sounds.

**One domestic input improved, and one is missing.** The weather office now forecasts August rainfall at 97% of the long-period average, plus or minus 9 percentage points, an improvement on a July it had put below normal, though central India enters a weaker phase. Treat that as reported: the release did not parse on direct fetch and the figures come from carriers of it. Less comfortably, our government-bond feed failed on every instrument for a second session, four sessions before the policy meeting. A single outside source puts the India 10-year at 6.82%, up 3 basis points. That fits the no-cut consensus directionally, but one source is not a measured reading and we are not building a rate call on it.

## The Market

**The internals were the story, and they pointed the other way from the headline.** Against a Nifty up 0.28%, breadth was 215 advances to 457 declines, the median stock fell 0.60%, MidCap fell 0.35% and SmallCap 0.56%. Independent BSE breadth agrees at 1,577 up against 2,476 down. India VIX rose 1.21% to 12.15 — the fear gauge going up while the index went up is unusual, and it says event risk four sessions out is being bought, not sold.

**The mechanical piece was an expiry, but not the one you would guess.** Today was the **BSE Sensex monthly and weekly derivatives expiry**, not an NSE one. NSE monthly contracts now roll on the last Tuesday, which was 28 July. Expiry-day pinning concentrates in the large, index-weighted names, which is why the Sensex (+0.35%) beat the Nifty (+0.28%) while the average stock fell. Index strength sitting on top of a distributing market is not participation.

When the rate path hardens, it reprices duration-sensitive insurers and non-bank lenders before it touches bank margins.

- **Eicher Motors (EICHERMOT, ₹2.2 lakh crore)** — a record quarter. Revenue rose 32% to ₹6,632 crore and net profit 21% to ₹1,463 crore, on Royal Enfield's highest-ever quarterly sales of 332,940 motorcycles, up 27%; the board also approved a ₹1,225 crore plant in Andhra Pradesh. The stock rose 1.72% rather than 5% because profit grew slower than operating profit.

**Three results where the market read the quality of the number, not its size. This is the day's real lesson.**

- **PCBL Chemical (PCBL, ₹12,906 crore)** — profit up 65%, stock down 10.31%. Revenue rose 17% to ₹2,474 crore and net profit 65% to ₹155 crore, which looks excellent. Brokerages then flagged that roughly ₹70 crore of it was a one-off gain on inventory, not operating earnings. Strip that and the profit growth is a fraction of the headline. The market repriced the *quality* of the beat, and this is the cleanest example you will get all quarter of why the composition of a profit number matters more than its size.
- **Hexaware Technologies (HEXT, ₹35,187 crore)** — the same mistake in reverse, and the stock fell 6.91% on it. Net profit did fall 13%. But operating profit *rose*, and the operating margin **expanded 352 basis points** to 13.6%. Every rupee of the profit decline sits below the operating line: other income swung to a ₹501 million loss, a large one-off writeback in the year-ago quarter did not repeat, finance costs rose 57% and tax rose 25%. The genuine soft spot is growth — 6.3% revenue growth in constant currency for a share that had run up 28% in three months. Anyone writing "profit fell 13%, so the stock fell 7%" has the cause and effect wrong.
- **KPIT Technologies (KPITTECH, ₹16,171 crore)** — fell 7.67% on the guidance, not the quarter. Profit fell 32% to ₹117 crore and revenue slipped 2% sequentially, but what broke the share was the forward statement: sudden spending cuts by European carmakers in June led the company to guide to flat sequential revenue for the September quarter. A business whose entire case rests on carmakers' research budgets telling you its European customers are cutting is a change to the story, not to one quarter.

**Two honest silences, both preserved.** **Deepak Fertilisers (DEEPAKFERT)** more than doubled its profit to ₹490 crore on income up 21.6%, filed at 13:21 today, and the stock fell 5.77%. It was already falling before the filing landed and kept falling after, so the result neither started nor stopped the move. We ran the full checklist — pledges, ratings, index events, broker downgrades, block deals — and found nothing. Its earnings call is tomorrow at 4 PM. **Kamdhenu (KAMDHENU)** reported what it called its highest-ever royalty income, franchise volumes and profit, with profit up 34%, and fell 4.99%. We have no sourced reason for either, and we would rather say so than fit a story to the price.

## The Connections

*Behind the session — what triggered what, why, and which groups it points at next.*

**A carmaker expanding margins *through* cost inflation is a read on the whole sector, not one company's good day.** Mahindra & Mahindra absorbed 400-500 basis points of raw-material inflation in autos and 300-400 in farm equipment and still lifted return on equity to 23%. That says the pricing power and mix in Indian autos are strong enough to eat input costs, which is a different and better message than "costs fell so profits rose." The cohort moved with it: Eicher up 1.72% on record Royal Enfield volumes, Exide to a 52-week high, Sona BLW to a 52-week high, Craftsman Automation still rising on a doubled profit. Auto and oil and gas together carried essentially the entire index gain. The test comes on Monday, when monthly sales numbers show whether the demand behind the margins is still there.

**Rate pain arrives in a fixed order, and today you could watch it move through the queue.** With 68 of 72 economists expecting no cut next week and four expecting a hike, money sold the things whose value depends most on the rate path. Property developers went first: realty was the worst group at roughly −2.06%, with Godrej Properties and Prestige Estates down as much as 4%. Insurers and non-bank lenders went next, HDFC Life −2.03% and Shriram Finance −1.68%. Banks barely moved, some rose. The mechanism is duration: a developer's project economics and an insurer's liabilities reprice immediately when the expected rate path shifts, while a bank's lending margin takes quarters to feel it. If the decision on 5 August lands hawkish, this is the order in which it spreads.

**Cheaper oil did not buy paint makers anything, because the constraint isn't the barrel.** Crude derivatives are the dominant input for paint, crude is down about 8.5% in six days, and the textbook says paint margins should expand. Instead the largest maker fell as much as 3% intraday on a 40% profit rise, with the reason given as worry about competitive intensity and input-cost pass-through. The read is that in a market with aggressive new entrants, a cheaper input gets competed away into lower prices rather than kept as margin. So watch the commentary on discounting and market share, not the oil price, if you follow this group.

**The war got hotter and the oil price fell anyway. Understand why, because it is the whole basis of India's macro relief right now.** Missiles at US bases in Jordan, retaliation on dozens of Revolutionary Guard sites, joint strikes in Iraq, three tankers claimed hit, and Black Sea loadings on the Caspian Pipeline Consortium suspended. Yet Brent is roughly 8.5% below where it sat on 24 July. Markets price barrels that fail to arrive, not headlines, and so far the barrels are arriving. That distinction is the single thing to hold onto: a strike headline moves nothing much, a confirmed and sustained blockage of flow re-prices everything, including India's import bill, the rupee and next month's inflation. Cheap-oil relief built on this foundation is contingent, not structural.
