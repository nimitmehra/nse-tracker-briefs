## The Lead

India's headline indices rose on Thursday while two of every three stocks fell. The Nifty 50 closed 0.28% higher at 24,317.15 and the Sensex 0.35% higher at 77,928.15, but 457 stocks declined against only 215 that advanced, the median stock fell 0.60%, and the fear gauge rose on an up day. The split has one main explanation: the market bought the earnings-and-cheaper-crude story and sold the interest-rate story, four sessions before the Reserve Bank of India decides. Anyone who read only the index number read the opposite of what happened.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side was oil, and the shape of it is strange enough to state plainly.** The halt in US-Iran strikes collapsed on the night of 28-29 July. Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at US forces at the Muwaffaq Salti air base and a US Central Command centre in Jordan, all intercepted, and the US retaliated the same day against dozens of Guard sites. Brent jumped 7.9% on that, then gave some back today to $89.66; across the six days since our last brief crude is down about 8.5% from roughly $98. An escalating war with a falling oil price is not a contradiction: markets price barrels that fail to arrive, not headlines, and so far the barrels are arriving. That distinction is the whole basis of India's macro relief, and its fragility — a strike headline moves little, a confirmed and sustained blockage of physical flow re-prices the import bill, the rupee and next month's inflation. Real relief for an economy importing about 85% of its oil, then, but contingent rather than structural. Brent is still up 22.96% on the month.

**The pressure side is the rate path, and it hardened on both sides of the world.** The Fed's hold came on a 9-3 vote in which all three dissenters wanted a rate *hike* — the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers dissented in the same direction — and its own year-end projections contain no cut. The US 10-year yield sits at 4.66%. At home, the Reserve Bank meets 3-5 August with the repo rate at 5.25%, and a Reuters poll of 72 economists reads 68 expecting a hold, four a hike and none a cut. The cut trade is dead and the only live tail is hawkish. That asymmetry is what a rising fear gauge on a rising market is pricing.

**A correction to what we told you on 24 July: there is no 1 August tariff deadline.** The question closed on 24 July, when the US Trade Representative's final Section 301 measures — from its forced-labour investigation — took effect and placed India in the lower 10% tier, cut from the 12.5% originally proposed, alongside 16 other economies. India's Commerce Ministry says roughly 45% of exports to the US fall outside the new duty. The same day, the emergency surcharge carrying the identical 10% hit its 150-day statutory limit and lapsed. The rate on Indian goods is unchanged. What changed is the legal basis — and treat this part as reported, not confirmed: it appears to have moved from a time-capped emergency measure to a finding with no expiry date. The USTR notice returned unreadable on direct fetch, so that reading rests on the Commerce Ministry's statement and trade-law advisories, not on our own read of the text. If it holds, exporters have lost the deadline they were planning around, which is worse than "unchanged" sounds.

**One domestic input improved, and one is missing.** The weather office now forecasts August rainfall at 97% of the long-period average, plus or minus 9 percentage points, an improvement on a July it had put below normal, though central India enters a weaker phase. Treat that as reported: the release did not parse on direct fetch. Less comfortably, our government-bond feed failed on every instrument for a second session, four sessions before the policy meeting. A single outside source puts the India 10-year at 6.82%, up 3 basis points — directionally consistent with the no-cut consensus, but not a measured reading, and we are not building a rate call on it.

## The Market

**The internals were the story, and they pointed the other way from the headline.** Against a Nifty up 0.28%, breadth was 215 advances to 457 declines, the median stock fell 0.60%, MidCap 0.35% and SmallCap 0.56%. Independent BSE breadth agrees at 1,577 up against 2,476 down. India VIX rose 1.21% to 12.15 — the fear gauge rising with the index is unusual, and it says event risk four sessions out is being bought, not sold.

**The mechanical piece was an expiry, but not the one you would guess.** Today was the **BSE Sensex monthly and weekly derivatives expiry**, not an NSE one. NSE monthly contracts now roll on the last Tuesday, which was 28 July. Expiry-day pinning concentrates in the large, index-weighted names, which is why the Sensex (+0.35%) beat the Nifty (+0.28%) while the average stock fell. Index strength sitting on top of a distributing market is not participation.

**Financials split rather than slumped.** Bank Nifty's −0.10% hides the divide: State Bank of India +1.14% and HDFC Bank +0.77% held up, while HDFC Life fell 2.03%, Shriram Finance 1.68%, SBI Life 0.92% and Bajaj Finserv 0.78%. That ordering is not random, and The Connections explains why.

*A data honesty note on sectors: only two Nifty sector indices returned a valid one-day reading today — **IT +0.23%** and **Pharma −0.10%**. Nine others had no usable value and are not reported as anything, least of all as flat. The two sector figures quoted below, **Auto +1.63%** and **Realty −2.06%**, come from close reports and constituent aggregation, not from the index feed.*

## Earnings

**Read the timestamps before you read the reactions.** Four large companies filed their June-quarter results in the final 8 to 21 minutes of the session — Vedanta at 15:09, Bajaj Finance at 15:19, Hyundai at 15:20 and Mankind at 15:21, against a 15:30 close. Their results cannot explain their share moves today. What those numbers do to the price happens tomorrow, and that is the most useful thing to take from tonight.

The day's slate, biggest companies first:

- **Bajaj Finance (BAJFINANCE, market value ₹6.6 lakh crore)** — profit up 27.6% to ₹6,080.60 crore, revenue up 19.58% to ₹23,165.45 crore, assets under management past ₹5.47 lakh crore, bad loans improving to 1.20% from 1.28%. Closed flat at −0.09%, but that is a pre-result reading: it filed eleven minutes before the bell, and today's price moved with the non-bank lending pack being sold ahead of the rate meeting.
- **Adani Enterprises (ADANIENT, ₹4.1 lakh crore)** — a headline loss the market treated as a one-off: an attributable net loss of ₹1,160 crore against a ₹885 crore profit, on revenue up 49.9% to ₹32,924 crore, caused by a one-time ₹2,644 crore settlement with the US Office of Foreign Assets Control agreed 14 May. Strip it and the quarter is profitable. The stock rose 0.81%.
- **Mahindra & Mahindra (M&M, ₹4.1 lakh crore)** — the engine of the index, and the reason is better than the headline. Profit rose about 34% to ₹5,455 crore on revenue up about 27% to ₹58,188 crore, with return on equity at 23% against 18.1% two years ago — while absorbing 400-500 basis points of raw-material inflation in autos and 300-400 in farm equipment. Expanding returns *through* cost pressure, rather than because of relief, is a read for the whole sector; auto and oil and gas together carried essentially the entire index gain. Filed 12:58, so the +1.92% is a genuine reaction.
- **Asian Paints (ASIANPAINT, ₹2.6 lakh crore)** — the most instructive small move of the day. Sales rose 17.9% to ₹10,521 crore and profit 40% to ₹1,539 crore, and the stock still fell as much as 3% intraday before closing −0.44%, with crude — its dominant input — down about 8.5% in a week. **Business Standard's close report** attributes the fall to worry about margin pressure from input costs and competitive intensity, with desks split: Nomura kept a Buy and raised its target to ₹3,750 from ₹3,600, Motilal Oswal stayed Neutral at ₹3,050. The binding constraint is pricing, not the barrel — with aggressive new entrants, a cheaper input gets competed away rather than kept as margin.
- **Eicher Motors (EICHERMOT, ₹2.2 lakh crore)** — a record quarter: revenue up 32% to ₹6,632 crore, profit up 21% to ₹1,463 crore, Royal Enfield's highest-ever quarterly sales of 332,940 motorcycles, up 27%, and a ₹1,225 crore Andhra Pradesh plant approved. Up 1.72% rather than 5% because profit grew slower than operating profit.
- **Dabur India (DABUR, ₹75,632 crore)** — double-digit growth was not enough. Profit rose 15% to ₹586.16 crore on revenue up 10.6% to ₹3,764.39 crore, and the stock fell as much as 4% intraday before closing −1.75%; **Business Standard** reports a brokerage trimmed its target after the print. The market is asking whether the rate sustains, not whether it happened.
- **Star Health & Allied Insurance (STARHEALTH, ₹35,924 crore)** — the best-quality number in financials today. Underwriting profit swung to ₹111 crore from ₹16 crore, gross written premium rose 19% to ₹4,287 crore and profit 25% to ₹550 crore. An insurer earning from underwriting rather than from its float is a change in kind, and it rose 1.93% while the rest of the insurance complex was sold.

**Three results where the market read the quality of the number, not its size. This is the day's real lesson.**

- **PCBL Chemical (PCBL, ₹12,906 crore)** — profit up 65%, stock down 10.31%. Revenue rose 17% to ₹2,474 crore and net profit 65% to ₹155 crore, which looks excellent. **Business Today, citing brokerage commentary, then reported** that roughly ₹70 crore of it was a one-off inventory gain, not operating earnings. Strip that and the growth is a fraction of the headline. The market repriced the *quality* of the beat — the cleanest example you will get all quarter of why the composition of a profit number matters more than its size.
- **Hexaware Technologies (HEXT, ₹35,187 crore)** — the same mistake in reverse, and the stock fell 6.91% on it. Net profit did fall 13%, but operating profit *rose* and the operating margin **expanded 352 basis points** to 13.6%. Every rupee of the decline sits below the operating line: other income swung to a ₹501 million loss, a large year-ago writeback did not repeat, finance costs rose 57% and tax 25%. The genuine soft spot is growth — 6.3% in constant currency, for a share that had run up 28% in three months. Anyone writing "profit fell 13%, so the stock fell 7%" has the cause and effect wrong.
- **KPIT Technologies (KPITTECH, ₹16,171 crore)** — fell 7.67% on the guidance, not the quarter. Profit fell 32% to ₹117 crore and revenue slipped 2% sequentially, but what broke the share was the forward statement: sudden June spending cuts by European carmakers led it to guide to flat sequential revenue for the September quarter. A business whose case rests on carmakers' research budgets telling you its European customers are cutting is a change to the story, not to one quarter.

**Two honest silences, both preserved.** **Deepak Fertilisers (DEEPAKFERT)** more than doubled profit to ₹490 crore on income up 21.6%, filed 13:21 today, and fell 5.77% — already falling before the filing landed and still falling after, so the result neither started nor stopped the move. We ran the full checklist — pledges, ratings, index events, broker downgrades, block deals — and found nothing; its call is tomorrow at 4 PM. **Kamdhenu (KAMDHENU)** reported what it called its highest-ever royalty income, franchise volumes and profit, with profit up 34%, and fell 4.99%. We have no sourced reason for either, and would rather say so than fit a story to the price.

*The three late filers, for the record. Vedanta posted record quarterly profit of ₹5,294 crore, up 152%, on revenue up 51%. Hyundai Motor India's profit fell 35% to ₹889 crore on flat revenue, with operating margin down 400 basis points to 9.3%; its +1.27% was auto-sector strength, and it in fact lagged that sector. Mankind Pharma's figures conflict across sources tonight — the versions differ in direction, not just size — so we print none.*

## The Connections

*Behind the session — what triggered what, why, and which groups it points at next.*

**Rate pain arrives in a fixed order, and today you could watch it move through the queue.** With 68 of 72 economists expecting no cut next week and four expecting a hike, money sold what depends most on the rate path — and it sold in sequence. Property developers went first, realty the worst group of the day. Insurers and non-bank lenders went next. Banks barely moved, and some rose. The mechanism is duration: a developer's project economics and an insurer's liabilities reprice the moment the expected rate path shifts, while a bank's lending margin takes quarters to feel it. Star Health is the instructive exception — it rose into that selling on an underwriting number good enough to override its own sector's direction, which is what a genuinely stock-specific result looks like.

**If 5 August lands hawkish, this is the order in which it spreads: developers, then insurers and non-bank lenders, then banks.** That ordering is the most useful thing to carry out of today — it tells you where to look first, and what a delayed reaction in banks does and does not mean.

## What to Watch Tomorrow

- **Reaction to the four results that landed at the buzzer** — Vedanta (profit up 152%), Bajaj Finance (profit up 27.6%), Hyundai (profit down 35%) and Mankind. None of them had time to trade today. Friday is the first real reading.
- **Bank of Japan decision and Governor Ueda's press conference (Friday)** — consensus is a hold at 1.00%. It matters because the yen jumped roughly 3% this evening with no economic trigger, which traders attribute to Japanese official intervention, though nothing is confirmed. A hawkish Bank of Japan on top of intervention is the classic setup for an unwind of borrowed-yen positions, which historically pulls money out of emerging markets including India.
- **RBI weekly reserves data, Friday around 5 PM** — the first read covering the Iran escalation and the rupee's recovery. A large drop would suggest the central bank spent dollars; last week reserves rose $1.08 billion to $676.24 billion.
- **Friday, India** — April-June fiscal accounts and the June eight-core-industries index, neither released yet; Deepak Fertilisers and Vardhman Textiles earnings calls at 4 PM, the two falls we could not explain today.
- **Overnight from the US** — Wall Street rallied hard after the Indian close and emerging-market funds jumped over 3%. Tomorrow's opening context, not today's cause.
- **RBI rate decision, Wednesday 5 August** (meeting 3-5 August) — repo at 5.25%, 68 of 72 economists expecting a hold, four a hike, none a cut. The single biggest scheduled domestic event, and the risk is one-sided.
- **Monthly auto sales, Monday 3 August** — the follow-through test on today's auto strength. **US jobs report, Friday 7 August** — the first read for a Fed that now has three members voting for a hike.
- **Rolling: Iran, Hormuz and the Black Sea pipeline** — a confirmed stoppage of physical flow, as opposed to another strike headline, is what re-prices crude. Dividend record dates: Balkrishna 4 August, Steelcast 7 August.
