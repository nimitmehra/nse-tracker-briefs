## The Lead

Thursday's rise on the Indian market was made in Washington. The US Treasury said on Wednesday it will at least double its buybacks of long-dated government bonds, from $2 billion to a minimum of $4 billion an operation, and long-term American yields fell the same session: the 10-year by 5.7 basis points to 4.647%, the 30-year by 9 to 5.196%. Asia rallied on Thursday and India rallied least. The Nifty 50 rose 0.64% to 24,231.85, ending a seven-session losing run, while Korea's Kospi gained somewhere between 5.5% and 6%. India came last because Brent crude rose a fifth straight session, to $93.87.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side, and what it is actually worth.** The Treasury's buybacks are repurchases of older, thinly traded government bonds, meant to relieve congestion at the long end of the American bond market. They are not the Federal Reserve, they are not new money and they are not stimulus. The enlarged operations do not begin until 9 September and run to early November. India rose on an announcement whose first effect is three weeks away — and one report has the 30-year yield's fall already reversing during American trading hours, after India had closed. That is a single source rather than a confirmed fact, but it is the right thing to watch.

**The worrying side, and it is domestic.** The Reserve Bank published the minutes of its 3–5 August rate meeting on 19 August, and they are hawkish. Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta records that no further easing is possible and that a case for a rate *increase* may emerge in 2026-27, with inflation peaking at 5.9% in the October–December quarter. The policy rate is unchanged at 5.25%, the stance neutral, and that August hold was unanimous. Crude feeds the same quarter the Deputy Governor is worried about: Brent has now risen five sessions running, the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively shut since 28 February, and eight attacks on ships passing through it have been reported in August alone. *(The minutes were read through four independent carriers rather than off the Reserve Bank's own document.)*

**Watch next.** Friday brings the Reserve Bank's weekly statistics, which is where the question of what is actually holding the rupee up gets its next answer. The fuller calendar is at the end of this brief.

## Regulatory

- **The market regulator made it easier for foreign funds to open an account here, and this is the fourth such change in a month.** SEBI said on 20 August, effective the same day, that a foreign portfolio investor may sign the power of attorney it gives its Indian custodian — the document that lets the custodian act for it — using a digital signature. That takes notarisation and overseas certification out of the onboarding chain. Set it beside three earlier changes: the new closing auction session, derivatives on a foreign-investor index launched on 12 August, and relaxed identity checks for individuals living abroad on 14 August. Four accommodations in one month, against foreign ownership of Indian shares at a 14-year low of 14.7%. The plumbing keeps arriving ahead of the money, and nobody has counted the four.
- **SEBI's annual study of equity derivatives landed around 21:00, after the close, so it moved nothing on Thursday.** In the year to March 2026, 87.7% of individual traders lost money, with net losses of about ₹91,685 crore against roughly ₹1.12 lakh crore the year before. On the participation measure the release leads with, unique active traders fell 18% from a year earlier to 87.5 lakh — the first contraction in a decade. A second, narrower measure inside the same document gives a different count on a different basis; the two are not interchangeable, so only the first is used here. The read is uncomfortable: losses fell because fewer people traded, not because more of them won. The loss rate stayed near 88%.

## The Market

**The best statistic of the day was the absence of falls.** After seven consecutive down sessions, 57 stocks in our screened universe of roughly 700 rose more than 3% and not one fell more than 5%. Advances beat declines by about three and a half to one. A market that stops going down is a different thing from a market being bought, and Thursday was the first of those.

**Now what we cannot tell you: which sectors led.** Nine of the eleven Nifty sector indices returned no usable one-day move on our feed. Only two are measurable — Nifty IT +0.79% and Nifty Pharma +0.39%. A widely carried close report described the session as led by realty, private bank and technology shares. We cannot corroborate a realty or a private-bank one-day figure from any source we trust, so we do not adopt that ranking, and no leaderboard can honestly be built from two readings. Technology's rise does sit with the day's cause, the same fall in long-term global yields that lifted the index, and with the rupee barely moved, currency arithmetic is not the explanation.

**The banks lagged, and that is the day's most interesting number.** Nifty Bank rose 0.45% against the Nifty's 0.64%, on a session whose entire cause was collapsing long-term bond yields. Rate-sensitive shares underperforming a rate-relief rally is backwards. The explanation is in The Connections below.

**And the fear gauge fell again, but not to a record.** India VIX ended at 10.755, down 4.99%, its lowest in weeks. Our own data shows the 52-week low equal to Thursday's close, which would make this a one-year low, and we are not publishing that. Two independent vendors put the 52-week range at 8.72 to 28.91 against our 10.755 to 27.89. Both of our bounds sit inside theirs, which is what a shortened lookback window looks like rather than a new low. Three sources also disagree on the level itself, at 10.57, 10.69 and 10.755. The fall is real; the superlative is not.

## Corporate News

- **Indo Borax & Chemicals (INDOBORAX) is buying control of a listed peer, and it told the exchange while the market was still open.** The board approved an agreement, jointly with promoter company Zenrock Chemicals, to buy 2,38,44,000 shares — 64.26% — of Kronox Lab Sciences (KRONOX) for ₹246.12 crore, at ₹103.22 a share. That triggers a compulsory offer to Kronox's public shareholders for up to a further 25.79%, at ₹157.27. The filings went out at 12:15 and 12:17, three hours before the close, which is why this is the cleanest attribution on the board today. The number worth a second look is the gap between those two prices: the public offer is about 52% above what the sellers negotiated. That is the takeover code doing its job when the market price sits well above the deal price, and it is why both the buyer and the target rose. Note what the filing does not disclose — how the purchase is funded, and what it does to earnings.

  *(Two per-share figures are circulating and they mean different things. ₹103.22 is the negotiated price; a separate ₹105.87 adds in consultancy fees the sellers receive. This brief uses ₹103.22.)*

## Big Movers

### Gainers — what worked Thursday (10 names, +3% or more)

- **Balrampur Chini Mills (BALRAMCHIN) +17.87% to ₹767.10, ₹15,498 crore** — the day's biggest move, and it belongs to the sugar industry rather than to this company. On 19 August the Department of Food and Public Distribution ordered that any bulk buyer using more than 10 tonnes of sugar a month as a raw material may hold no more than 15 days of stock, in force from 1 September to 30 November. Confectioners, soft-drink makers, food processors and institutional buyers are covered; government bodies are exempt. Sugar shares rose up to 18% across the board, Bannari Amman Sugars +14.78% and Dwarikesh Sugar +13.63% among them, with Dalmia Bharat Sugar, Avadh Sugar and Dhampur Sugar closing at 52-week highs.

  Read the mechanism the right way round, because much of the coverage will not. This is an order designed to *cool* sugar prices and it binds buyers, not mills. It lifted the shares because it is the government's own confirmation of how far the physical price has run: spot sugar touched ₹5,530 a quintal on 19 August, reported as a 16-year high. The cohort was already in its third rising session before the order landed. An intraday figure of ₹744 is circulating as a record; the ₹767.10 close supersedes it.
- **Indo Borax & Chemicals (INDOBORAX) +14.82% to ₹498.15, ₹1,557 crore** — see Corporate News. One line belongs here and not there: the *buyer* rose 15% on announcing a ₹246 crore cash purchase plus a compulsory offer that could cost a good deal more. Buyers do not usually get that reception.
- **Suven Life Sciences (SUVEN) +12.34% to ₹373.60, ₹10,412 crore — no cause established**, by either of our two independent searches. No filing, no order, no rating change, no dated analyst event. The name itself is a trap: this is Suven Life Sciences, the drug developer, and not the contract manufacturer once called Suven Pharmaceuticals that now trades as Cohance. The one obvious candidate, a trial safety review, is dated June with full results guided to 2027, so it is refused on date. The move is published unattributed.
- **Genus Power Infrastructures (GENUSPOWER) +8.07% to ₹334.25, ₹9,267 crore — no cause established, and a false one killed.** A first search returned, confidently, that Genus had won three orders worth over ₹3,000 crore. The company has filed nothing with the exchange since 14 August. Its results came out on 13 August and the stock is only 9.04% higher on the week, so Thursday's 8.07% is essentially the entire weekly move arriving in one session, seven days late. That is not a reaction to results.
- **Netweb Technologies India (NETWEB) +7.00% to ₹5,419.80, ₹31,262 crore — no cause established for Thursday, and this corrects our own earlier working assumption.** The ₹1,200 crore share sale to institutions is real: 25,05,219 shares were allotted at ₹4,790, which is 1.96% *below* the floor price of ₹4,885.90, not above the band as we had it. A 29-fold jump in trading with nothing to explain it is a finding rather than a gap. Our large-deal feed, which is where the answer would most plausibly sit, is not wired in at all.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**Global rate relief stopped at India's border, and the reason is the Reserve Bank's own text.** Thursday's entire cause was long-term American yields falling hard, which ought to be best news of all for lenders. Instead Nifty Bank rose 0.45% against the market's 0.64%. The domestic rate story ran the opposite way inside the same window. The minutes published on 19 August carry a Deputy Governor's explicit case for a rate increase, and the central bank spent the week pulling cash out of the banking system, running overnight absorption auctions twice in one day on both the 18th and the 20th before extending them across the weekend. The bond market said the same thing from its own side — the emerging-market local-currency bond index in which India sits fell 0.35% on the day American bonds rallied. Relief from Washington does not travel into a curve whose own central bank has just argued for higher rates.

**Two sets of central-bank minutes were published seven hours apart, and both are arguing about rate rises.** The Reserve Bank's landed around 17:00 India time on 19 August. The US Federal Reserve's minutes of its 28–29 July meeting followed at about 23:30, recording a 9–3 hold in which three regional presidents dissented in favour of a *higher* rate — the most divided Fed vote in years. Nobody has set the two documents side by side. For anyone holding assets priced off a discount rate — lenders, housing finance, expensive growth shares — both curves are now debating the same direction, and it is not downwards.

**The order that made sugar the strongest corner of the market is designed to bring sugar prices down, and it expires.** The stockholding limit binds buyers for three months and lapses on 30 November, at the end of the festive season. What actually moved was the physical price, not the notification. Anyone following this should be watching the spot quote — ₹5,530 a quintal on 19 August — and the 30 November date, rather than the announcement that confirmed the run.

**Eight of the ten biggest risers have no cause anyone can point to, and that is a statement about the market rather than about our searching.** Between two independent lanes we ran fifteen separate checks and killed three stories that would have shipped as reasons on a shallower pass, all of them wrongly dated. The single feed most likely to close the gap — the record of large block trades — is one we do not have, and MMTC trading at twenty-nine times its normal volume with nothing attached is where that hole shows. Until it is wired in, "nobody knows" is the accurate answer, and it is a better one than something plausible.

## What to Watch Tomorrow

The last two weeks published were builds in foreign-exchange reserves of more than $5 billion each, to about $707 billion — the opposite of what heavy dollar-selling to defend the rupee would look like. ## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own press-release pages read directly, the US Treasury's and the Federal Reserve's own releases, end-of-day market data from two independent feeds, and established Indian financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim.

**The timing rule, stated once because it decided two stories tonight.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35. A document published after that cannot explain that day's close. It removed the regulator's derivatives study, out at about 21:00, and Netweb's share-sale allotment, disclosed on 21 August, from consideration as causes.

**This brief covers Thursday and was written on Friday**, roughly fifteen hours late. Everything dated 21 August is labelled as such and appears only as forward-looking context, never as Thursday's news.

**Cause not established is a result, not a gap.** Eight of the ten biggest risers carry it, after fifteen separate checks across two independent lanes. Three stories were killed on their dates: a Genus Power order win that traced to 2024, a trial catalyst for Suven dated June, and a GST rate meeting reported for this week that turned out to be 2025 material.

**What we could not see.** Our global index and currency data arrived stamped a day forward and was replaced by hand. The government bond curve failed for a twentieth consecutive run, so this brief carries no Indian yield. GIFT Nifty was blocked for a seventh. The results scan returned nothing priced against five reporters. Large-deal data is not wired in at all, which weakens every "cause not established" above — that is a hole, not a clean negative.
