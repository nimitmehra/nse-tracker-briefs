## The Lead

Thursday's rise on the Indian market was made in Washington. The US Treasury said on Wednesday it will at least double its buybacks of long-dated government bonds, from $2 billion to a minimum of $4 billion an operation, and long-term American yields fell the same session: the 10-year by 5.7 basis points to 4.647%, the 30-year by 9 to 5.196%. Asia rallied on Thursday and India rallied least. The Nifty 50 rose 0.64% to 24,231.85, ending a seven-session losing run, while Korea's Kospi gained somewhere between 5.5% and 6%. India came last because Brent crude rose a fifth straight session, to $93.87.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side, and what it is worth.** The Treasury's buybacks are repurchases of older, thinly traded government bonds, meant to relieve congestion at the long end of the American bond market. They are not the Federal Reserve, they are not new money and they are not stimulus, and the enlarged operations do not begin until 9 September. India rose on an announcement whose first effect is three weeks away, and one report has the 30-year yield's fall already reversing during American hours, after India had closed. That is a single source, but it is the right thing to watch.

**The worrying side, and it is domestic.** The Reserve Bank published the minutes of its 3–5 August rate meeting on 19 August, and they are hawkish. Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta records that no further easing is possible and that a case for a rate *increase* may emerge in 2026-27, with inflation peaking at 5.9% in the October–December quarter. The rate itself is unchanged at 5.25%, the stance neutral, and that August hold was unanimous. Crude feeds the same quarter: Brent has risen five sessions running, the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively shut since 28 February, and eight attacks on ships passing through it have been reported in August alone. *(Minutes read through four independent carriers, not off the Reserve Bank's own document.)*

**Watch next.** Friday's weekly Reserve Bank statistics are where the question of what is actually holding the rupee up gets its next answer.

## Regulatory

- **The market regulator made it easier for foreign funds to open an account here, and this is the fourth such change in a month.** SEBI said on 20 August, effective the same day, that a foreign portfolio investor may sign the power of attorney it gives its Indian custodian — the document that lets the custodian act for it — using a digital signature, which takes notarisation and overseas certification out of onboarding. Set it beside the new closing auction session, derivatives on a foreign-investor index launched on 12 August, and relaxed identity checks for individuals living abroad on 14 August. Four accommodations in one month, against foreign ownership of Indian shares at a 14-year low of 14.7%. The plumbing keeps arriving ahead of the money.
- **SEBI's annual study of equity derivatives landed around 21:00, after the close, so it moved nothing on Thursday.** In the year to March 2026, 87.7% of individual traders lost money, with net losses of about ₹91,685 crore against roughly ₹1.12 lakh crore the year before. On the measure the release leads with, unique active traders fell 18% from a year earlier to 87.5 lakh, the first contraction in a decade; a second, narrower measure inside the same document counts on a different basis and is not used here. Losses fell because fewer people traded, not because more of them won. The loss rate stayed near 88%.

## Corporate News

- **Indo Borax & Chemicals (INDOBORAX) is buying control of a listed peer, and it told the exchange while the market was still open.** The board approved an agreement, jointly with promoter company Zenrock Chemicals, to buy 2,38,44,000 shares — 64.26% — of Kronox Lab Sciences (KRONOX) for ₹246.12 crore at ₹103.22 a share. ## Big Movers

### Gainers — what worked Thursday (10 names, +3% or more)

- **Balrampur Chini Mills (BALRAMCHIN) +17.87% to ₹767.10** — the biggest move of the day, and it belongs to the sugar industry rather than to this company. On 19 August the Department of Food and Public Distribution ordered that any bulk buyer using more than 10 tonnes of sugar a month may hold no more than 15 days of stock, in force from 1 September to 30 November; confectioners, soft-drink makers, food processors and institutional buyers are covered, government bodies exempt. Sugar shares rose up to 18% across the industry, several mills closing at 52-week highs.

  Read the mechanism the right way round, because much of the coverage will not. This order is designed to *cool* sugar prices and it binds buyers, not mills. It lifted the shares because it is the government's own confirmation of how far the physical price has run — spot sugar touched ₹5,530 a quintal on 19 August, reported as a 16-year high — and the industry was already in its third rising session before the order landed. An intraday figure of ₹744 is circulating as a record; the ₹767.10 close supersedes it.
- **Indo Borax & Chemicals (INDOBORAX) +14.82% to ₹498.15** — see Corporate News. One line belongs here: the *buyer* rose 15% on announcing a purchase plus an open offer that could cost a good deal more. Buyers do not usually get that reception.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**Two sets of central-bank minutes were published seven hours apart, and both are arguing about rate rises.** The Reserve Bank's landed around 17:00 India time on 19 August, with a Deputy Governor's explicit case for an increase inside them. The US Federal Reserve's minutes of its 28–29 July meeting followed at about 23:30, recording a 9–3 hold in which three regional presidents dissented in favour of a *higher* rate — the most divided Fed vote in years. Nobody has set the two documents side by side, and they sit oddly against Thursday itself, which rose on falling long-term American yields. That relief came from a debt-management operation, not from a change in the rate path. For anyone holding assets priced off a discount rate — lenders, housing finance, expensive growth shares — both curves are now debating the same direction, and it is not downwards.

**The order that made sugar the strongest corner of the market is designed to bring sugar prices down, and it expires.** The stockholding limit binds buyers for three months and lapses on 30 November, at the end of the festive season. What actually moved was the physical price, not the notification. Anyone following this should be watching the spot quote and the 30 November date rather than the announcement that confirmed the run.

**Eight of the ten biggest risers, and the only big faller, have no cause anyone can point to.** Across two independent lanes we ran fifteen separate checks and killed three stories that would have shipped as reasons on a shallower pass, all of them wrongly dated. The single feed most likely to close the gap — the record of large block trades — is one we do not have, and MMTC trading at twenty-nine times its normal volume with nothing attached is where that hole shows. "Nobody knows" is the accurate answer, and it is a better one than something plausible.

## What to Watch Tomorrow

- **Friday 21 August, the Reserve Bank's weekly statistics, about 17:00**, covering the week to 14 August. The last two published weeks were builds in foreign-exchange reserves of more than $5 billion each, to about $707 billion. Reserves rise and fall for several reasons, including the revaluation of assets the Reserve Bank already holds, so no single week settles the question — but heavy dollar-selling to defend the rupee would tend to draw reserves down, and two builds in a row do not look like that.
- **Friday 21 August** — the central bank extends its cash-draining auction to three days across the weekend, and runs an underwriting auction for ₹28,000 crore of government bonds.
- **Tuesday 25 August** — monthly derivatives expiry on the NSE, on the last-Tuesday convention. The first one since the regulator published evidence that retail participation in derivatives is shrinking.
- **Wednesday 26 August** — Jio Financial Services (JIOFIN) holds its third annual general meeting at 14:00, by video. 19 August was the cut-off for voting eligibility; no deadline for casting a vote has been established and none should be assumed.
- **Friday 28 August** — comments close on the Reserve Bank's draft rules for non-bank lenders. Still the most consequential unpriced date on the calendar: whether the restriction survives comment, whether the credit-card carve-out widens, and whether a transition period appears are all open.
- **Monday 31 August** — the MSCI August review takes effect at the close, with four additions and three removals published in MSCI's own review document. Funds that track the index have to trade at that close whatever they think of it. We hold no estimate of how much money that is and are not inventing one.
- **Tuesday 1 September** — the sugar stockholding limit takes effect and runs to 30 November, and CEAT's methanol licence suspension window opens, running to 15 September.
- **Wednesday 9 September** — the US Treasury buybacks that caused Thursday's rally actually begin, and run to early November.
- **5–7 October** — the Reserve Bank's next rate-setting meeting, which the August minutes have made considerably more interesting than it looked a week ago.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own press-release pages, the US Treasury's and the Federal Reserve's own releases, end-of-day data from two independent market feeds, and established Indian financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim.

**The timing rule, stated once because it decided two stories.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35. A document published after that cannot explain that day's close. It removed the regulator's derivatives study, out around 21:00, and Netweb's share-sale allotment, disclosed on 21 August, from consideration as causes. This brief covers Thursday and was written on Friday, roughly fifteen hours late; anything dated 21 August is labelled and appears only as forward context.

**Cause not established is a result, not a gap.** Eight of the ten biggest risers and the day's only big faller carry it, after fifteen checks across two independent lanes. Three stories were killed on their dates: a Genus Power order win that traced to 2024, a trial catalyst for Suven dated June, and a GST rate meeting reported for this week that turned out to be 2025 material.

**The three withheld numbers, and one correction to ourselves.** No India 10-year yield: our bond curve failed for a twentieth consecutive run, and the only figure available is one vendor's ~6.84% for 19 August, which is second-hand and dated. No year-to-date figures: our feed's year-start base shifted between the 18 and 20 August runs, moving the Nifty's reading 1.2 percentage points on a 0.3% price gain, so at least one of them is wrong. No sector ranking: nine of eleven sector indices returned nothing usable, and two readings are not a ranking. The correction is HSCL — our own mover screen reported zero fallers, which was a description of what its filters passed through rather than of the market, and the stock is restored above.

**What else we could not see.** Global index and currency data arrived stamped a day forward and was replaced by hand. GIFT Nifty was blocked for a seventh run. The results scan returned nothing priced against five reporters. Large-deal data is not wired in at all, which weakens every "cause not established" above — that is a hole, not a clean negative.
