## The Lead

Thursday's rise on the Indian market was made in Washington. The US Treasury said on Wednesday it will at least double its buybacks of long-dated government bonds, from $2 billion to a minimum of $4 billion an operation, and long-term American yields fell the same session: the 10-year by 5.7 basis points to 4.647%, the 30-year by 9 to 5.196%. Asia rallied on Thursday and India rallied least. The Nifty 50 rose 0.64% to 24,231.85, ending a seven-session losing run, while Korea's Kospi gained somewhere between 5.5% and 6%. India came last because Brent crude rose a fifth straight session, to $93.87.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side, and what it is worth.** Those buybacks are repurchases of older, thinly traded bonds meant to relieve congestion at the long end. They are not the Federal Reserve, not new money and not stimulus, and the enlarged operations do not begin until 9 September. So India rose on an announcement whose first effect is three weeks away — and one report has the 30-year yield's fall already reversing during American hours, after India had closed. Single source, but the right thing to watch.

**The worrying side, and it is domestic.** The Reserve Bank published the minutes of its 3–5 August meeting on 19 August, and they are hawkish. Deputy Governor Poonam Gupta records that no further easing is possible and that a case for a rate *increase* may emerge in 2026-27, with inflation peaking at 5.9% in the October–December quarter. The rate itself is unchanged at 5.25%, the stance neutral, that hold unanimous. Crude feeds the same quarter: Brent has risen five sessions running, the Strait of Hormuz has been effectively shut since 28 February, and eight attacks on ships have been reported in August. *(Minutes read through four independent carriers, not off the Reserve Bank's own document.)*

## Regulatory

- **The market regulator made it easier for foreign funds to open an account here, and this is the fourth such change in a month.** From 20 August a foreign portfolio investor may sign the power of attorney it gives its Indian custodian, the document that lets the custodian act for it, with a digital signature. Notarisation and overseas certification drop out of onboarding. Set it beside the new closing auction session, derivatives on a foreign-investor index launched on 12 August, and relaxed identity checks for individuals living abroad on 14 August. Four accommodations in a month, against foreign ownership of Indian shares at a 14-year low of 14.7%. The plumbing keeps arriving ahead of the money.
- **SEBI's annual derivatives study landed around 21:00, after the close, so it moved nothing on Thursday.** In the year to March 2026, 87.7% of individual traders lost money, ₹91,685 crore net against about ₹1.12 lakh crore the year before. On the measure the release leads with, unique active traders fell 18% to 87.5 lakh, the first contraction in a decade; a second, narrower measure inside the same document counts differently and is not used here. Losses fell because fewer people traded, not because more of them won.

## Corporate News

- **Indo Borax & Chemicals (INDOBORAX) is buying control of a listed peer, and told the exchange while the market was still open.** The board approved an agreement, jointly with promoter company Zenrock Chemicals, to buy 2,38,44,000 shares — 64.26% — of Kronox Lab Sciences (KRONOX) for ₹246.12 crore at ₹103.22 a share, which triggers a compulsory offer to Kronox's public shareholders for up to 25.79% more at ₹157.27. The filings went out at 12:15 and 12:17, three hours before the close. The gap between the two prices is the thing: the public offer sits about 52% above what the sellers negotiated, which is the takeover code doing its job when the market price is well above the deal price, and it is why both companies rose. The filing does not disclose how the purchase is funded or what it does to earnings. *(A second per-share figure of ₹105.87 also circulates; it adds consultancy fees the sellers receive.)*

## Big Movers

### Gainers — what worked Thursday (10 names, +3% or more)

- **Balrampur Chini Mills (BALRAMCHIN) +17.87% to ₹767.10** — the day's biggest move, and it belongs to the sugar industry rather than to this company. On 19 August the Department of Food and Public Distribution capped bulk buyers using more than 10 tonnes of sugar a month at 15 days of stock, in force 1 September to 30 November. Sugar shares rose up to 18% across the industry. Read it the right way round: the order is designed to *cool* sugar prices and it binds buyers, not mills. It lifted the shares because it is the government's own confirmation of how far the physical price has run — spot sugar touched ₹5,530 a quintal on 19 August, reported as a 16-year high — and the industry was already in its third rising session before the order landed. An intraday ₹744 is circulating as a record; the ₹767.10 close supersedes it.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**Two sets of central-bank minutes were published seven hours apart, and both are arguing about rate rises.** The Reserve Bank's landed around 17:00 India time on 19 August, carrying a Deputy Governor's explicit case for an increase. The US Federal Reserve's minutes of its 28–29 July meeting followed about 23:30, recording a 9–3 hold in which three regional presidents dissented in favour of a *higher* rate, the most divided Fed vote in years. Nobody has set the two documents side by side. They sit oddly against Thursday itself, which rose on falling long-term American yields — but that relief came from a debt-management operation, not from a change in the rate path. For anyone holding assets priced off a discount rate, from lenders to expensive growth shares, both curves are now debating the same direction, and it is not downwards.

**The order that lifted sugar is designed to bring sugar prices down, and it expires.** The limit binds buyers for three months and lapses on 30 November, at the end of the festive season. What actually moved was the physical price, not the notification, so the things to follow are the spot quote and that end-date.

**Eight of the ten biggest risers, and the only big faller, have no cause anyone can point to.** Across two independent lanes we ran fifteen checks on the risers and killed three stories that would have shipped as reasons, all of them wrongly dated. The feed most likely to close the gap, the record of large block trades, is one we do not have — and MMTC at twenty-nine times normal volume with nothing attached is where that hole shows. "Nobody knows" is the accurate answer and a better one than something plausible.

## What to Watch Tomorrow

- **Friday 21 August, the Reserve Bank's weekly statistics, about 17:00**, for the week to 14 August. The last two weeks were builds in foreign-exchange reserves of more than $5 billion each, to about $707 billion, a figure carried forward and not re-verified this run. Reserves also move on revaluation, so one week settles nothing — but heavy dollar-selling to defend the rupee would tend to draw them down, and two builds do not look like that.
- **Friday 21 August** — the central bank extends its cash-draining auction to three days across the weekend, and runs an underwriting auction for ₹28,000 crore of government bonds.
- **Tuesday 25 August** — monthly derivatives expiry on the NSE, on the last-Tuesday convention. The first since the regulator published evidence that retail participation in derivatives is shrinking.
- **Wednesday 26 August** — Jio Financial Services (JIOFIN) holds its third annual general meeting at 14:00, by video. 19 August was the cut-off for voting eligibility; no deadline for casting a vote has been established and none should be assumed.
- **Friday 28 August** — comments close on the Reserve Bank's draft rules for non-bank lenders. Still the most consequential unpriced date on the calendar: whether the restriction survives, whether the credit-card carve-out widens, and whether a transition period appears are all open.
- **Monday 31 August** — the MSCI August review takes effect at the close, with four additions and three removals listed in MSCI's own review document. Funds that track the index must trade at that close whatever they think of it. We hold no estimate of how much money that is and are not inventing one.

- **Wednesday 9 September** — the US Treasury buybacks that caused Thursday's rally actually begin, and run to early November.
- **5–7 October** — the Reserve Bank's next rate-setting meeting, which the August minutes have made more interesting than it looked a week ago.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own press-release pages, the US Treasury's and the Federal Reserve's own releases, end-of-day data from two independent market feeds, and established Indian financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim.

**The timing rule, stated once because it decided two stories.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35, so a document published later cannot explain that day's close. It removed the regulator's derivatives study and Netweb's share-sale allotment from consideration as causes. This brief covers Thursday and was written on Friday, roughly fifteen hours late; anything dated 21 August is labelled and appears only as forward context.

**Three stories were killed on their dates:** a Genus Power order win that traced to 2024, a trial catalyst for Suven dated June, and a GST rate meeting reported for this week that turned out to be 2025 material.

**The three withheld numbers, and one correction to ourselves.** No India 10-year yield: our bond curve failed for a twentieth consecutive run, and the only figure available is one vendor's ~6.84% for 19 August, second-hand and dated. No year-to-date figures: our feed's year-start base shifted between the 18 and 20 August runs, moving the Nifty's reading 1.2 percentage points on a 0.3% price gain, so at least one is wrong. No sector ranking: nine of eleven indices returned nothing usable, and two readings are not a ranking. The correction is HSCL — our own mover screen reported zero fallers, which described what its filters passed through rather than the market.

**What else we could not see.** GIFT Nifty was blocked for a seventh run. The results scan returned nothing priced against five reporters. Large-deal data is not wired in at all, which weakens every "cause not established" above — that is a hole, not a clean negative.
