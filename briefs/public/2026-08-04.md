## The Lead

The Reserve Bank of India announces its rate decision at 10:00 tomorrow morning, and the number worth watching is not the rate. It is the inflation forecast. In June the Bank raised its projection for this financial year to 5.1% from 4.6%, blaming the West Asia conflict and higher energy prices, and it built that projection on Brent crude averaging about **$95 a barrel**. Brent has traded between **$79 and $86** all week. Nobody is publicly expecting the Bank to mark that assumption down, and one input has moved the other way since June: the weather office now forecasts below-normal rain in August. Indian shares fell into the decision, the Nifty 50 by 0.64%, with the fear gauge up 2.18%.

## Macro & Policy

**On the supportive side**, the oil story keeps improving for a country that imports 85% of what it burns, and not only because of diplomacy. The OPEC+ core added another 188,000 barrels a day, a fifth straight monthly increase; Turkey and Iraq extended their pipeline deal by a year; Kazakhstan resumed Caspian flows. Three pieces of alternative supply stacked up in one week, underneath the louder headlines. Separately, an open question of ours closed on primary documents: the United States applies a **reciprocal tariff of 18%** to Indian goods, per the US–India Joint Statement on whitehouse.gov and Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal on the government's own broadcaster.

**On the worrying side**, the weather office now forecasts **below-normal August rainfall at 94% of the long-period average**, and below normal for the rest of the season. Rain through July was 1% above normal, so this is a forecast turning rather than a shortfall that has happened. But the Bank named monsoon uncertainty as one of its three reasons for raising the forecast, so **crude has moved in its favour since June while the monsoon has moved against it** — anyone arguing the forecast must come down has to net the two. Alongside that, the July factory survey was finalised at 53.5, revised down from its own early estimate, and the services survey lands tomorrow morning before the decision.

**What forecasters actually expect, as input and not as authority.** A hold at 5.25% for a fourth meeting, though not unanimously: a Reuters poll has **68 of 72 economists** expecting no change, leaving four who do not. A PTI poll expects the neutral stance kept with a hawkish tone, most seeing **no change** to 5.1% and a few seeing it revised **up**. On the path beyond tomorrow the published sell-side view runs hawkish — Goldman Sachs has two quarter-point increases pencilled in from October. **A downward revision is the branch nobody is publicly underwriting.** Both branches are informative: mark the crude assumption to market and 5.1% has to come down; leave it alone and the Bank is saying it does not believe the fall in oil will last, which is a statement about Hormuz rather than about inflation.

**On Hormuz, nothing changed today, and that is the point.** Confirmed: Iran's foreign ministry said on Monday there are no negotiations with the United States, and its talks are with Oman about a route it calls **temporary**; that position was not withdrawn today. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has called those talks "final stages" since 29 July. Nothing is signed, and the strait has been largely closed to commercial shipping since 28 February. Claimed, by the American side only: that a deal is imminent, that talks resumed Monday, and that the strait will be **"permanently open"** — which contradicts Iran's own word, temporary. President Donald Trump today called this Tehran's "last chance." The diplomacy stood still; the oil price moved 9% inside a day.

## Regulatory

**Day three of the new closing-price mechanism, and the number that explains it finally arrived.** Since Monday, under a SEBI framework dated 16 January, the official close of every stock with listed derivatives is set by a single call auction between 15:15 and 15:35 instead of by averaging the last half-hour of ordinary trading. Monday produced a 90 basis-point gap between the Nifty's rise and the Sensex's. Today the two spent most of the session moving in **opposite directions** — the Sensex up 0.25% while the Nifty was down 0.58% at one point — before closing 37 basis points apart, with the Nifty now the weaker one.

The mechanism is a liquidity split. **Auction turnover on 04 August was ₹1,542.65 crore on the NSE against ₹9.36 crore on the BSE, roughly 165 to one.** Institutional orders go almost entirely into the NSE auction; the BSE auction is nearly empty. A thin auction moves a price further per rupee traded, so the same heavyweight can close at two different levels on two exchanges — Trent, Bharat Electronics, Bajaj Finance, Mahindra & Mahindra, Titan, Tata Consultancy Services and UltraTech Cement are the names reported to have done so — and two indices holding largely the same companies then report different days. **That turnover figure comes from a single newspaper report (Business Standard, 04 August) whose page we could not open, so we attribute it rather than assert it.**

Two things follow. **This gap is plumbing, not a market signal** — it says nothing about breadth, direction or appetite for risk. And if Monday's Nifty close was pushed *above* fair value by an auction imbalance, today began from an inflated base, making the Nifty's −0.64% an overstatement of the day's deterioration and the Sensex's −0.27% the cleaner read. The official response is thin: the NSE's first comment answered a question about price divergence with a statistic about participation, and **SEBI has published nothing on the auction since the January circular, three sessions in.**

## The Market

**Start with what we could not see.** Nine sector indices — Auto, FMCG, Metal, Realty, Energy, Media, PSU Bank, Financial Services and Consumption — came back with no previous day's close, so their one-day change is unknown, not zero, and four more failed outright. **Only two survived: Nifty IT −0.82% and Nifty Pharma −0.24%.** We will not tell you a sector moved when its index has no reading. The whole Indian government-bond curve also failed for a third straight session, so this brief covers the eve of a rate decision with no measured domestic yield behind it.

Where a sector read was needed we built it from named companies and label it as such. **Property was the day's worst corner** — DLF −3.76%, Ajmera Realty −5.71%, Prestige Estates −4.49%, Oberoi Realty −4.42%, Godrej Properties −2.68%, about −1.9% as a group — and the one cohort move with a dated cause behind it, in the results below. **Metals were the only green group**, up roughly 0.6%. Of the two indices that did print, technology carries a continuity problem: Nifty IT led the market on Monday with a 3%-plus gain and was the worst index today, with no dated India-specific catalyst on either day, and Tata Consultancy Services sits on the list of shares whose two exchange closes diverged under the new auction. We are not narrating an IT story off those two numbers.

**Two breadth counts disagree, and the disagreement is the finding.** In our own universe — Nifty 500 plus tracked names, so tilted towards larger companies — 279 rose against 392 that fell, median stock −0.25%. Across the whole exchange, close reports put it at 2,030 advances against 2,037 declines, essentially even, with smallcaps up 0.2% and midcaps down 0.3%. **The selling was concentrated where the institutions are and never reached the small-company tail.** That is de-risking before a policy event, not a broad sell-off, and today should not be described as one. The fear gauge fits: India VIX rose 2.18% to 12.19, a one-week high, but sits 56% below its 52-week high and 5.5% above its low — insurance being bought ahead of a known event, not fear of the outcome. A weekly derivatives settlement also fell today, the NSE's weeklies now settling on Tuesdays.

**Now the flows, and this is where honesty costs us the best headline of the day.** For weeks the pattern has been token foreign buying swamped by larger domestic buying — ₹277 crore against ₹2,260 crore on 31 July, ₹922 crore against ₹1,571 crore on 03 August. Today's reported figures flip both legs at once: **foreign investors net buyers of ₹2,446 crore, domestic institutions net sellers of ₹936 crore**, on a day the index fell.

**But it comes from one Tier-2 aggregator, it is provisional, and we could not verify it** — a second channel matched our own data for 03 August to the decimal but had no 04 August figure, and a third refused to load. So state the awkwardness plainly: a ₹2,446 crore foreign net purchase does not sit comfortably beside a 0.64% fall. Three explanations fit — the number is wrong and gets revised, or foreign buying landed in names other than the heavyweights that did the damage, or it is an artefact of auction-window flow on a mechanism demonstrably distorting where closes print. **One session and one source cannot tell those apart.** The rupee closed at ₹95.38, unchanged, the dollar flat, on a day crude spanned 9%. That stillness is absence of pressure, not strength.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what triggered, why, and which corners of the market it reaches next.*

**The same barrel of oil now runs through four Indian datasets, and tomorrow morning the Reserve Bank has to decide which one it believes.** Brent ran up more than 20% through July. That shows up first in the tax data, where goods-and-services tax on imports grew 28.8% against 10.1% on domestic sales, because import tax is charged on landed value. Second in the factory survey, finalised at a near five-year low with the release naming West Asia tension. Third today, inside Emami's accounts, where a 16% profit fall on 15% sales growth was blamed on "one of the sharpest inflationary phases the sector has faced" — and inside Torrent Power's, where disrupted gas supply hit its thermal plants. And fourth in the Bank's own 5.1% inflation forecast, built on crude at $95 while crude trades at $79 to $86.

**Tomorrow is where that assumption gets marked or defended.** One oddity alongside it, and it sits after India's close so it explains nothing about today: gold rose 2.29% on the same evening crude fell more than 5%. If a Gulf settlement were genuinely believed, both should have fallen. The metals market is not buying what the oil market is selling.

**Two suppliers to the same car industry reported today and got opposite verdicts, and the difference was copper.** Motherson Sumi Wiring grew revenue 36.6% and its profit did not move, because copper and wage costs ate the gain — copper itself rose 2.03% today and 7.58% over the month, so the company's stated reason is visible in the commodity price. UNO Minda grew revenue by a similar amount, kept operating profit rising 21%, and closed up. **Same industry, same month, same input costs, opposite outcomes** — a reminder that "auto parts did well" is rarely a sentence worth trusting. In a rising-metal month, a supplier's ability to pass costs on matters more than how many cars were sold.

**Property was the day's worst corner, and the reason has an invoice attached rather than an interest rate.** It is tempting to read developers falling on the eve of a rate decision as rate fear. The evidence says otherwise: three of the five worst property names filed weak results inside the window. **Do not credit the Monetary Policy Committee with a move that already has an earnings explanation.** The useful point inside those filings is that a developer's profit and its sales can move in opposite directions in one quarter — Godrej Properties' bookings rose 22% while its profit fell 42% — because revenue is recognised only when construction crosses a milestone. Bookings tell you about the future; profit tells you about the past.
