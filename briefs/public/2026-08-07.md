## The Lead

The Reserve Bank of India put out a draft rule on Thursday that would stop non-bank lenders from offering revolving credit, the kind of limit you can repay and draw again, leaving them only fixed-schedule term loans. Comments are open until 28 August, so nothing here is law. The market priced it as though it were. Bajaj Finance (BAJFINANCE) fell 5.84% to ₹1,078, the worst stock in both benchmarks, with Bajaj Finserv (BAJAJFINSV) down 3.70% and Jio Financial Services (JIOFIN) down 2.39%.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side.** Brent crude is down roughly 9.4% over the week, which for an oil-importing country matters far more than any single day's tick. The Reserve Bank's weekly statistics showed foreign exchange reserves up $10.512 billion to $692.866 billion. A correction goes with that: yesterday's brief said this release would cover the crude fall and the post-decision rupee. It does not, because it covers the week ended 31 July, which ends before both.

**The worrying side.** The rupee weakened 0.13% to 95.198 against a dollar that fell 0.54% against everything else, and it lost ground to the euro and the yen too. When your currency slips while the dollar is being sold, the weakness is yours. We cannot cross-check that against the bond market tonight.

**On the Strait of Hormuz, two separate things are being confused.** Iran and Oman have agreed in principle on reopening: route coordinates, a proposed joint coordination centre, inbound traffic through a northern corridor near Iran and outbound through a southern one near Oman, moving to a central corridor later, and transit fees based on services rendered rather than a share of cargo value. A joint statement is in final drafting. Separately, and at an earlier stage, an Iranian parliamentary committee is reviewing a measure to bar American and Israeli vessels; that ban is not a term of the Iran-Oman arrangement. President Donald Trump has balked at any deal giving Iran more control of the strait, and American acceptance is unclear. That is the live condition on all of it.

## Regulatory

**The draft in plain terms, and what is still open about it.** Non-bank lenders would be allowed only term loans: a fixed amount, disbursed once or in instalments, repaid on a set schedule, with a limit that cannot be topped back up once repaid. The only exemption is for lenders the Reserve Bank authorises to issue credit cards, reportedly two companies, SBI Card and BoB Cards, so an exemption that reads wide is narrow in practice. The draft also says the rules would take effect immediately on notification, with no transition period in the text. A comment window exists precisely so things like that can change, and it closes on 28 August.

Two more Reserve Bank consultations went out today, both on bank capital rules, and we found no coverage of either. We have confirmed they exist and what they are called; we have not read them, so we assert nothing about their contents. The market regulator separately rewrote how intermediaries are inspected, requiring joint inspections by exchanges and depositories and cutting its own inspection target for the coming year to roughly a third. That is a change in who does the watching.

## The Connections

*Behind the session — what moved, what did not, and which industries it points at.*

**A draft rule aimed at non-banks was paid for by banks it does not touch.** It would bind non-bank lenders only. Yet ICICI Bank (ICICIBANK) fell 2.50% and Axis Bank (AXISBANK) 1.43%, neither exposed to it, while State Bank of India rose 1.12% on its own results. When a cohort gets repriced, the selling stops where investors stop reading rather than at the boundary of the document. For anyone holding a lender, that boundary is the thing worth locating.

**Nobody traded the oil story, in either direction.** The refiners and marketers that buy crude should have cheered a 9.4% weekly fall in it, and did nothing: Bharat Petroleum (BPCL) −0.83%, Indian Oil (IOC) +0.50%, Reliance Industries (RELIANCE) +0.74%. Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) sits on the other side, because it pumps crude rather than buying it, so a cheaper barrel cuts what it earns. It closed up 0.44%. That is the sharper half of this: the three that stood to gain barely moved, and the one that stood to lose did not fall at all. Several close reports framed the session on crude rising during Indian hours; this complex is quiet evidence against that. Second session running that neither side of India's oil chain has priced a large move in crude, and we cannot tell you why.

**Gold split the jewellery business in two, and the split is the useful part.** Domestic gold futures rose 1.56% and silver 3.78% during Indian hours, so the timing works. The retailers fell: Titan Company −1.14%, D.P. Abhushan (DPABHUSHAN) −1.06%, Senco Gold (SENCO) −0.72%. The makers and suppliers rose: Sky Gold (SKYGOLD) +4.09% to a 52-week high, Shanti Gold International (SHANTIGOLD) +2.45%, Hindustan Zinc (HINDZINC) +2.20% on silver. A fast bullion move marks up the stock a fabricator already holds and marks down the footfall walking into a showroom. That is our reading of the prices, not a sourced explanation.

**One index number, two different businesses.** Nifty IT closed +1.42% on its four largest members, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) +3.36% among them. Inside the same index Saksoft (SAKSOFT) filed a quarter with revenue down 0.2% at 14:00 and fell 5.99%, eClerx Services (ECLERX) fell 5.52% and Firstsource Solutions (FSL) 4.80%. The large exporters rose on global sentiment nobody in India generated; the mid-caps were marked on their own results. Pharma did it in reverse, a −0.09% index hiding mid-cap ingredient makers up over 4% while the large formulators drifted lower.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read directly with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own press-release indexes, company releases, end-of-day market data, and established Indian financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim. A "Change in Auditors" heading on the exchange feed covers internal, cost and tax auditors at least as often as statutory ones, so we read the board outcome before repeating the label.

Three withholdings worth naming. Our own feed and an independent read disagree on the size of Friday's move in Brent and agree it was down, so we publish the direction and the weekly figure and no daily number. Our screening feed and the reported figures disagree on the sign of Hero MotoCorp's profit growth, so we published levels and withheld the rate. And a widely circulated article explaining a 10% fall in EIH "on August 7" is from 2024, while several results that surfaced today were from 2025, so every item was checked against its dateline.

Known gaps, stated once and in full. Thirteen of the fifteen sector indices are unmeasured rather than flat: nine carry a level with no computable change, and four were never built, Consumer Durables among them. The government bond curve failed on all seven maturities for a fourth run, so on a day driven by a rule about credit, the rate instrument is exactly what we could not read. The overnight futures feed was blocked a fifth time. Today's institutional flows are unpublished, and one aggregator's numbers were implausible enough that we did not print them. Our fundamentals feed carries 38 companies with no date attached, so its freshness cannot be asserted either way. The movers table carries no market value for Bajaj Finance, shown as unavailable rather than as zero. Large-deal data is not wired into this pipeline, which weakens every "cause not established" verdict above: a hole, not a clean negative.
