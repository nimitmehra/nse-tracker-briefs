## Macro & Policy

**On the supportive side, a Friday data release almost nobody covered.** The statistics ministry published July industrial production at 6.7% against a year earlier, manufacturing up 7.3%, with 19 of 23 industry groups growing. The figure inside it worth holding is **capital goods, up 16.1%** — machinery and equipment, which companies only order when they intend to expand. **We are not writing "growth slowed from June," because three different June figures are circulating and the base is unsettled.** The level is the fact. *(Reported; the ministry's own release was not opened.)* Also supportive, with a qualification: the Reserve Bank's weekly statistics show reserves up US$12.42 billion to US$729.33 billion, a fourth consecutive build. **A rising reserve pile is not evidence of the central bank defending the rupee — that would show as a fall** — and US$2.80 billion of it is the gold line, which moves when the gold price moves. *(Reported; every carrier traces to one wire.)*

**On the worrying side, India's own bond market is pricing rate rises.** The 10-year yield sat around 6.9% on Friday, a two-month high, on a heavy supply calendar — a ₹34,000 crore bond sale, a buyback notice and two auctions in one week — plus renewed expectation that the Reserve Bank could raise rates after its August minutes flagged the possibility. *(Reported, single source; our own bond feed has failed for 24 straight sessions, so we cannot check it.)* Thursday's 13% monsoon deficit is unchanged, with no reading past 26 August.

**And the thing to watch, which lands after the weekend.** Monday brings India's June-quarter growth figure, and **the forecasts run from about 6.9% to 8.0%** against 7.8% the quarter before — one house alone moved from around 7% on 1 August to 8% on 11 August. Against last quarter, 7% is a four-quarter low and the story is a slowdown; against 86% of leading indicators accelerating, 8% is an upgrade. **We adopt neither framing; the spread itself is the finding.** The same release switches the industrial and price series to a revised 2022-23 base year, so any "lowest since" claim built on it is not a clean comparison.

## Regulatory

- **A seventh action on the same corner of the financial system in six business days, and a heavier instrument this time.** The Reserve Bank issued Directions under Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act against Ashok Sahakari Bank Ltd., Ahmednagar, on 28 August. Directions **restrict what a bank may do**; the six before it, which we counted on Thursday, were fines. So it is seven actions, not seven penalties. All seven landed on a non-bank lender or a co-operative bank, in the six days ending on the comment deadline for the draft rules governing exactly that group. **That timing is an observation, not a claim of cause.** *(Confirmed from the Reserve Bank's own release index; grounds not read.)*
- **The market regulator cleared the Jio Platforms public issue, which carriers call India's largest-ever listing.** Per the draft document: up to 27 crore new shares, no existing holder selling, size not yet fixed, indicative range reported at US$3.8–4 billion. **A clearance is permission to proceed, not a schedule — there is no listing date.** *(Reported.)* It also narrows Thursday's finding: SEBI has now gone four business days without a press release, nine days before the second phase of the new closing auction, but it has plainly not gone quiet on everything.

**A green headline on a flat market, and the gap between the two is the day.** Nifty IT rose 3.51% — Coforge +5.93%, Tata Consultancy Services +4.16%, Persistent Systems +3.91%, Tech Mahindra +3.53%, Infosys +2.99%, Mphasis +1.72%. **Infosys, the name most people would assume led, actually lagged its own sector by half a percentage point.** Strip technology out of the Nifty 50 and the day was 18 up, 18 down, nothing. The banks did not join in either: Nifty Bank closed −0.02%, with ICICI Bank off about 1.3% the largest drag named in the press, alongside UltraTech Cement about 1.2% lower and Asian Paints about 1.1%. **No sourced reason was found for that consumer-goods weakness**; it fits the flat non-technology market rather than any separate bad news.

**What we cannot show you, for a fifth session: the official sector table.** Nine of eleven sector indices returned no usable one-day move — only Nifty IT (+3.51%) and Nifty Pharma (+0.53%) are live. Our own reconstruction from the stocks in each group, checked against those two live indices as controls, puts **Metal +0.79% as the only one up**, with Auto −0.48%, FMCG −0.46%, Consumption −0.44%, Media −0.42%, Financial Services −0.37%, Realty −0.22%, Energy −0.19% and PSU Bank −0.14%. **Consumer Durables, Healthcare, Oil & Gas and Private Bank could not be reconstructed at all.** Those are our figures, not the exchange's. So the honest sentence is "technology led, and eight of the nine sectors we can see were flat to down" — never "IT led the market."

**Nobody fell, and the fear gauge went to sleep.** Not one stock in the universe lost 5% or more, and the worst close anywhere was −4.66%. **This needs no explanation of our screen: on Thursday the screen did hide real fallers and we said so at length, but on Friday there were none to hide.** India VIX fell 3.52% to 10.68, leaving it 1.04% above its own 52-week low — the market is charging almost nothing to insure against a surprise, three days before a growth number with a one-point forecast spread on it.

**On flows, and they are Friday's.** Foreign investors sold ₹5,039.80 crore and domestic institutions bought ₹5,183.93 crore, **absorbing it almost exactly on a day the index closed up.** Two sessions this week saw small foreign buying — ₹1,181.66 crore on 24 August, ₹502.63 crore on 26 August — and Friday's sale is an order of magnitude bigger than either. Whatever else Friday was, it was not foreign money coming back.

## Corporate News

- **Hero MotoCorp (HEROMOTOCO) bought ₹1,758 crore of Ather Energy (ATHERENERG) shares from other holders, and the distinction in that sentence is the story.** 1,18,80,000 shares changed hands at ₹1,480 in a block deal, taking Hero — already Ather's largest shareholder — to about 32.8% fully diluted. **This is a secondary transaction: no new money entered Ather.** Hero bought existing shares from existing owners, which is a different thing from funding the company, and it is how this headline usually gets written wrong. Completion is expected 3 September, no approval is required, and Ather says it is not a related-party transaction. *(Confirmed — Business Standard twice, plus independent outlets.)* **We are not implying a takeover offer**, since a holder already above 25% may creep up 5% a year without triggering one. A separate ₹1,200 crore share issue **was allotted on 25 August** — a genuine fundraise, and not Friday's news.
- **Ola Electric (OLAELEC) launched the S1Z scooter at ₹79,999 during Friday's session, a day before its closest rival was due to unveil a new one.** The S1Z Plus is ₹99,999, deliveries start December 2026, and it is Ola's first range on its own lithium-iron-phosphate cell. **The price is the point: it lands roughly 20–35% below the ₹1–1.25 lakh bracket the market expected Ather's new scooter to occupy.** *(Confirmed across three carriers; the range figures are the manufacturer's own test-cycle claims and we do not repeat them as facts.)* This is the first Indian electric two-wheeler launch that reads as a price war rather than a product cycle.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**Friday's technology rally was borrowed price movement, not borrowed business — and four things say so.** The tempting sentence is that a chip company's demand converts into Indian software revenue. **We could not source a single step of that chain, and the evidence points the other way.** Participation was uniform, including companies with no artificial-intelligence story at all — Wipro +2.58%, Mphasis +1.72%. The leaders were high-beta mid-sized names rather than the large caps most exposed to the theme, and Infosys lagged. **The rupee moved the wrong way for the story**: it did not weaken, and a weaker rupee is what helps a company that earns dollars and spends rupees. And no brokerage published an Indian software sector note all day. This is imported share-price movement arriving a session late — a real thing, and a fragile one.

**Foreign money left, domestic money caught it, and the banks did not move.** Foreign investors sold ₹5,039.80 crore and domestic institutions bought ₹5,183.93 crore, near-perfectly matched — yet Nifty Bank, the usual destination for domestic institutional money, closed −0.02%. **We could not establish why**, and it is the more interesting non-event of the day: when five thousand crore of domestic buying leaves no mark on the largest domestic sector, either the money went elsewhere or someone else was selling the same names.

**Two central banks are now openly discussing rate rises at once, and India's bond market has started pricing it.** The Reserve Bank's August minutes flagged that policymakers might consider raising rates if inflation risks broaden; the American central bank's chief said on Friday evening that inflation is still too high and the Fed may have to raise. **India's 10-year yield near 6.9%, a two-month high, is the visible consequence on this side.** The mechanism worth understanding: a higher risk-free yield makes every future rupee of company profit worth less today, and it bites hardest on companies priced for growth far out — which is exactly the corner that rallied on Friday.

**And the cheapest insurance in a year sits three days from the widest forecast spread on this desk.** India VIX at 10.68 is 1.04% above its 52-week low, and Friday produced not one 5% faller. Monday carries a growth number forecast anywhere between 6.9% and 8.0%, an index reshuffle transacting in the closing auction, a foreign-deposit window shutting, and the first Indian chance to price Friday night's Fed speech. **Low volatility is not a forecast of calm; it is what protection costs when nobody is buying any.**

## What to Watch — Monday 31 August

*The weekend sits between this brief and the next session. Everything below is Monday's, not Friday's.*

- **Monday is the first Indian session that can price the Jackson Hole speech, and it runs against what this market has assumed all year.** Kevin Warsh, in his first keynote as chairman of the American central bank, said inflation is still too high and that the Fed **may have to raise rates**, and refused to give any guidance on what comes next. Inflation there is 3.4% and the 30-year government bond yield is near its highest since 2007. *(Confirmed at the Federal Reserve's own speech page plus four independent publishers.)* **Odds of a September rate rise moved up on it. We are not printing a number — the figure in circulation could not be checked at source and a pre-speech reading was very different.**
- **Monday, India's June-quarter growth figure** — forecasts spanning roughly 6.9% to 8.0% against 7.8% last quarter, with a base-year change folded into the same release.
- **Monday, the MSCI index review transacts in the closing auction**, effective 1 September; funds that track the index must trade at that close whatever they think. In go Adani Energy Solutions, Billionbrains Garage Ventures, Laurus Labs and Lenskart Solutions; out go Astral, Balkrishna Industries and SBI Cards. **Any rupee figure needs its scope attached** — roughly US$600 million covers only the stocks joining, US$2.3–3.2 billion covers the whole review, and the stock-level estimate in circulation dates to about 13 August, not to now.
- **Monday, the Reserve Bank's special window for foreign-currency deposits from Indians abroad closes**, though swaps against eligible deposits run to 11 September and the overseas-borrowing leg to 31 December. Three dates, not one. **Also Monday, a 15-day auction to absorb surplus bank cash** — the tenor has moved four times in five business days, from overnight out to 15 days, and that volatility is itself the finding.
- **Monday is also the first session that can price whatever Ather Energy announces this weekend.** Its Community Day is today, Saturday 29 August, and the new scooter is named Konarc. **We assert nothing about it — as of this writing the event had not happened, no price has been announced, and outlets disagree on what to expect.** A search summary will confidently tell you it launched; that is pre-event copy rewritten in the past tense.
- **Tuesday 1 September** — weekly Nifty settlement, monthly vehicle sales (the first read on whether the two-wheeler price war is shifting anything), and the sugar stockholding limit for bulk buyers. **Thursday 3 September** — the Hero–Ather block deal completes. **Friday 4 September** — weekly central-bank statistics, and whether a fifth reserve build arrives in the last week of the deposit window. **Monday 7 September** — the second phase of the closing auction session.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own release pages opened directly, end-of-day data from two independent feeds, and established Indian and international financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim. Where a cause could not be established, the entry says so — Friday produced six of those, one corroborated by the exchange itself asking the same question.

**The timing rule, which decided two stories today.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35, so nothing published later can explain that day's close. Tejas Networks' order was filed at 18:33 on Thursday, which is why Friday priced it; the Jackson Hole speech landed around 19:30 on Friday, which is why it explains nothing here.

**What we are deliberately not publishing.** No daily change for the rupee — our feed printed an impossible +2.11% two sessions ago and one plausible reading since does not clear it. No single-day change for any commodity; our snapshots are not aligned to the Indian close and our two Brent sources sit about a dollar apart on the same day. No year-to-date column on any index: the figures our feed returns for several are identical to their distance from a 52-week high, which is almost certainly a bug. No official sector ranking, for a fifth session. No United States two-year bond yield — our file's label for it points at the wrong instrument. No odds figure on an American rate rise, no bare rupee total for the index reshuffle, and nothing whatsoever about what Ather announced this weekend.

**Dates on the borrowed numbers.** Institutional flows are 28 August, from aggregators rather than the exchange's own page. Reserve figures cover the week to 21 August; industrial production is July; monsoon data is cumulative through 26 August. One name on the movers table is withheld because its ticker resolves to no company in any file we hold — we would rather print a ticker than the wrong name.
