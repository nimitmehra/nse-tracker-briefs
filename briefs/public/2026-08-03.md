## The Lead

The way India's closing prices are calculated changed on Monday morning, and by the bell the country's two headline indices disagreed about the size of the day's rally by 90 basis points. The Sensex rose 0.70% and the Nifty 50 rose 1.60%, and neither number is wrong. The honest answer to how much the market went up sits in the breadth of it: of the 680 stocks in our universe, 534 rose and 144 fell, and the median stock gained 1.35%. What lifted them was crude, which fell 7.30% over the weekend to $83.54 a barrel on an OPEC+ supply increase and a US-Iran de-escalation story that Iran itself denies.

## Macro & Policy

**On the supportive side**, two separate things hit oil over one weekend. OPEC+ met on Sunday and agreed a September output increase that completes the rollback of the 2.2 million-barrel-a-day voluntary cuts, and Donald Trump called off a planned strike on Iran. India buys roughly 85% of the crude it burns, so a 7.30% fall is money that stays in the country: a smaller import bill, an easier current account, a gentler inflation path. Two domestic numbers landed the same weekend. July GST collections came in at ₹2.11 lakh crore, up 15.4% and the fastest in fourteen months, and July passenger-vehicle wholesales crossed 4.7 lakh units, up 33% and the strongest month on record.

**On the worrying side**, both of those numbers are weaker on inspection. Inside the GST total, domestic collections grew only 10.1% while collections on imports grew 28.8%. Import GST is charged on landed value and July was the month Brent ran up more than 20%, so much of that is price rather than volume. Separately, India's monthly factory-activity survey, the HSBC manufacturing purchasing managers' index, was finalised for July at 53.5, down from 54.2 in June and below its own early estimate of 53.9. Anything above 50 means factories are still growing, so this is a slowdown rather than a contraction, but Business Standard called it a near five-year low and sales, purchasing and hiring all slowed. It landed on the morning of the year's broadest rally and nobody traded it.

**On Hormuz, be careful what you believe.** Confirmed: the strike was called off, and Iran says its separate talks with Oman are in their final stages. Also confirmed: Iran's foreign ministry said on Monday that there are no negotiations with the United States, and the strait is still physically restricted, with passage requiring Iranian permission. The claim of an imminent deal and a complete reopening is contradicted by Iran's own stated position. Monday's crude collapse, and therefore Monday's rally, was priced off the claimed half of the story rather than the confirmed half.

## Regulatory

**What changed on Monday, and why your screen behaves differently from now on.** Under a SEBI circular dated 16-January-2026 and effective 03-August, the closing price of every NSE- and BSE-listed stock that has futures and options contracts is now set by a single call auction at the end of the day, instead of by averaging the last thirty minutes of trading. The auction runs from 15:15 to 15:30, with orders allowed inside a 3% band around a reference price. On its first day, some participants entered orders to purchase above the prevailing price in heavily weighted Nifty names, which is enough to lift an index close. That explanation comes from one brokerage official rather than from an exchange, so the mechanism is established while the specific order flow is only reported.

Two things follow. Every closing price, index level, fund valuation strike and percentage-change figure on any Indian screen is now an auction outcome, so day-one oddities are plumbing rather than opinion. And the exchanges ran a joint mock session on Saturday 01-August before the switch, which tells you how they rated the risk. Phase 2, a pre-open auction, is scheduled for 07-September.

## The Market

Nine of the eleven Nifty sector indices came back with no previous day's close in our data, which means their one-day change is unknown, not zero. We will not tell you a sector moved when the index has no reading. The two valid readings both matter. **Nifty IT rose 3.28% to 31,715.25**, the strongest thing on the board and a second consecutive outsized week, up 15.58% over a month. No catalyst dated to Monday was established for it, and two widely shared explanations failed an arithmetic check. Note also that the usual currency logic ran backwards: IT firms earn dollars and spend rupees, so a stronger rupee is a headwind for them, and the rupee gained 0.36% on the day. Whatever lifted IT, it was not the currency. **Nifty Pharma rose 0.48%**, less than a third of the median stock, and was the laggard, with Divi's Laboratories up 6.57% to a 52-week closing high against Blue Jet Healthcare down 5.87%.

Auto ancillaries led at +3.83%, ahead of auto makers at +2.26%, capital goods +2.19%, private banks +1.84%, metals and consumer staples both +1.45%, energy and refiners +0.97%, and pharma at −0.05%. The broader market confirms the breadth: the Nifty Midcap and Smallcap indices each rose about 1.2%, and 74 names closed at 52-week closing highs.

Two loose ends. India VIX, the market's fear gauge, rose 1.40% to 11.93 on a day the market rallied, and sits just 3.25% above its 52-week low two days before a rate decision. ## Earnings

- **Muthoot Finance (MUTHOOTFIN)** — the clearest case this quarter of a headline hiding the story. Profit of ₹2,825 crore was up 43% and assets under management rose 43% to ₹1.91 lakh crore, which reads wonderfully. But the lending margin fell to 10.41%, down 174 basis points on the year and 297 on the previous quarter, and profit was 16.9% lower than that quarter. The 43% is flattered by a weak year-ago base; the sequential trend is going the other way. The same Saturday batch named Alexander George as managing director from 01-October, a long-planned internal handover. The stock fell 7.33%.
- **Narayana Health (NH)** — record revenue, collapsing margin, and the market traded the margin. Revenue rose 78% to ₹2,683.6 crore, but operating margin compressed 511 basis points to 18.82% and net profit rose only 5.35% to ₹207.18 crore. The reported cause is the late-2025 purchase of Practice Plus Group in the UK, a lower-margin business, plus continuing losses in its insurance arm. Growth bought at a thinner margin dilutes the group. Shares fell 5.40%.
- **Persistent Systems (PERSISTENT, market value ₹0.86 lakh crore)** — revenue of $452.4 million was up 16.1%, or ₹4,303 crore and 29% in rupees, with operating profit up 32.7% and a record $1.15 billion of new business signed, including a six-and-a-half-year deal worth over $650 million. Net profit rose 13.67% but fell 8.7% against the previous quarter on foreign-exchange losses. The stock closed up just 0.47% on a day its own sector rose 3.28%. The currency loss is the visible candidate for the market's objection, but no outlet states it, so we are not asserting it.
- **Escorts Kubota (ESCORTS)** — the headline here is a trap. Revenue rose 28% to ₹3,207.55 crore and domestic tractor volumes were the highest ever for a first quarter, up 22.9%. Profit of ₹385.93 crore looks 72% lower than a year ago, but the year-ago figure included ₹1,027.63 crore from divesting the railway equipment division and land. On continuing operations this is growth. The real negative is operating margin, down to 11.2% from 13.0% on commodity costs. Shares rose 1.60%, below the car makers' 2.26%.
- **Sundaram Finance (SUNDARMFIN)** — an unusually clean quarter, filed 68 minutes before the close. Profit rose 22% to ₹522 crore, assets under management rose 17% to ₹62,275 crore, disbursements rose 22%, return on assets improved to 3.06%, and bad loans fell to 1.71% of the book with better provision cover. Growth, margin and credit quality all moved the same way. The stock rose 3.50%.

**A note on timing, because it protects you from a common error.** Monday's reporters split three ways. Blue Jet Healthcare, Computer Age Management Services, Dhanuka Agritech, Escorts and Sundaram Finance all filed comfortably before the close, so the day's move is a real reaction. Ather Energy (15:10), UPL (15:17) and Jindal Stainless (15:21) filed in the last twenty minutes, so their prices were already set. GlaxoSmithKline Pharmaceuticals filed at 16:05, thirty-five minutes after the market shut, so its 0.26% close was struck before the numbers existed publicly. For those four the market's verdict is Tuesday's story. Ather's numbers are worth knowing anyway: revenue up 89% to ₹1,217 crore, loss narrowed 72% to ₹51.09 crore, and a first-ever positive operating profit of ₹9.45 crore.

**Two reactions we cannot explain, stated rather than guessed.** Computer Age Management Services grew revenue 11.5% and profit 17.6%, kept a 46% operating margin and declared an interim dividend of ₹2.50, and the stock closed *down* 0.65% on a day 534 of 680 names rose. Dhanuka Agritech did the opposite: revenue fell about 13%, profit fell 35%, guidance was cut to low single-digit revenue growth, and the stock finished flat at +0.14%. Neither reaction has a sourced explanation and we did not invent one. **Omitted:** ITC reported the previous week, outside this brief's window.

## Corporate News

- **Zee Entertainment Enterprises (ZEEL)** — SEBI issued a 150-page final order dated Friday 31-July barring the company from the securities market for two months and promoters Punit Goenka and Subhash Chandra for a year each, with penalties totalling ₹1.48 crore. The case concerns the unauthorised pledge of Zee's Hyderabad land to secure ₹726 crore that four Essel Group entities borrowed from Indiabulls Housing Finance in December 2016; the regulator found the two acted on a common understanding and used a deceptive device connected with dealing in Zee's shares. The sequencing is brutal. Shareholders had approved a ₹3,143 crore preferential issue of warrants to the promoters at ₹126 a share on that same 31-July, and the stock closed Monday at ₹98.14, some 22% below the subscription price. The company says the order has no direct bearing on the fundraise and it will complete it.
- **Rossell Techsys (ROSSTECH)** — the promoter group sold 18,47,117 shares, about 4.9% of the company, to Kotak Mutual Fund in a block deal disclosed after Friday's close, at roughly ₹900 a share. On its face a promoter reducing his stake is a negative. The market read the buyer instead and the stock closed 18.6% above the block price. Both sides deserve stating: a domestic fund taking 4.9% is a vote of confidence, and the promoter, who knows the business best, chose ₹900 for a stock that closed at ₹1,067 on around 148 times trailing earnings.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what triggered, why, and which corners of the market it reaches next.*

**The same barrel of oil turned up in three different Indian datasets inside seventy-two hours, and nobody joined them.** Brent ran up more than 20% through July. That shows up first in the GST numbers, where collections on imports grew 28.8% while domestic collections grew 10.1%, because import GST is charged on landed value and a costlier barrel inflates the base. It shows up second in the manufacturing survey, where July was finalised at a near five-year low with the release itself naming West Asia tension among the causes. And it showed up third on Monday, when the risk premium came out of the price and the whole equity book repriced. The July run-up was a genuine tax on Indian manufacturing; Monday was a partial refund. Watch the services survey on Wednesday and July inflation on 12-August, both of which still contain the spike rather than the relief.

**Cheaper crude is not uniformly good for anything called "energy", and Monday's session sorted that correctly.** Reliance Industries rose 0.86%, Oil and Natural Gas Corporation fell 0.22% and GAIL fell 3.94%, all on a day the median stock rose 1.35%. The mechanism is simple once separated. A producer sells oil, so a lower price means lower revenue per barrel. A refiner and petrochemical maker buys it, so the relief is partly offset by what happens to its own product prices. And a terminal operator earns on volume passing through rather than on the price of it. That is our own reading of why Aegis Logistics rose 8.39% inside the same nominal sector, and not a sourced one, because no filing or outlet states it. The cleanest beneficiary was none of them: InterGlobe Aviation rose 4.43%, because fuel is roughly a third of an Indian airline's cost base.

**The record car month was bought one layer down the chain, not at the car makers.** July passenger-vehicle wholesales set an all-time record, up 33%, and Maruti Suzuki's own domestic number was its highest ever, up 42.4%. Maruti closed down 0.59%, the only red name among the large-cap car makers, while the parts suppliers rose 3.83% as a group against the makers' 2.26%. Monthly volumes are announced by the makers themselves and were largely already in the price; a supplier's profit leverage on a 33% industry month was not. Two caveats belong with this. These are dispatches to dealers rather than sales to customers, and the retail confirmation from dealer body FADA has not landed. And both Maruti and Mahindra were flat to lower against June, so record does not mean accelerating.

**The market is pricing almost no risk into Wednesday, and we cannot see the one place that would tell us otherwise.** India VIX rose 1.40% to 11.93 on a strongly rising market, and sits 3.25% above its 52-week low with a rate decision two days away. Meanwhile our Indian bond-yield feed failed completely for a second consecutive session, on the opening day of the rate-setting meeting. A 7.3% move in the largest single input to India's inflation path, landing on day one of a meeting whose last forecast raised the inflation projection to 5.1% citing energy costs, is the most consequential juxtaposition of the session. Nobody in this chain can see how the bond market priced it. One free observation alongside it: gold did not fall on the peace headline that knocked crude down. The safest asset in the world did not believe the story.

## What to Watch Tomorrow

- **Tuesday 04-August, the second CAS close.** Day one produced a 90 basis-point gap between the Sensex and the Nifty, the second widest since 2010. Whether that narrows tells you if Monday was teething or structural.
- **Whether crude holds near $83.** Iran's denial that it is talking to the United States was on the record before Monday's close and the market bought the rally anyway. If the diplomacy half unwinds, the OPEC+ supply increase is the half that survives.
- **Tuesday's reaction to Monday's late filings** — Ather Energy, UPL, Jindal Stainless and GlaxoSmithKline Pharmaceuticals all reported too late to be judged on Monday. Jindal Stainless holds its earnings call at 16:00.
- **Wednesday 05-August: the Reserve Bank's rate decision**, announced by Governor Sanjay Malhotra. The repo rate has sat at 5.25% for three meetings and two separate economist polls are unanimous for a hold, so the language, the inflation forecast and any comment on the crude round-trip carry more information than the rate itself. With no disagreement priced in either direction, any deviation would be a larger shock than usual.
- **Wednesday also brings the July services survey**, whose flash reading was weak at 53.1 against 57.4 in June. Manufacturing was revised down from flash to final, so the services final is a live downside risk on rate-decision morning.
- **Friday 07-August: the RBI's weekly reserves data**, the first covering the crude collapse and the rupee's move, plus a heavy results day including Life Insurance Corporation, HPCL and Godrej Consumer. More than 350 companies report across this week, among them State Bank of India, Bharti Airtel, ONGC, Trent and Titan.
