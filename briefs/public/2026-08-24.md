## The Lead

The Reserve Bank's concessional window for FCNR(B) deposits, the dollar accounts Indians abroad hold with Indian banks, closes on 31 August rather than 30 September, and Monday left seven days of it. The central bank brought the date forward on 14 August because the response was so large. Its own release on Saturday 22 August puts total inflows under the facility at US$72.85 billion, of which US$65.4 billion is those deposits. Swaps against eligible deposits can still be availed until 11 September, and the overseas-borrowing leg stays open to 31 December. This is the plumbing sitting under three consecutive weekly builds in India's foreign-exchange reserves, and it now has an expiry date on it.

## Macro & Policy

**The supportive side, and what it is worth.** Foreign-exchange reserves rose US$9.905 billion to US$716.907 billion in the week to 14 August, published Friday evening. That is a third consecutive build, and each has been well past the US$5 billion weekly drawdown this desk uses as its rough test for heavy dollar-selling to defend the rupee. Three weekly readings have now gone the other way, so the "the Reserve Bank is burning reserves" line has failed three weeks running and we retire it. One caution travels with that. A reserve build funded by swapping in FCNR(B) dollars is mechanically different from one earned by trade or investment, and it stops when the window stops. We have not verified how much of the build is swap-related, because the weekly supplement does not break it out, so that is a question to test against the next three weekly releases and not something to state as cause.

**Alongside it, the central bank is draining cash for longer.** Its reverse repo auctions, the operations that take surplus rupees out of the banking system, went from overnight to three-day to seven-day inside three business days, with a two-day auction announced for Tuesday. Absorption is being termed out rather than simply rolled. We do not publish the amounts because we did not open the individual result pages.

**The worrying side, and it is domestic.** The Reserve Bank's All-India House Price Index for the June quarter landed on Monday. The index rose 1.1% on the quarter to 117.5, but annual growth slowed to 3.6% from 4.5%, which is below the bank's own projected inflation path. In plain terms, house prices across the eighteen cities it tracks are falling in real money. What growth remains sits in Chandigarh, Jaipur, Kanpur, Lucknow and Thiruvananthapuram, and not one of those is a top-six metro. Separately, the eight core industries grew 5.4% in July against 6.0% in June, with fertiliser output down 8.0% and domestic crude down 5.3%. On the monsoon we have no current reading: the latest we hold is 5 to 9 August, roughly 11% below the long-period average, with the weather office forecasting below-normal rain for August and September. Two runs without a fresh number in the season's most important month is a gap and we state it as one.

**A correction to ourselves.** This brief carried "the concessional swap window closes 30 September" for several runs at reported confidence and never went back to check it. The Reserve Bank moved the date on 14 August and the press carried it that day. The 30 September date is retired.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what connects across industries, and what it points at next.*

**The rice trade and the oil trade priced opposite outcomes of the same event, on the same day.** Rice exporters rose up to 13% during Monday on the premise that West Asian shipping is normalising and export volumes recover from the September quarter. On that same session Indian indices were reported down because Iran threatened to fine, seize or confiscate ships crossing the Strait of Hormuz, and crude fell 1.81% because traders concluded any disruption was already in the price. Three markets, three readings of one event, and at least two of them are wrong. Which one is right will be settled in freight rates and in shipping insurance, not in share prices, so that is what to follow.

**We do not adopt "Indian shares fell on Hormuz fears", and the oil market is the reason.** A genuine threat to the world's most important oil chokepoint does not send Brent down two percent. Indian refiners went the other way and rose on cheaper crude, with Indian Oil +1.91%, GAIL +1.02% and BPCL +0.29%, which is not how an oil-importing market behaves when it believes a supply shock is coming. And the American sanctions announcement that much of the coverage attached to Monday landed between roughly 17:30 and 19:00 India time, after our market had shut. Monday traded the anticipation, which was pre-announced on Sunday; it did not trade the contents. A 0.14% move does not need a geopolitical cause and we are not giving it one.

**Higher gold split the gold trade in two, and the split is the more useful read than the rally.** Gold rose 1.70% and the lenders and smaller jewellers ran with it: Senco +7.94%, Muthoot +6.19%, RBZ Jewellers +4.75%, Manappuram +2.11%. Large-cap jewellery retail sat the day out, with Titan −0.14% and Kalyan Jewellers +0.73%. That is coherent rather than odd. A higher metal price is unambiguously good for a lender holding pledged gold, and genuinely two-sided for a jeweller, who books an inventory gain now and loses volume later if the price stays here. The names with the most volume at risk and the least inventory leverage are precisely the ones that did not join in. And do not read the day as a precious-metals rally: silver fell 0.80% on the same session, which narrows this to a bid for gold alone.

**The Reserve Bank published falling real house prices on the day property shares outperformed.** Its index has annual growth down to 3.6% and below the bank's own projected inflation path, with what growth remains sitting in Chandigarh, Jaipur, Kanpur, Lucknow and Thiruvananthapuram rather than the six biggest cities. Realty was one of Monday's two outperforming corners on our constituent reading, with ten of thirteen names up, led by Ventive +3.82%, Raymond Realty +2.82%, Brigade +2.32% and DLF +1.61%. No source we found connects the two and we are not inventing a link. Set the price index and the share prices side by side and the tension is real; what settles it is whether the next quarterly reading confirms the deceleration or reverses it.

**Five of the twelve biggest movers have no cause anyone can point to.** Two independent lanes worked all twelve names; seven came back with something and five did not. IDBI is where the hole shows clearest, and the missing piece is not effort but plumbing: the record of large negotiated trades has been absent from our data for six runs. "Nobody knows" is the accurate answer, and it is a better one than something plausible.

## Sources & Method

Built from exchange filings read with their broadcast timestamps, the Reserve Bank's and the market regulator's own press-release pages, end-of-day data from two independent market feeds, and established Indian and international financial press. Anything reported rather than confirmed carries that word in the same sentence as the claim. Where a cause could not be established, the entry says so and says how many lanes looked.

**The timing rule, stated once because it decided three stories.** Continuous trading ends at 15:15 and the closing auction at 15:35, so nothing published later can explain that day's close. It kept the American sanctions announcement out of Monday's causes, since that landed between roughly 17:30 and 19:00 India time. It also works in reverse, and did twice: Vishal Mega Mart's board decision was filed on Friday evening and Urban Company's court outcome on Sunday afternoon, so Monday was simply the first session that could price either. This brief covers Monday and was written late; the whole American session of 24 August closes at 01:30 on the 25th and is excluded from it entirely.
