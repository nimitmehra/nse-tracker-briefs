A fifth straight down day, and the mood into the weekend was defensive.

The Nifty 50 slipped 0.43% to 23,767.45, its worst losing streak since early January. Two things hung over the tape: a US tariff deadline and $100 oil.

The lead: the 10% US surcharge on roughly half of India's exports hit its legal limit and lapsed on Friday, but it brought no relief. Washington is reported to be moving the same duty onto an open-ended footing, and the larger 1 August reciprocal-tariff deadline is still live with the India-US trade deal unsigned. Reported, not confirmed.

The tape was not all red, though. The Bank Nifty closed up 0.18% and Nifty IT rose 0.82%, the lone green sector, while auto, metal and energy led lower. India VIX, the fear gauge, jumped 4.08% to 14.03.

Why the fall stayed shallow: domestic institutions bought 5,454 crore of shares against 3,893 crore of foreign selling. Home money absorbed the exit.

Two movers with a real story:
- PVR INOX rose 5.61%, a genuine turnaround. It swung to a 56 crore profit from a year-ago loss, grew revenue 11.9%, and turned net-cash positive.
- The insight of the day: a good past quarter is not enough if the future looks softer. Acutaas grew revenue 59% but fell 5.8% because it held its guidance flat on a 69-times-earnings stock. Motilal Oswal's profit rose 10%, but on treasury gains rather than its core business, so it fell 7.3%.

Next up: the 1 August US reciprocal-tariff deadline.
