## The Lead

The 10% US tariff surcharge on Indian exports reached its legal expiry on Friday and lapsed, but it brought no relief. Washington is shifting the same duty onto a new open-ended legal footing, and the larger 1 August reciprocal-tariff deadline stays live with the India-US trade deal still unsigned. That anxiety, layered on a crude-oil overhang that has sat near $100 all week, kept the market defensive. The Nifty 50 fell 0.43% to 23,767.45, a fifth straight down day and its worst losing streak since early January. The fall stayed shallow only because domestic funds bought ₹5,454 crore of shares against ₹3,893 crore of foreign selling, while India VIX, the fear gauge, rose 4.08% to 14.03 into the weekend.

## Global Setup

The backdrop India traded through on Friday was a risk-off Asia. North Asia led the losses — Taiwan's TAIEX fell 2.67%, Shanghai 1.61% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng 0.98% — as the AI-and-chip trade that had powered the region cooled. India fell alongside them, but far less. The dollar held firm (the dollar index at 101.5), gold was flat near $4,052 and the US 10-year yield sat at 4.67%. The one wire that mattered most for India was crude: Brent had breached $100 on Thursday on the Red Sea and Hormuz supply scare and stayed elevated near $98 through Friday's session. For a country that imports about 85% of its oil, that is the pressure the market kept selling. Wall Street's overnight softness in the big tech names added to the caution.

## Macro & Policy

The day's macro was mostly a story of pressure, with one quiet domestic cushion underneath it.

**The heaviest negative was the US tariff cliff, and it did not clear.** A 10% American surcharge that has sat on roughly half of India's exports since February hit its 150-day legal limit and lapsed on Friday morning. But instead of reverting to normal duties, Washington is reported to be moving the same rate onto a different legal vehicle (a forced-labour investigation under "Section 301") that carries no expiry date. The separate 1 August reciprocal-tariff deadline is still live, with the interim India-US trade deal parked at "the last 1%." So the cliff did not remove the overhang; it reset it to 1 August. Because none of this is signed, treat it as reported, not confirmed.

**The second negative was oil, still elevated.** Brent breached $100 on Thursday after fresh attacks on shipping off Saudi Arabia and in the Strait of Hormuz, and it held near $98 through Friday. For a large net importer that feeds the import bill, the rupee and inflation, and it is the wire that has driven this whole five-day slide. It did ease about 2.7% after Friday's close, which is a possible relief for Monday, not a help to Friday.

**The one cushion was domestic and monetary.** Domestic institutions bought ₹5,454 crore against ₹3,893 crore of foreign selling, the same configuration for a second day running. And the RBI's weekly reserves data, out around 5 PM, showed reserves actually *rose* $1.08 billion to $676 billion — so the central bank did not visibly spend dollars defending the rupee this week, and the rupee held near, not below, its record-weak mark.

**Watch next week:** the 1 August tariff deadline is the single biggest scheduled event; the US Federal Reserve decides on 28-29 July; and the RBI's own rate meeting in early August is now genuinely two-sided, with hot June inflation and $100 oil on one side and the tariff-shock cushion on the other.

## The Market

The headline fall was small but the internals were split. The Nifty 50 and the Sensex both slipped 0.43%, yet the Bank Nifty actually closed *up* 0.18% — the one large-cap corner that bucked the decline, alongside IT. Per close reports, auto, metal and energy shares led the losses, while IT and media were the only supports. The clearest split of the day was inside financials: the big banks held green as domestic money moved into them defensively, but the rate-sensitive non-bank lenders fell (Bajaj Finance down 2.6%, Shriram Finance down 2.0% despite a strong result). So money moved toward banks and away from NBFCs within the same sector.

The rupee ended at ₹96.55, effectively flat and still holding a hair off its record-weak level. India VIX rose 4.08% to 14.03 — a jump into the weekend that says nerves are building, though the reading is still low in absolute terms.

## Earnings

A heavy Q1 results day, biggest companies first, with what they reported and how the market read it:

- **Shriram Finance (SHRIRAMFIN, market value ₹2.4 lakh crore)** — a strong quarter: profit jumped 59.9% to ₹3,453 crore on 16.2% revenue growth. Yet the stock fell 2.0%, because the whole rate-sensitive non-bank lending pack gave back ground on Friday even where the numbers were good. The result was fine; the sector mood was not.
- **Hindustan Zinc (HINDZINC, ₹2.2 lakh crore)** — the day's blowout that nobody rewarded: profit up 144.8% to ₹5,469 crore and revenue up 76.9%, yet the stock closed dead flat (+0.15%). The market ignored the beat because the metals complex was soft (aluminium fell 3.4% overnight) and the strength was largely expected.
- **InterGlobe Aviation / IndiGo (INDIGO, ₹1.9 lakh crore)** — swung to a ₹238 crore Q1 loss on the fuel spike (reported Thursday), with revenue still up 19.9%. The stock held remarkably well, down just 0.77%, because the loss was already flagged and crude eased after the close.
- **Tata Consumer (TATACONSUM, ₹1.1 lakh crore)** — a clean beat, profit up 28.6% and revenue up 11.9%, but the stock slipped 1.8%. On a 65-times earnings valuation, even a good result gets faded when the broader market is heavy.
- **Cipla (CIPLA, ₹1.1 lakh crore)** — profit fell 39.2% to ₹786 crore on US and one-off pressure, revenue up just 2.3% (reported Thursday); the stock still edged up 1.3%, cushioned by its domestic base.

**The day's real selling came from a cluster of Q1 misses lower down the list:**
- **Spandana Sphoorty (SPANDANA)** — turned to a wafer-thin ₹12 crore profit; the market read it as an unconvincing microfinance recovery and the stock fell 10.5%, the day's biggest drop (see Big Movers).
- **Go Digit General Insurance (GODIGIT)** — profit down 37.5% *and* premium income shrinking 8.4%; both the top and bottom line contracting sent the stock down 8.8%.
- **Motilal Oswal Financial Services (MOTILALOFS)** — profit technically rose about 10%, but it was carried by treasury gains while the core wealth and broking businesses softened, so the market sold the low-quality beat; the stock fell 7.3%.

*Omission note:* IndiGo, Cipla, Spandana, Go Digit and Motilal Oswal reported on Thursday (23 July) and their share reaction played out into Friday; Shriram Finance, Hindustan Zinc and Tata Consumer reported Friday itself.

## The Connections

*Behind the session: what triggered the moves, why, and which sectors to watch next.*

**One motif ran through the whole results day: a good past quarter is not enough if the future looks softer.** Acutaas reported revenue up 59% and still fell 5.8%, because management held its growth guidance flat and the stock was already expensive. Motilal Oswal's profit rose 10% but on treasury gains, not its core business, and it fell 7.3%. Route Mobile's profit was up year-on-year but down 40% from the prior quarter, and it fell too. The read for the reader: in a heavy market, investors are pricing the next two quarters, not the last one, and any wobble in quality or momentum gets sold hard.

**The blowout that the market ignored is the more telling signal.** Hindustan Zinc posted a 145% profit jump and closed flat; Steel Authority (+47% profit) and Sona BLW (+52% revenue) barely moved either. When results this strong fail to lift a stock, the message is that the sector tone is doing the pricing, not the individual numbers — here, a soft metals complex, with aluminium down 3.4% overnight, capped the whole group regardless of how good any one company's quarter was.

**Inside financials, the split told you where the domestic money is hiding.** The big banks closed green while the non-bank lenders fell, even though Shriram Finance reported a 60% profit rise. On a nervous, oil-and-tariff day, domestic funds parked in the safer large-cap banks and stepped away from the rate-sensitive NBFCs. That is defensive positioning, and it is worth watching whether it holds if oil stays near $100.

**India fell, but it fell less than its neighbours, and that is a flows story, not a good-news story.** The emerging-market equity basket dropped 1.8% and Taiwan fell 2.67% on the same global risk-off, while India lost only 0.43%. Foreign investors were still net sellers; the difference was the ₹5,454 crore of domestic buying that cushioned the exit. India is being sold less because Indian institutions keep absorbing the selling, not because anything turned positive.
