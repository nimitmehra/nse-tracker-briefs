## ERRATUM — We were wrong about the Hormuz arrangement, and here is the correction

*Issued after publication. The original text is left standing verbatim below and in Cascade Watch; nothing above or below this block has been rewritten, and no number in this brief changes.*

**Cascade Watch states, and the Provenance table tags CONFIRMED:**

> *"...Hormuz transit arrangement in principle — and the agreed terms would bar United States and Israeli vessels and demand compensation from hostile states, with any reopening still conditional on Washington lifting its blockade of Iranian ports"*

**That is wrong.** It conflates two separate instruments at two different stages — the same error class this brief was scrupulous about avoiding for the RBI draft.

**What the Iran–Oman arrangement actually contains.** Agreed geographical coordinates for a safe shipping route, technical arrangements, the security and sovereignty of Iran and Oman, and a proposed joint coordination centre for maritime traffic. Inbound traffic is routed through the **northern corridor** near the Iranian coast and outbound through the **southern corridor** along the Omani coast, shifting to a **central corridor** after a transitional phase. **Transit fees are structured around services rendered rather than as a percentage of cargo value.** A joint Tehran–Muscat statement is in final drafting and under review.

**The vessel bar is not a term of that arrangement.** The ban on United States and Israeli vessels, together with tolls, comes from a **separate Iranian parliamentary committee** — a different body, a different instrument, at an earlier stage.

**The fee basis is inverted, not merely misattributed.** The agreement's stated basis is services rendered and explicitly *not* a percentage of cargo value; the cargo-value-percentage concept belongs to the parliamentary bill. The brief attached the bill's terms to the agreement and then reversed the one term it did carry.

**The conditionality named is also the wrong one.** **Trump has balked at any deal granting Iran more control over the strait, and United States support is unclear.** That is the live conditionality — a question of American acceptance, not a term Iran and Oman agreed.

**Why the CONFIRMED tag did not catch it.** Three outlets carried the same conflation, so multi-source corroboration confirmed the *reporting* and not the *underlying instrument distinction*. **Corroboration across outlets is not corroboration of a primary document.** Where two instruments are in play, each one has to be traced to its own source before either is tagged, and that was not done here.

**What is unaffected.** The **"Brent sustained under $85"** trigger reasoning stands. That trigger was explicitly **not** fired, the six conditions failed on their own terms, and the elevated-not-fired judgement rests on facts this correction does not touch.

## What Happened

### The regulator wrote a draft and the market read a law

On Thursday evening the Reserve Bank issued draft amendments to the rules governing credit facilities at non-bank lenders, and the proposal is narrow and severe. Non-bank lenders would be allowed to offer only term loans and no revolving credit products at all, with the sole exception of those the central bank has authorised to issue credit cards. A term loan gets a formal definition — fixed principal, a pre-determined repayment schedule — and a sanctioned limit would not be replenished once principal is repaid. Flexi, overdraft and revolving-limit products in corporate, small-business and unsecured personal lending are the exposed set. **Bajaj Finance fell 5.84% to ₹1,078.00, the worst constituent of either headline index.** But feedback closes on 28 August and nothing has been notified, so what moved today was a consultation paper.

### Jio Financial is inside the perimeter and nobody knows which way

**Jio Financial fell 2.39% to ₹256.80 and is the only holding the draft reaches.** The direction is genuinely unsettled rather than quietly bad. The rule would strip revolving credit from incumbent lenders whose flexi books are their most profitable product, while carving out card-authorised entities and pushing the product toward banks. A young, well-capitalised, bank-adjacent lender with a small legacy revolving book could be a net beneficiary of that. **This desk has not established Jio Financial's revolving share or its card-authorisation status, and it is not guessing at either.** The dividend record date is Monday 10 August, the e-voting eligibility cut-off is 19 August, and the meeting on 26 August carries the ₹7,200 crore related-party authorisation. Both unknowns need answering before the consultation closes.

### Blue Star's own document made the new low

**Blue Star closed at ₹1,514.00, down 3.63%, and that close is its 52-week low.** The document that did it was the company's own decomposed investor update, filed at 11:28 into a live session. **Revenue grew 13.3% to ₹3,377.92 crore while the operating margin went 6.7% to 5.2% and pre-tax profit before exceptionals fell 23.7%** — the operating number, against a reported profit fall of 15.1% cushioned by a lighter tax charge. The room-air-conditioner segment margin halved, 5.8% to 2.9%. Management named its own headwinds in plain language rather than burying them. The offsets are real: order book ₹7,764.38 crore, up 13.5%, and net cash ₹900.25 crore. **Crompton Greaves fell 7.43% the same day on the same cooling problem.**

### Garware's record quarter is signed and still sold

**Garware Hi-Tech Films fell 4.98% to ₹6,973.00, a second consecutive session of selling the best quarter in its history.** The numbers are now signed and published: consolidated total income ₹652.80 crore, profit ₹132.65 crore, up 59.8%, and earnings of ₹57.10 a share. Those came from the newspaper results extract, which survived extraction cleanly and reconciles exactly with what we parsed from the filing on Thursday. **The auditor's limited-review conclusion is still unread across both document sets, so nothing about the audit opinion is asserted here.** The give-back from Wednesday's 52-week high is 5.89% against a three-month run of +40.92%. The earnings call was held today and its audio was filed at 16:48, after the close — so Monday is the session that tests it.

**Yesterday this desk wrote that KPI Green "has not even called a board meeting this year." That was false.** The intimation is dated 5 August 2026 — one day *before* the sentence was written — and the board meets on 11 August to approve the June-quarter results. Our own sweep missed a filing that existed, and the brief then hardened a wrong finding into a sharper one. **What survives is narrower and it is not a disclosure failure: the company reported this quarter on 5 August last year and reports on 11 August this year, a six-day slip year on year.** The research-queue entry made on 6 August still clears on its other seven signals, but its evidence list now contains a falsified item.
