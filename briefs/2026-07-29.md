## What Happened

### Half the institutions voted against KPI Green's director
KPI Green Energy (KPIGREEN), a portfolio holding, filed its postal-ballot results on Monday 27 July, and the cover letter says the resolutions passed with the requisite majority. The table underneath says something else. **48.48% of institutional votes cast went against re-appointing independent director Mrs Venu Birappa (DIN 09123017) for a second five-year term**, with 23.49% against elevating another director to whole-time director. Consolidated dissent reads just **4.93%**, because the promoter block holding 49.4% voted entirely in favour — so any screen-scraped summary shows nothing at all. Why institutions objected is not established, and no reason has been invented here. It is the first governance signal on this name in the desk's record, so it grades monitor-for-repeat rather than thesis break. One ballot is data; a second would be the chapter.

### The market closed on relief that had already expired
Nifty closed up 1.10% and the breadth was genuine — mid-caps +0.82%, small-caps +1.48%. India VIX fell to 12.01, and both foreign and domestic institutions bought, with foreigners the larger at ₹2,982 crore. That flip is the structural item of the day, inverting the foreign-selling, domestic-absorbing pattern that has framed the last several briefs, though one session is not a regime. What the session could not see: Brent was marked at **$90.80, up 7.98%, at roughly 21:03 IST**. That is five and a half hours after the close, and **+24.13% on the month**, with Hormuz throughput still under a tenth of normal. The relief the market bought had expired by dinner.

### Infosys cut its guidance and rose 4.5% anyway
Infosys (INFY) filed its June-quarter earnings-call transcript on Tuesday 28 July, and it confirms a cut, not a trim. Full-year constant-currency revenue guidance moved from 1.5–3.5% to **1.5–3.0%** — the floor held, the ceiling came down 50 basis points, the midpoint fell to about 2.25%. On the arithmetic put to management on that call by analyst Kumar Rakesh — and not disputed by them — stripping roughly 1.7% of contribution from acquisitions leaves organic growth near **0.5%**. Margin guidance was untouched at 20–22%, and large-deal bookings were strong at $3.6 billion, 61% of it new business. The stock closed +4.51% at ₹1,155.60 anyway, leading a sector index up 2.32%. The rupee *strengthened* 0.73% this week, so the usual weak-currency story for an exporter rally does not apply.

### Varun Beverages sold on margin, bought back on volume
Varun Beverages (VBL) reported on Tuesday 28 July and fell about 8% that session. The company runs a January–December year, so this is the second calendar quarter. Revenue rose 20.4% to ₹8,451 crore, volume 19.8% to 466.7 million cases, profit 15.1% to ₹1,525 crore. The line that moved the stock was operating margin: **down 76 basis points to 27.7%**, on consolidating the lower-margin Twizza business in South Africa. That is a knowable drag which will persist for several quarters, and selling it was coherent. Today VBL recovered 4.00% to ₹447.20 — a second-day bounce matching Hindustan Unilever's shape exactly, not a repair. It is still −11.89% on the month and 17.8% below its high.

### Waaree Renewable's research is now three events behind
Waaree Renewable Technologies (WAAREERTL) collected three material positives inside the five-day gap, and its research report predates every one. Two Letters of Award for ground-mounted solar construction, filed Sunday 26 July, aggregate **800 MWac / 1,082 MWp** for FY2027-28 delivery — real and quantified, counterparty unnamed. A pre-construction agreement in New Zealand, filed 27 July, is the first engagement outside India and the first with battery storage attached. In the company's own words the principal *may* later sign a construction contract, so it is pipeline, not a project win. Against all that, the stock fell 1.08% on a rising market and is −10.37% on the week, with June-quarter revenue and profit both below the March quarter.

All three edges were already at the weight cap, so what moved was activation count. The forward effect is narrow but real: KPI Green's silence is now measured against a loud cohort.

**The voting season put institutional dissent on four unrelated names in one week; the graph came a day short of promoting it to a node.** It was logged as a candidate for a dissent node, but spanned six days against a seven-day rule, so no edge exists. KPIGREEN at 48.48%, ABSLAMC at 66.82%, IIFL at 41.73% and SHRIPISTON at 31.99% all cleared the threshold, out of 23 ballots read. The forward effect is a cost-of-capital one. **When a holding next asks shareholders to pre-authorise a share issue, assume a materially higher base rate of institutional opposition than a year ago.**

**The oil-import edge did not activate today, and the reason is timing rather than direction.** Five tracked names carry conditions keyed to Hormuz resolving with Brent sustained below $85. Brent printed $90.80, but that mark is 5.5 hours after the close, so no same-session causal link may be computed and none was. The state that matters is the month: **+24.13%, with Saudi Arabia's only post-Hormuz export corridor under drone attack for a second day.** This thread is extended and re-escalated, not closed — the strike pause broke, and Hormuz throughput never rose above a tenth of normal at any point. The effect lands on 30 July — on the same paints, tyres, auto-component and beverage-packaging cohort that got a three-session reprieve and has now had it taken back.

**Zero derived sector cascades were written today, and that is the correct answer rather than an empty result.** The cascade script was ready to fan 13 signals into 12 stock nodes off ten sector nodes carrying a high-severity signal.
