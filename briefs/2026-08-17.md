## What Happened

### Megacap IT sold and the rest of the market did not

The index fell 0.32% for a narrow reason. **Nifty IT closed −1.75%, the only sector index past its threshold in either direction and four and a half times the benchmark's fall.** FMCG at −1.05% was the second drag. Underneath, breadth improved: 127 tracked mid-tier names rose against 124 that fell, and both the Midcap 50 (+0.10%) and Smallcap 250 (+0.18%) closed above the benchmark. **That is the exact reverse of Friday**, when only 10 of the 50 Nifty names advanced and the smaller stocks fell harder. Money moved out of dollar-earners and defensives into domestic cyclicals — Hindalco +1.98%, Tata Steel +1.36% and HDFC Life +1.33% at the top, against HCL Tech −2.57%, Sun Pharma −2.49%, TCS −2.02% and ITC −1.85% at the bottom.

### Two published reasons for the IT fall are both wrong

The dispersion inside the index is the finding, not the index. **L&T Technology Services closed +1.49% and Persistent, Coforge and Oracle Financial were within a third of a percent of flat, while Infosys, HCL Tech and TCS fell 2.0% to 2.6%** — a spread of 4.06 percentage points. A currency shock moves the whole basket together; this did not. Two circulating explanations were checked and neither survives. **The "stronger rupee" story is directionally inverted** — USD/INR went 95.43 to 95.592, so the rupee *weakened*, which helps exporters. **And the Jefferies downgrade of Infosys quoted as today's cause is dated February 2026**, six months stale, with a ₹1,290 target 13% *above* the ₹1,139.90 close. What is left is a re-pricing of the large-cap managed-services model.

### Jio Financial's ₹7,200 crore vote closes on Wednesday

The most time-critical item in the book. **Jio Financial Services meets on 26-August and electronic voting closes on 19-August — two days away.** Shareholders are asked to authorise related-party transactions of ₹7,200 crore in aggregate, **split across three annexures that are never added up anywhere in the 143-page notice.** Friday's presentation on those items is a hyperlink and nothing else, so the detail sits outside the primary record. Lawful; no violation is alleged. **The substantive flaw is a construction one: the rule requires a total, and one counterparty's row reports an average instead — ₹513.34 crore for FY26 — hiding the peak, while the limit is measured at peak.** Three cautions travel with it: the 204.94% ratio is inflated by construction, no "₹750 crore already deployed" figure exists, and **Resolution 4 is a scheduled rotation inside a regulator-mandated two-auditor panel, not an auditor resignation.**

### Voltas turns Blue Star's margin problem into a share-loss problem

Blue Star's room-air-conditioner margin halving from 5.8% to 2.9% has been read here as an input-cost story. **Voltas reported the identical quarter, product and weather — and expanded its core cooling operating margin from 3.7% to 5.3% on room-air-conditioner volumes up 45%, widening its market lead to four points.** Two firms cannot both be victims of the same input costs when one expands margin on 45% volume growth. **That reclassifies the problem from sectoral to competitive**, which is a different and worse thing to own. Separately, Blue Star's electro-mechanical arm agreed on 14-August to sell its MedTech division to Cyrix Healthcare for up to ₹40 crore — immaterial at 0.33% of income, but unseen for 72 hours. **The stock closed +0.01%, sits 1.21% above its 52-week low, and has no live condition attached to it.**

### Innova Captab is revising the audited year it just called strong

The window's most serious governance finding sits on a name in the research queue, and it surfaced from an insider-dealing filing. **Innova Captab disclosed on 13-August that its dealing window stays shut — not until results, but until 48 hours after it declares *revised* audited accounts for the year ended 31-March-2026.** The board meeting to approve them is "to be informed in due course." There is no date. Two days earlier the same company issued a release headed "strong results" for the June quarter, **printing those very FY26 figures as comparatives with no mention of a revision**, over an unqualified audit opinion from B S R & Co. LLP. Nothing here claims the company knew on the 11th. **But its employees are locked out indefinitely, and the FY26 revenue figure has two versions on the record — ₹1,630.0 crore and ₹1,637.44 crore.**

## Cascade Watch

**The 60-day US–Iran understanding expired today with no deal, no ceasefire and no extension, and the Strait of Hormuz stays partially closed.** The chain that matters for India is the crude-import one: about 85% of the country's oil arrives by sea, so a Hormuz risk premium is a mechanical debit to the current account and lands on the rupee before it lands on anything else. **Brent closed at US$89.01, up 0.55% against Friday's settle and above $88 for a second session.** Say the size plainly, because it governs what follows: **at +0.55% this is nowhere near the 3% move that would pull the paint, tyre, auto-ancillary and airline chain with it, and no such cascade fired tonight.** A "+5% on the week" figure circulating in the press is not reconcilable with an $89.01 level against Friday's roughly $88.52 settle. Tomorrow's watch is the level, not the day-move.

**The Reserve Bank closed its discounted dollar-swap window for foreign-currency deposits six weeks ahead of schedule** — mobilisation now ends 31-August instead of 30-September, with swaps executable to 11-September. Cumulative inflows under the scheme reached US$56.85 billion as of 13-August, of which US$52.3 billion came through those deposits. The mechanism is a funding-cost one: banks lose a subsidised route to dollar liabilities six weeks early, and the rupee took it immediately — 95.43 on Friday to 95.592 today, weaker for a second session while the dollar index itself *fell* 0.23%. **India took none of the dollar's softness, and that divergence is the India-specific part.** It also resolves a contradiction carried from Friday: reserves rose US$14.14 billion to US$707 billion while the central bank was selling dollars, because those are two different books.

**Copper rose 1.44% and aluminium 2.97%, and the Indian metals and cable complex went with them.** The edge here is the base-metal input chain into wires, pipes and long products, and it fired cleanly on both legs. Nifty Metal closed +1.26% against a −0.32% benchmark; inside it Hindustan Copper +8.24%, Godawari Power +6.54%, Hindalco +1.98% and Tata Steel +1.36%. **The more interesting leg is the cables — KEI +3.04% and Finolex Cables +5.93%, both to 52-week highs.** Copper is their largest input, and the market is pricing the pass-through as pricing power rather than as margin risk. Tomorrow that read is falsifiable in one direction: if copper holds and cable margins compress at the next print, the market had it backwards.

**Three filings landed in windows the market could not trade against, and each produced its damage on the first available bell.** NGL Fine-Chem filed an investor presentation at **15:02 IST, 28 minutes before the close.** In it, management warned that the quarter's 620-basis-point margin gain was partly inventory gains, plus a ₹7.0 crore currency swing worth about 29% of pre-tax profit that "may not recur." **The stock closed −15.80% on 16 times normal volume.** Zaggle's income-tax appeal dismissal was filed **at 22:09 on Saturday 15-August, Independence Day, 65 hours before anyone could react** — it opened into a locked 20% lower circuit. Blue Star's divestment agreement was filed at 21:01 on the Friday of a three-day break. **None of this is a rule breach, and none is alleged. The pattern is that the disclosure clock and the trading clock are not the same clock**, and a desk reading only same-session filings will keep arriving second.
