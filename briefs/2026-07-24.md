## What Happened

### The tariff cliff lapsed unsigned, and the weekend clock keeps ticking
The temporary US tariff arrangement on Indian goods expired today, 24-July, with the first-phase trade pact still stuck at the "last 1%" of legal text — close, but not signed and not published. The reciprocal tariff sits at 18%, down from 25%, and a second clock runs in parallel to 1-August, so there are two dates, not one, and India is negotiating to terms rather than the calendar. This is the broadest-reach story on the board: it sat over the whole tape, lifted India VIX 4.08% into the weekend, and bears most directly on Garware Hi-Tech Films, the book's most tariff-levered name, where the revenue-and-margin damage is already booked. A no-deal lapse extends that pressure; a signed 18% pact would cap it. The binary did not resolve today — it just moved from a one-day cliff to a rolling weekend watch.

### Acutaas beat +70% on profit and still fell 6%
Acutaas Chemicals, a portfolio holding, printed a strong June quarter — revenue up 59.1% to ₹329.7 crore and profit up 70.4% to ₹75.0 crore year-on-year — and the stock fell 5.77% to ₹3,259.5 on it. The reason is plain: on a roughly 69-times earnings multiple, after an 88% run this year, the market wanted a raised outlook and management held the full-year growth guidance at about 25%. Strong year-on-year numbers hid a sharp sequential slowdown too — revenue down 24% and profit down 44% against the March quarter, with the operating margin easing from 42.4% to 34.3%. The fundamentals are intact and this is multiple compression, not a thesis break, but it carried the valuation-discipline lesson of the day: at the top of the range, a beat without a guidance raise gets sold. The 4pm concall on the FY27 pace is the swing variable, and the research refresh waits on that transcript.

### Jio Financial cleared its final RBI hurdle to become a holding company
Jio Financial Services, a holding, received final Reserve Bank of India approval today to convert from a non-banking finance company into a Core Investment Company — a systemically-important, non-deposit-taking holding structure. This is a genuine regulatory milestone, not a filing footnote: it formalises Jio Financial as the group parent vehicle for its financial-services subsidiaries and cleans the path for subsidiary-level capital raises and the lending and joint-venture build-out. It clears a structural overhang that has sat on the name, and the stock quietly outperformed on a soft-financials day, up 0.66% while Bajaj Finance and Shriram Finance both fell about 2%. Clean positive on a name that needed one.

### Oil drove the session down, but the relief came after the bell
The fifth down day was, at heart, a crude scare. Fresh Houthi tanker strikes in the Red Sea plus the Hormuz overlay pushed Brent above $100 during the session, and for a country that imports about 85% of its crude that is a live cost line for autos, auto-components, paints, aviation and logistics — the cohort that led the decline. The important timing point: the −2.73% Brent mark sitting in today's data is the post-close, overnight easing back under $100, a potential relief setup for tomorrow — it is not what happened during trading hours, which sold off on crude-above-$100 fear. Two facts, side by side. Underneath it, the same structural support held: domestic institutions bought ₹5,454 crore against ₹3,893 crore of foreign selling, and the market recovered from a roughly 500-point Sensex morning low to close down only 332. Cautious consolidation, not capitulation.
