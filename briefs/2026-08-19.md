## What Happened

### IRCTC's monopoly business stopped growing

**IRCTC put its June-quarter transcript on the exchange at 13:48 today, and the headline is the least useful number in it.** Revenue rose 18.1% to ₹1,370 crore — but operating profit *fell* 2.77% and the margin dropped 510 basis points to 28.17%. **The decomposition is the thesis.** Internet Ticketing, the business that earns above 80% at the operating line and handles 89% of India's reserved rail bookings, grew **0.5%** — effectively flat. Catering grew **33.8%** and is now 53% of revenue, and management named it as the margin diluter itself. Worse, Internet Ticketing's own operating margin fell from **84.12% to 80.33%**. The mix is migrating off monopoly economics, and a company that grows 18% by selling more of its cheapest product has not grown.

### The board is being fixed but is not fixed

**The other half of IRCTC is governance, and one item that looked settled is not.** The appointment of Dr Sachin Balkrishna Sabnis on 13-August was carried two different ways by two different lines, so we read both filings. They are one appointment at two stages: the railways ministry conveyed him as a nominee on 13-August, and the company's own material-event filing the next day confirms the board approved him as **an independent director**. That is real movement. **But the same filing's board list shows six members with exactly one independent director — 16.7%, against a listing requirement of at least half for a board with an executive chairman.** The chairman's seat is itself held on additional charge since 20-July. **This is the first independent appointment since the exchanges fined the company in July, and it moves toward a cure without completing one.** All three research gates on this name pass and it is surfaced below, not queued — your rule holds.

### The day's biggest gainer was a flow event, not news

**Zaggle rose 12.15%, and the reason is one buyer.** Kedia Securities bought 20,00,000 shares — about 1.48% of the company — in a bulk deal at a weighted-average ₹164.72, ₹32.94 crore of consideration. **The tax order this desk flagged yesterday is not the cause and points the other way: that appeal was dismissed, which is adverse, and the roughly 20% fall happened on 17-August on a June-quarter miss** — revenue ₹420 crore up 27.5%, but profit ₹17.5 crore, **down 32.9%**. **Nothing about the quarter changed today.** Two guards travel with this: the company's own filing states no amount at all, so the ₹4,09,15,780 figure for assessment year 2022-23 is press-recovered rather than confirmed; and the ₹6.6 crore number circulating is ₹6,60,51,200 for a *different* assessment year and a different instrument. **The uncomfortable part is ours: two documented holes in our own plumbing — no feed for income-tax or appellate orders, and no bulk-deal data — jointly explain the largest move in a 761-name universe. We recovered it because the buyer was famous.**

### Engineers India's 141% profit rise came from somewhere else

**Engineers India fell 3.22% to ₹236.90 on the first session in which the market could trade its June-quarter call, and the fall is the market reading past the headline.** Profit rose 141% to ₹157.94 crore. **Revenue fell 5.8% to ₹819.84 crore.** And ₹49.88 crore of the ₹92.54 crore profit increase — 54% of it — came from joint ventures and associates swinging from a loss to a profit, which sits **below the operating line entirely.** The consultancy business did not produce that. **The transcript went out at 16:11 yesterday, after the close, so it could not have driven yesterday's rise; today is the first day it could be priced, and it was.** The order-inflow question is separately settled and settled favourably — ₹2,750 crore year-to-date against an ₹8,000 crore target, roughly on pace — so this is a quality-of-profit observation, not a demand one.
