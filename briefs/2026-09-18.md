## What Happened

### Tata Sons split in public; the Sensex paid for it

On Thursday evening the Tata Sons board re-appointed N. Chandrasekaran executive chairman for five years by a 4–1 vote, Noel Tata against. The same evening Tata Trusts, which owns about 66% of Tata Sons, called the resolution "a legal nullity" under the Articles — an assertion; no court, regulator or filing has ruled. A wire line that the board also "approved a listing" is a single anonymous-source Bloomberg item, contradicted by the Trusts the same day — a claim, not a fact. Friday priced the rift: Tata Chemicals −11.04% on NSE to ₹693.25, TCS −3.88% to ₹2,105, and TCS's weight is the whole gap between Sensex −0.03% and Nifty +0.33%. For your book this is contagion, not information — Infosys fell 0.68%, three points less than TCS, with no Trusts problem.

### The crowded Thursday cleared; the relief is in oil, not rates

The Federal Reserve raised 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%, 12–0, the first hike since 2023, with 16 of 18 members seeing one more this year; the US ten-year is near 5.00%. The Bank of Japan raised 25 bp to 1.25%, the highest since 1995, and the yen fell — no carry-trade unwind hit India. The RBI's first ₹50,000 crore bond sale drew ₹66,590 crore of bids, fully accepted, without a yield event. The relief is Brent: Thursday's settle was $104.82, Friday traded near $103–105, and Saudi Arabia says half its East–West pipeline returns "within days", the full line in about six weeks.
