## What Happened

### Washington's Iran offensive landed and crude went the wrong way

**US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled what he called "the single greatest financial offensive ever" against Iran this afternoon, trailed on Sunday by his own warning that "an economic D-Day is coming."** The mechanism reaching India: **Washington will sanction any country that keeps trading with Iran.** Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority answered that vessels breaching its rules face "fines, seizure, or confiscation," and both sides let the ceasefire lapse. **But read the tape first: Brent fell to about $92.7–93.1, roughly 1.4–1.8% lower, on profit-taking after a 5%-plus week.** A supply shock that leaves crude cheaper did not price a supply threat. **The market priced the payments-and-trade channel, not the import-bill one.** A 0.14% fall is also very small for a country importing 85% of its crude through that waterway.

### Friday priced a record order and you never saw it

**Welspun Corp (WELCORP) filed a US$1.8 billion pipe order — about ₹17,200 crore — at 22:57 on Thursday 20-August, two and a half hours after our own evening brief went out.** Friday was the first session that could price it, and Friday you never saw. **The order is 41% of the company's global book, takes that book to a record US$4.4 billion, and the revenue lands in FY2028 and FY2029.** The stock added 4.21% today and 28.01% on the week, re-rating on cash that begins two years out. **The counterparty is still not named, which the rules require.** The other fuse is shorter and is not Friday's either: **the Reserve Bank's concessional dollar-deposit window, pulled forward to 31-August on 14-August, has seven days left.** Ten days old, seven days short.
